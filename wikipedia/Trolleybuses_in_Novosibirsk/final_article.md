# Trolleybuses in Novosibirsk

The Novosibirsk trolleybus system () is part of the public transport network of Novosibirsk, Russia. The system opened on 6 November 1957.

A test drive took place on November 6, 1957.
Regular passenger service began the next day.

In 1957, 10 trolleybuses operated in the city.
By the end of 1957, 17 trolleybuses operated.
By 1958, the number of trolleybuses increased to 45.

== Early plans and launch ==

Plans to launch trolleybuses in Novosibirsk were considered as early as 1936, but the postponement was indefinite due to a lack of free production capacity.
The test run occurred the day before the 40th anniversary of the October Revolution on a route that started at the airport and ended at Moskovskaya Street (bus station).
The initial trolleybuses were model MTB-82.
The total length of trolleybus lines reached 53 kilometers in 1960.
Trolleybuses of the ZiU-5 model began operation in October 1961 and were supplied from Engels city.
The Kirov trolleybus depot opened on December 22, 1962, at Sibiryakov-Gvardeyets Street, and 16 trolleybuses appeared on the city streets.
The total length of trolleybus lines was more than 238 kilometers by 1984.

== Network expansion and fleet growth ==

The Zelyatsov trolleybus depot opened on October 19, 1976.
By the end of 1977, the length of trolleybus lines was greater than that of tram lines, with trolleybus lines measuring 177.1 km and tram lines measuring 174.62 km.
Trolleybus trains began successful operation in Novosibirsk starting in 1984, consisting of two ZIU-682 cars and operating using Vladimir Veklich's system.
The total length of trolleybus lines exceeded 238 kilometers by 1984, and the trolleybus fleet consisted of 331 vehicles.
The Leningrad trolleybus depot opened on December 28, 1988.

== Decline and modernization ==

The dismantling of some sections of the overhead contact network began in 1992.
The length of trolleybus lines was approximately 133 kilometers by 2006.
In 2008, the share of trolleybuses in total city transport was approximately 13%, while their share in municipal transport was 32%.
In 2008, trolleybuses had the second-highest share among municipal transport modes, behind only the metro.
The decline in trolleybus share is primarily explained by an increase in passengers transported by private buses.
The passenger volume was 52.1 million people.

== Route 5 history ==

Route 5 was opened in 1995 and was initially served by 16 vehicles with an interval of 4 minutes between them.

It followed the Champion Store stop, located at Kalinina Square, until June 15, 2006, when the route was extended.
In April 2008, the route was extended to the Gorodskoy Aeroport stop.
In early June 2008, Route 5 followed the path from Stanislavsky residential quarter to Airport due to trolleybus wire repairs on Boris Bogatkova Street.
Until June 1, 2011, Route 5 ran from Stanislavsky residential quarter to Autopark.
The route was shortened back to Kalinina Square (Metro Zayeltsovskaya stop) in November 2012.
On June 5, 2018, the route was rerouted as an experiment to increase passenger flow.
The route's starting terminal was changed from Metro Zailcsovskaya to Rychnoy Vokzal, and at Frunze Street, the route turned left onto Krasny Prospekt instead of right.
The experimental operation was scheduled to last until October 1, 2018.
After the experiment, the route was returned to its previous path ending at Kalinina Square.

== Relationship with other transport systems ==

The Novosibirsk tramway is also an electrically operated public transport system.
The trolleybus system in Novosibirsk is the second such electrically operated public transport system in the city.

The system consists of 14 routes (№№ 1, 2, 4, 5, 7, 8, 10, 13, 22, 23, 24, 26, 29, 36).

The trolleybus fleet is represented by such "classic'" trolleybuses as Soviet ZiU-9, and ZiU-10, Russian VMZ-184, VMZ-201, VMZ-263, VMZ-375, BTZ-5276-01, Trolza-5265.00, Trolza-5275.05, and Trolza-5275.06, Belarusian AKSM-101A, and AKSM-101M. In 2011-2012, trolleybuses ST-6217M with battery packs, manufactured by Novosibirsk factory Liotech, were tested.
Experiment had ended when Liotech was declared insolvent.
In 2022, municipal authorities decided to repeat the experiment with new trolleybuses with battery packs - UTTZ-6241.01.

== Fleet composition and historical context ==

The total number of vehicles remains approximately constant, and no Siberian city has preserved such a quantity of this type of electric transport rolling stock.
In 2004, trolleybuses accounted for 24% of urban transport, while buses had a higher share than trolleybuses in urban transport.
The expansion of the route network is not currently planned, and the construction of new overhead lines is not currently planned.
New rolling stock is being purchased, and the new rolling stock has an extended autonomous range (ТУАХ).

== Fares and transfers ==

A pilot network transfer project was launched on July 16, 2018.
Passengers transferring within one hour from any trolleybus or tram to another trolleybus or tram receive a 100% discount on the second trip, a rule that applies even if the passenger stays on the same route.
Passengers receive a 50% discount on their second trip, which also applies when transferring from the metro back to tram route 29.
The project demonstrated that most passengers prefer transferring to the metro, and trolleybuses run empty after passengers transfer to the metro.
Route 29K was included in the program.
The fare for riding the Novosibirsk trolleybus started on December 23, 2023.
The standard fare for the Novosibirsk trolleybus is 35 rubles, the fare using the Unified Transport Card is 34 rubles, and the fare using student and school cards is 17.30 rubles.

== Depots ==

There are currently 4 trolleybus depots in Novosibirsk.
The first digit corresponds to the depot number, which refers to the main depot rather than a branch, and this sequential numbering started in 1978.
The depot named Dzerzhinsky was designated as number 1 and is the city's first trolleybus depot.
This facility was opened several years after the trolleybus service began.
It is the only depot that does not operate articulated trolleybuses, and the small size of the depot building is one reason for this limitation.
The entity serves route number 13 jointly with the main depot of MKP 'GET'.
The fleet numbers range from 1174 to 1314 and also from 1501 to 1527.
The address is Aleyskaya Street.
A former trolleybus depot was located in Kirov and designated as number 2.
Route numbers 26 and 29 are served jointly with branch number 3.
The address is Sibiryakov-Gvardeyets Street.

3.
