# Vesuvius red zone

The Vesuvius red zone () is the area designated to be quickly evacuated within a few days in the case of an eruption of Mount Vesuvius. The authorities believe that they will have two weeks' warning of an imminent eruption; the questions that arise are whether the population can be evacuated in time, despite a reluctance to leave their homes; and if there be as much as two weeks' warning in reality.

As the area around Mount Vesuvius is highly urbanized, it became necessary to develop evacuation plans to be applied when the signs of a forthcoming eruption appear. A danger zone around the volcano was first identified in 2001 by the Italian ; its boundaries were then redefined in 2013 following new studies on the behaviour of Plinian eruptions.

The zone is composed by two subzones, called red zone 1 and 2. The former is the most dangerous area in the case of an eruption, being at greatest risk from pyroclastic flows. The red zone 2 will be subjected to falling ash and lapilli, whose intensity will depend on the intensity and direction of the winds at the time.

Mount Vesuvius is continuously kept under surveillance by the Vesuvius Observatory of the National Institute of Geophysics and Volcanology. When an appointed set of geologic activities occurs, an eruption warning is issued and the evacuation procedure begins. It has been estimated that 72 hours are needed to complete the evacuation: 12 hours to organize it, 48 hours to transfer all the people outside the red zone and 12 hours are kept as a safety margin for unexpected events.

== Evolution of the red zone perimeter ==

The high-risk perimeter was expanded prudentially using previous administrative boundaries, although the previous red zone was larger than the current one.
The Civil Protection issued a new perimeter for the red zone on January 11, 2013.
The previous red zone included 18 municipalities.
New municipalities were added to the red zone, either fully or partially.
Nola, Palma Campania, Poggiomarino, San Gennaro Vesuviano, and Scafati were added to the red zone.
La Masseria Cutinelli was added to the red zone.
La Masseria Cutinelli is an enclave of Pomigliano d'Arco and is surrounded by the municipality of Sant'Anastasia.
The Sixth Municipality of Naples was added to the red zone.
The Sixth Municipality of Naples consists of the districts (partially) of Barra, San Giovanni a Teduccio, and Ponticelli.
The old emergency plan placed the Sixth Municipality of Naples in the yellow zone.

== Urbanization and land-use restrictions ==

High urbanization has developed since the post-war period in the base area and the foothills of Mount Vesuvius.
Mount Vesuvius now characterizes a territory considered to be at high volcanic risk.
This level of human activity defines Mount Vesuvius as the most dangerous volcano in the world.
This area is declared by the State as having high volcanic hazard.

== Emergency planning and alert procedures ==

The red zone is subject to a national emergency plan that must be integrated as soon as possible with a valid evacuation plan.
An evacuation plan regarding the risk of Mount Vesuvius is being updated by 25 municipalities in the Vesuvian area.
The document must have departmental and regional oversight, with the Civil Protection Department serving as the responsible departmental body.
Evacuation routes must be identified considering the needs of all municipalities and must not conflict with one another.
The evacuation plan has been the subject of many controversies.
When the plan is completed in all its parts, it will be made known to all citizens of the Vesuvian area and integrated into the emergency plan.
Operational phases of the emergency plan are linked to volcanic alert levels established by the High-Risk Commission, which consulted with centers of expertise such as the Vesuvius Observatory.
The volcanic alarm must be declared by the president of the council of ministers, while the 'attention' phase can be declared directly by the head of the Civil Protection Department.
The pre-alarm must be declared by the President of the Council of Ministers, and the declaration of both the pre-alarm and volcanic alarm requires hearing the President of the Campania Region.
The transition between volcanic alert levels does not have precise timings.

Volcanic alert levels introduce corresponding operational phases.
These strategies include the spontaneous evacuation of the population, which can occur during the pre-alarm phase.
In the event of an alarm, the population must follow rescuers.
The evacuation plan is calibrated for a low-to-medium intensity volcanic eruption but is not calibrated for the maximum known eruption scenario, which scientists consider unlikely.
The Campanian regional authorities have calculated the time needed to evacuate the entire population at risk in case an alarm state is declared.
