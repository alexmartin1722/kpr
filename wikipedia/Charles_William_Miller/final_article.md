# Charles William Miller

Charles William Miller (24 November 1874 – 30 June 1953; ) was a Brazilian sportsman, who is considered to be the father of football in Brazil. Miller founded São Paulo Athletic Club (SPAC), one of the oldest sports club in Brazil, and found the Liga Paulista de Foot-Ball, current Campeonato Paulista, Brazil's first football league. He is also considered the father of Rugby union in Brazil.

He was born in São Paulo to John Miller, a Scottish railway engineer and Brazilian mother of English descent, Carlota Fox.

In 1884 he was sent to the Banister Court public school in Southampton, England where he learnt to play football and cricket.
Whilst at school, he played for and against both the Corinthians and St. Mary's (now Southampton FC).
He was recorded in the 1891 United Kingdom census whilst a boarder at Millbrook School.

== Controversy and historical context ==

Hans Nobiling, Arthur Friedenreich, Fritz Essenfelder, Hermann Friese, Oscar Alfredo Cox, and Belfort Duarte were major promoters of football in Brazil.
Some historians contest Charles Miller's pioneering role in the history of Brazilian football.
These historians argue and present documentation as proof that Brazil's most popular sport was already practiced in the country before the return of the famous Brazilian footballer from his ancestors' land.
There are records of recreational and unorganized (popularly known as 'pelada') football practice in the country since the mid-1870s.
This early practice was carried out by sailors from merchant or warships of British, French, and Dutch origin.
One such event took place in 1878 in front of Palace Isabel (today Guanabara) in the city of Rio de Janeiro.
Miller was not the first practitioner of football.
According to this criticism, Miller's merit was arriving in Brazil with the necessary equipment to practice the sport in an organized manner and attracting the practice of football within clubs.
At that time, English people were more inclined towards cricket.
