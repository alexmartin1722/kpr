# Henry de Cornhill sheriff

Henry de Cornhill (c. 1135 – c. 1193) was a medieval English royal official and sheriff who served King Henry II of England. Henry's son King Richard I of England put him in charge of assembling part of the fleet for the Third Crusade, plus appointing him as sheriff of three jurisdictions. Through marriage he acquired lands in Somerset, Dorset, Oxfordshire, and Northamptonshire.

Henry de Cornhill was the eldest son of Gervase de Cornhill, a wealthy merchant from London who served as a royal judge multiple times and was Sheriff of Kent, Surrey, and London during the reign of King Henry II.
Henry de Cornhill was likely born around 1135.
Henry married Alice, the daughter of William de Courcy, and sister and heiress of William de Courcy, lord of Stogursey in Somerset.
Through this marriage, Henry acquired lands in Somerset, Dorset, Oxfordshire, and Northamptonshire worth 25 and a quarter knight's fees.
Henry's father died before September 1184.

By 1175, Henry was a royal official in charge of purchasing cloth and other items for the royal household.
He also was given custody of two estates that had escheated to the crown – Rayleigh in 1181 and Boulogne in 1183.
In 1181, he became the administrator of the lordship of Rayleigh.
Henry often acted as a purchaser for the king, for example in 1188 Cornhill spent 290 pounds on gold, jewels, furs, and cloth for the king.
Henry was present at the deathbed of Henry II in 1189.
He was one of the few loyal supporters who were still in King Henry II's retinue.
The new king, Richard I, kept Henry close and put him in charge of assembling the fleet that Richard needed to go on the Third Crusade.
Richard the Lionheart confirmed Cornhill's offices.
His accounts for his efforts in 1190 in assembling the fleet still survive.
These show that Henry bought part or all of 40 ships and paid out a year's wages for over a thousand sailors for the expedition.
The total expenses for Henry's efforts totalled over 5,000 pounds.
Henry was appointed to the office of Sheriff of Surrey early in the reign of King Richard I.
As a result of this inheritance, Henry became Sheriff of Surrey.
He was also Sheriff of Kent during Richard's reign, in 1190 as well as one of the Sheriffs of London from 1187 to 1189.
He served as one of the two Sheriffs of London.
Henry was only one of six sheriffs that retained office after the accession of Richard I.
He owed his appointments to the influence of William Longchamp, Richard's chancellor.
He was also Warden of the Mint in 1191.
Cornhill was appointed Keeper of the Exchange in 1190.
The appointment required a fee of 100 marks.
Henry lost his offices when Longchamp fell from favour in 1192.
He died sometime between Michaelmas 1192 and Michaelmas 1193.

== Marriage and barony ==

Henry of Cornhill was an English nobleman who married Alice de Courcy, the daughter of Baron William de Courcy.
Through this marriage, Henry acquired the Barony of Stogursey, which included over 25 knight's fees and additional knight's fees in other parts of England.
The inheritance elevated Henry to the status of a significant baron.

Henry inherited his father's possessions, including properties in London and other parts of England.
Henry's heir was his daughter Joan, who married Hugh de Neville, either in or soon after 1195.
Joan died after 1224.
He left debts at his death that his brothers Reginald de Cornhill and Ralph were responsible for.
His widow married Warin fitzGerold sometime in 1193.
Warin was the son of Henry fitzGerold.
