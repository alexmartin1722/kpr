# LaGuardia Airport

LaGuardia Airport is a civil airport in East Elmhurst, Queens, New York City. Covering , the facility was established in 1929 and began operating as a public airport in 1939. It is named after former New York City mayor Fiorello La Guardia.

The airport primarily accommodates airline service to domestic (and limited international) destinations. , it was the third-busiest airport in the New York metropolitan area, behind Kennedy and Newark airports, and the twenty-first busiest in the United States by passenger volume. The airport is located directly to the north of the Grand Central Parkway, the airport's primary access highway. While the airport is a hub for both American Airlines and Delta Air Lines, commercial service is strictly governed by unique regulations including a curfew, a slot system, and a "perimeter rule" prohibiting most non-stop flights to or from destinations greater than .

Throughout the 2000s and 2010s, LaGuardia was criticized for its outdated facilities, inefficient air operations, and poor customer service metrics. In response, the Port Authority of New York and New Jersey (PANYNJ) in 2015 announced a multibillion-dollar reconstruction of the airport's passenger infrastructure, which is expected to be completed by 2025.

== Preclearance requirements ==

Most flights departing from LaGuardia have a destination within the United States, with the exception being flights to Canada, specifically Toronto, Montreal, and Ottawa. Border preclearance operations take place for flights from these locations, where there is special customs control for US citizens at airports in Toronto, Montreal, and Ottawa. Currently, only international flights departing from 15 airports outside the United States can use this airport.

To mitigate the impact of aircraft noise pollution and facilitate airfield maintenance, a seasonal curfew traditionally exists between the hours of 12am and 6am during the warm months of the year.

== Early accidents (1945–1952) ==

On July 13, 1945, a United States Army Air Forces Douglas DC-4/C-54A-1-DO aircraft with registration number 41-107437 experienced a severe engine fire during flight, resulting in an emergency landing at New York-LaGuardia Airport where the aircraft was completely destroyed by fire, though all six crew members survived.
On May 29, 1947, a Douglas DC-4 aircraft with registration number NC30046, also known as United Airlines Flight 521, crashed at New York-La Guardia Airport after a delayed takeoff abort caused the aircraft to run off the airport grounds because the rudder locks were not released, leaving only 5 of the 48 passengers to survive.
On January 14, 1952, a Convair CV-240 aircraft belonging to Northeast Airlines with registration number N91238 crashed.

The crash happened approximately 1100 meters before the runway of New York-LaGuardia Airport and the crash site was in Flushing Bay.
Three crew members and thirty-three passengers were rescued.
The aircraft was written off as a total loss.

== Northeast Airlines Flight 823 crash ==

A Northeast Airlines Douglas DC-6A (registration N34954) was involved in an incident also known as Northeast Airlines Flight 823 on February 1, 1957.
The aircraft skimmed trees on Rikers Island shortly after takeoff, and the crash occurred 60 seconds after the aircraft left the ground.
There were 101 people on board, and 20 people died.

== American Airlines Flight 320 crash ==

The accident occurred on February 3, 1959.
The aircraft involved was a Lockheed L-188A Electra belonging to American Airlines with the registration number N6101A.
There were 73 people on board the aircraft.
The pilots performed an excessively steep approach to LaGuardia Airport.
The aircraft flew into the East River approximately 1500 meters before the runway.
65 people died in the accident.
This was the first total loss of an Electra aircraft.

== Mid-air collision over New York ==

The incident occurred on September 14, 1960.
United Airlines Flight 826 was a Douglas DC-8 that crashed in Brooklyn.
World Airways Flight 266 crashed in Staten Island.
135 people died in the World Airways and United Airlines collision, and 7 of the victims were on the ground.

== 1971 Douglas C-47 crash ==

A Douglas C-47 Skytrain aircraft crashed at LaGuardia Airport on January 4, 1971.
The cause of the crash was atmospheric turbulence.

== American Airlines Flight 96 incident ==

American Airlines Flight 96, a McDonnell Douglas DC-10, departed from Los Angeles International Airport on June 12, 1972, with a destination of LaGuardia Airport and stops at Detroit Metropolitan Airport and Buffalo Niagara International Airport.
The rear cargo door failed, causing damage to the primary flight controls.
The pilot and co-pilot successfully executed an emergency landing at Detroit Metropolitan Airport.

== 1975 LaGuardia Airport bombing ==

A bomb exploded on December 29, 1975, in the main building of LaGuardia Airport.
The explosion killed 11 people and injured 74.
Among the victims were travelers, limousine drivers, and airline employees.

== US Airways Flight 5050 crash ==

On September 21, 1989, US Airways Flight 5050, which was traveling from Charlotte to New York, overshot the runway during takeoff at the edge of LaGuardia Airport's runway and crashed into Flushing Bay and the East River.
The aircraft broke into three pieces, and two passengers and three people died.

== US Airways Flight 405 crash ==

US Airways Flight 405 crashed on March 22, 1992, while traveling from New York to Cleveland, Ohio.
The takeoff was aborted due to a snowstorm.
Icy weather conditions caused the crash, which was caused by ice accumulation on the wings.
There were 51 people on board the flight.
27 people died in the accident, and 24 people survived the accident.

== Continental Airlines Flight 705 incident ==

Continental Airlines Flight 705 crashed on March 2, 1994, while traveling from New York to Denver.
All 116 passengers and crew members survived the incident.

== United Airlines Flight 1549 incident ==

On January 15, 2009, United Airlines Flight 1549 was traveling from New York to Charlotte when the crash occurred during takeoff.
US Airways Flight 1549 took off on January 15, 2009, and the aircraft collided with a flock of birds shortly after takeoff.
As a result of the collision, both engines lost power, and the aircraft made an emergency landing on the Hudson River.
No one died in the incident.

== 1975 Terminal B bombing ==

On December 29, 1975, a bomb explosion occurred at Terminal B of the port, killing 11 people and injuring 74 people.
The perpetrators of the attack were not identified.

== 1989 USAir Flight 1016 crash ==

A USAir Boeing 737-400 with registration N416US crashed on September 21, 1989, during takeoff [cite: 46,47].
The crash occurred after the takeoff roll went beyond the runway end, causing the plane to plunge into the East River [cite: 45].
The aircraft broke into three pieces, and two passengers died [cite: 45].
The causes included a side rudder trimmed contrary to the checklists and unclear and misunderstood commands from the captain [cite: 45].

== 1992 USAir Flight 405 crash ==

On March 22, 1992, a USAir Fokker F28-4000 aircraft with registration number N485US crashed in Flushing Bay while heading to Cleveland.
The crash was caused by ice accumulation on the wings.
There were 51 people on board, and 27 people died.

== 2003 Northwest Airlines ground collision ==

On January 19, 2003, a mechanic pushed a Northwest Airlines Airbus A319 with registration N313NB and applied excessive thrust while moving the aircraft.
The aircraft struck a Boeing 757-251 with registration N550NW and the concrete base of an airplane bridge [cite: 48].
The impact speed was high enough to cause total damage to the Airbus, and a crack measuring 2 meters by 0.60 meters was created in the fuselage of the Boeing 757 [cite: 48].
No people were injured and no people were killed [cite: 48].

== 2009 US Airways Flight 1549 Hudson River landing ==

On January 15, 2009, an Airbus A320 operated by US Airways (registration N106US) took off from LaGuardia Airport with an intended destination of Charlotte Douglas International Airport [cite: 49,50].
Shortly after takeoff during the aircraft's climb, both engines experienced problems and failed due to a bird strike caused by geese that entered the Airbus engines [cite: 49,50].
The pilot in command, Chesley Sullenberger, performed an emergency water landing on the Hudson River [cite: 49,50].
All 155 people on board were rescued and survived [cite: 49,50].
Rescue operations involved harbor ferries and other watercraft [cite: 49,50].

On January 15, 2009, US Airways Flight 1549 departed from New York en route to Seattle via Charlotte.
The flight took off from the airport, and birds entered the engines causing the failure at an altitude of 900 meters.
The aircraft made an emergency landing in the waters of the Hudson River near Manhattan, New York City, west of Manhattan Island.
All people on board survived.

== 2013 Southwest Airlines nose gear collapse ==

On July 22, 2013, the nose gear of a Southwest Airlines Boeing 737-700 (registration N753SW) collapsed during landing.
Ten people sustained minor injuries, and the aircraft had to be written off.

== 2015 Delta Air Lines Flight 1086 runway excursion ==

On March 5, 2015, around 11:00 AM local time, Delta Air Lines Flight 1086, a McDonnell Douglas MD-88 with registration N909DL, slid off the runway during landing and collided with a fence beside the runway, where it remained stuck.
Of the 132 people on board, 29 passengers were injured, and the aircraft was damaged beyond repair.

== 2018 Southwest Airlines Flight 1380 emergency landing ==

Southwest Airlines Flight 1380 was forced to make an emergency landing on April 17, 2018, after the flight had an engine malfunction.

== Operational delays and schedule revisions ==

Flight delays once reached approximately 30%, a rate that was among the worst in the United States at that time.
Airlines revised their flight schedules to address the issue, and these schedule revisions led to significant improvements in delays.

== 1950s Lockheed Electra incident ==

A Lockheed L-188A Electra aircraft with registration number N6127A was involved in an incident where the main landing gear scraped an unmarked dike just short of the runway.
All 76 passengers survived the event, and there were 76 people on board.

== 1975 Bombing ==

A bomb attack occurred at the airport on December 29, 1975.
The attack killed 11 people and injured 74.
The bomb attack was never solved.

== Miracle on the Hudson ==

On January 15, 2009, a US Airways flight designated as Flight 1549 departed from this airport.
The aircraft involved was an Airbus A320 that collided with a flock of Canada geese.
Birds struck both engines of the aircraft, and the aircraft could not continue its flight due to the engine failure.
The pilots had to land the aircraft on the Hudson River, and the pilot successfully made a water landing in the Hudson River.
This event is known as the 'Miracle on the Hudson'.

== Other bird strike incident ==

A McDonnell Douglas MD-88 with registration number N909DL, which originated in Atlanta and was destined for New York LaGuardia Airport, collided with a flock of birds shortly after takeoff while en route to Charlotte, North Carolina.
The collision caused the engines to catch fire, and the aircraft failed to land at the airport.
There were 127 passengers and 5 crew members on the flight, and all people on board survived the incident.
Twenty-eight people were injured in the accident, and 5 people were transported to hospitals in the city.
New York City was experiencing heavy snowfall at the time of the accident.
