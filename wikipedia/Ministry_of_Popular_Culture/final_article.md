# Ministry of Popular Culture

The Ministry of Popular Culture (, commonly abbreviated to MinCulPop) was a ministry of the Italian government from 1937 to 1944.

It was established by the Fascist government in 1922 as the Press Office of the Presidency of the Council, before being renamed to Press Office of the Head of Government in 1925.
In 1934 it became the Secretariat for Press and Propaganda.
It became a ministry in 1935 and was given its definitive designation in 1937.
During its existence, it controlled most of the literary and radio channels in Italy.
It was the Italian analogue of the German Reich Ministry of Public Enlightenment and Propaganda.

The Ministry financed various Fascist publications, including La Difesa della Razza.
It famously outlawed the importation and translation of all American comic books, with the lone exception of Mickey Mouse, in 1938.

The Ministry was officially suppressed by the Kingdom of Italy on 3 July 1944, having remained vacant ever since the overthrow of Benito Mussolini in the 25 Luglio coup a year earlier.
During the Italian Social Republic, Mussolini revived the Ministry of Popular Culture and appointed Ferdinando Mezzasoma as its head.

== Evolution of propaganda strategy ==

Agitation propaganda was a subversive type of propaganda used between 1919 and 1922 and between 1924 and 1926 that aimed to trigger violence and rebellion.
The regime implemented a new concept of cultural policy and transitioned in 1926 from agitation propaganda to integration propaganda.
This transition was due to a significant expansion of the functions of the government chief's press service starting in 1926, which became one of the main developers of this type of propaganda.

== Organizational expansion in the 1920s ==

The expansion of the press office began in 1926, and Capasso Torre had been directing the press office since 1924. Mussolini placed the press office of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs under the direction of Capasso Torre, and the press service of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs became a section of the head of government's press service. The head of government's press service was divided into two internal sections. The section for Italian press was based at the Viminale and worked in close collaboration with the Ministry of the Interior.
The employees of the section were a very small number of journalists and writers.
The main tasks of the press service included reading all major newspapers and periodicals published in the country.
Various materials were distributed to journalists, including photographs and instructions on how to handle certain current events topics.
Journal editors who published unauthorized information were reprimanded.
Financial subsidies were distributed to newspapers in the form of secret payments to influence the newspapers and make them dependent on the regime.
The foreign press section was based at Palazzo Chigi and had more employees than the Italian press section.
The tasks of the foreign press section included collecting articles concerning Italy.

== Foreign press operations ==

One of the tasks was coordinating foreign policy news.
Responses to questions posed by foreign journalists were distributed to the Ministry of Foreign Affairs.

== Leadership under Ferretti and structural changes ==

Lando Ferretti became director of the press office in September 1928 and modernized the office under his direction.
In 1929, the Italian press section was elevated to a general directorate.

== Leadership under Ciano and institutional upgrades ==

In 1933, Galeazzo Ciano took over the leadership of the press office and served as the press secretary.
Ciano was impressed by Joseph Goebbels' new Reich Ministry of Public Enlightenment and Propaganda.
In September 1934, Ciano upgraded his press office to the State Secretariat for Press and Propaganda via a royal decree.
The office was transformed into a State Undersecretariat for Press and Propaganda by a royal decree.
The new undersecretariat consisted of three general directorates responsible for the Italian press, foreign press, and propaganda.
A fourth general directorate of cinematography was created by a royal decree.
The new under-secretariat assumed the competencies of the Tourism Commissioner, which was transformed into a general directorate for this occasion.
The under-secretariat became the Ministry of Press and Propaganda.

== Leadership under Alfieri and Pavolini ==

Dino Alfieri succeeded Galeazzo Ciano as the head of the press office, and the office was expanded under his leadership.
Alessandro Pavolini led the ministry from 1939 to 1943.

== Departmental structure and directorates ==

In 1936, the ministry was divided into General Directorates (departments).
There was a General Directorate for Italian Press, a General Directorate for Foreign Press, a General Directorate for Propaganda, a General Directorate for Theater, a General Directorate for the Istituto Nazionale del Dramma Antico, a General Directorate for the State Phonotheca (Records), and a General Directorate for the Hotel Credit Committee.
There was a General Directorate for Film, which included Cinecittà and Centro Sperimentale di Cinematografia.
There was a General Directorate for Tourism, which included subordinate tourism offices.
There was a General Directorate for Istituto Luce (Cinema).
A new general directorate was created for the performing arts.

== Control over cultural sectors ==

The ministry took over the content direction of the press, visual arts, film, theater, and music.
The ministry controlled music in Italy and theater in Italy [cite: 1].
Promoting fascist propaganda cinema was an important task.

== Tourism and related entities ==

The ministry promoted tourism and controlled tourism in Italy.
Various organizations and institutes were placed under the direct authority of the ministry.
Istituto Luce, the National Office of Tourism Industries (ENIT), the National Institute of Ancient Dramas, and the State Discotheque were entities.
Provincial tourist offices exist.
The hotel credit committee is mentioned.

== Final years and abolition ==

The Italian Social Republic (RSI) was established and the Ministry of Popular Culture was moved to Salò.
A legislative decree issued by the Bonomi II government abolished the ministry and replaced it with a sub-secretariat for press and information that operates under the presidency of the Council of Ministers [cite: 2].
A decree transformed the ministry into a State Secretariat for Press, Performing Arts, and Tourism, which was later abolished by another legislative decree.
In June 1935, it was further upgraded to the Ministry of Press and Propaganda.
The office was renamed on May 22, 1937.
It had a Vice-Secretariat for Popular Education.
The decision provided impetus to the fascist regime.
The fascist regime sought to centralize more competencies within a single ministry.

== Themes of integration propaganda ==

Integration propaganda was disseminated and developed through censorship and revision activities by the press office in the 1920s and early 1930s.
This propaganda was based on two general themes: the myth of the Duce and the idea of the New Italy.
The myth of the Duce involved constructing a popular image of Mussolini that rejected the values of pre-fascist Italy.
The idea of the New Italy involved constructing a fictional image that depicted Italy as stable, well-ordered, and vigorous, where society led a sober and moralistic life and embodied fascist ideals and values.
Pre-fascist Italy was characterized by bourgeois liberalism, to which a negative value was attributed because liberal and bourgeois values were considered symptoms of a corrupt and decadent society.
These values were presented to the masses as outdated and obsolete with the goal of convincing them that the ordered and stable society of New Italy must completely reject them.
A very important aspect of integration propaganda was the mediation between the new and the traditional, which occurred during the construction of the myth of the Duce and the idea of New Italy.
The Press Bureau was responsible for carrying out this mediation.
The resulting propaganda presented themes of the new and social and moral modernization brought by fascism while remaining linked to the traditional values of the agricultural and rural world.

These traditional values were the basic values of the majority of Italians.

== Construction of the Duce myth ==

The press service constructed the myth of the Duce by aiming to create an image of a man with superior qualities compared to the norm, to show that the Duce was close to the lives of the masses, and to show that the Duce was close to rural life values.
The Fascist press service constructed an image of Mussolini as a man with almost unreal qualities, portraying him as having absolute authority and as a symbol of vigor and masculinity.
One method was instructing newspapers to omit news about the Duce's illnesses, to omit news about the Duce's birthday (his advanced age), and to omit news about the Duce's private life.
The directive stated that Mussolini is no longer just the head of government and that Mussolini is the Duce.
There was a rule requiring all of Mussolini's speeches to be published on the front page.
Journalists were forbidden to praise other politicians, and the only politicians journalists could praise were Mussolini and the king.
Information about the Duce's athletic exploits was disseminated, and the Duce's prowess as an athlete was highlighted.
Photographs showed Mussolini riding a horse, swimming, and directing military maneuvers.

Mussolini's image was created to appear close to the lives of the masses.
The photographs showed Mussolini wearing work clothes among peasants, driving a tractor, and using a blowtorch.

== Promotion of the New Italy and social control ==

The Press Bureau carried out the construction of the idea of New Italy and promoted the abolition of the use of Christmas trees and traditional ways of celebrating New Year's Day, which were considered specific to an archaic bourgeois society.
The broader campaign, pursued by the regime for more than a decade, aimed to eliminate news from newspapers that suggested fascism did not fully control national life and to convince Italians that fascism had created a stable and orderly society [cite: 10].
The Press Bureau implemented a campaign against crime through an order from Capasso Torre to newspapers, which were ordered not to use slogans, not to use photographs, and not to publish articles referring to criminal episodes such as epidemics, crimes of passion, and suicides [cite: 10].
The regime sought to strengthen the traditional family and promote the value of motherhood as a symbol of social stability by banning images of half-naked women and excessively thin women from publication, suppressing beauty contests, and awarding prizes to the largest families [cite: 10].
The regime attempted to impose a traditional view of women's roles on Italians [cite: 10].

Women were expected to remain tied to their duties as wives and mothers.

== Debate on cultural unification and Ciano's reforms ==

In the early 1930s, there was a need within the regime for systematic control of propaganda and culture, which was to be placed under a single unified structure.
The goal of this unification was to give propaganda a cultural function, involving the use of propaganda to make fascist cultural politics a concrete reality for the masses and to create a bridge between fascist culture and the majority of Italians.
Proposals for creating a single organization to control both propaganda and culture appeared in the journal Critica fascista, and in 1933, the creation of a Ministry of Propaganda was suggested within the journal Critica fascista.
The suggested Ministry of Propaganda was inspired by Joseph Goebbels' Ministry of Propaganda in Nazi Germany.
Following this debate, it became apparent that the government chief's press service was no longer suitable for the regime's cultural ambitions because the press service had defined general criteria for propaganda but had not paid attention to culture and cultural issues or used propaganda tools and techniques to make fascist values a concrete reality for the masses.
Galeazzo Ciano, who was Mussolini's son-in-law, was appointed director of the office in August 1933 and sought to remedy the shortcomings of the press office.
Ciano reorganized the press service between August 1933 and September 1934 with the goal of expanding the functions of the press service and aligning its activities with cultural issues and culture.
In 1934, press attachés were assigned to the prefects of Rome, Florence, Milan, Turin, Bologna, Naples, and Palermo to make the dissemination and collection of information more effective.

The reorganization included an extension of press controls and surveillance, while subsidies were increased for newspapers and individual journalists.
There was an interest in mass media, preliminary studies on radio and cinema were launched, and a project was proposed to create a section of the office dedicated to radio and cinema.
The management of the regime's foreign propaganda was centralized under the Press Office, whereas previously it had been handled by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs.
Funds necessary for creating a propaganda section were transferred to the Press Office itself, and a foreign propaganda section was created within the Press Office.
The construction of the idea of New Italy was based on a negative value attributed by the regime.

== Creation of the Propaganda Section ==

The Propaganda Section was created by Ciano in early 1934 and became operational starting in April 1934. This new entity functioned as a foreign propaganda bureau within the Press Office and was responsible for organizing the regime's foreign propaganda. The offices of the propaganda section were located in the building of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, yet the section was an external organ separate from the Ministry of Foreign Affairs itself. The cabinet of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs reiterated the impossibility of clearly separating foreign policy and foreign propaganda. Because clear separation is impossible, the new propaganda section and the ministry itself must maintain close relations, and each entity must be informed of the other's activities. == Establishment of the Undersecretariat ==

On September 6, 1934, Mussolini abolished the government chief's press office and replaced it with the Undersecretariat for Press and Propaganda [cite: 16].
Galeazzo Ciano was appointed Undersecretary for Press and Propaganda and was granted full powers to issue decrees and make decisions regarding press and propaganda.
This body represents a total reorganization of the press service aimed at making it more suitable for the new ambitions of fascist cultural policy.
The reorganization represents a step forward in the unification of propaganda and culture under a single structure, a unification considered a fundamental necessity of fascist cultural policy by the late 1920s.
The presence of the word 'propaganda' indicates the regime's desire to make the dissemination of fascism in Italy and abroad more systematic, while the persistence of the word 'press' indicates that newspapers are still considered the main instruments of political and cultural propaganda for the State.
The creation of the Undersecretariat for Press and Propaganda was influenced by the Ministry of Public Enlightenment and Propaganda created by Joseph Goebbels in Germany [cite: 15].
Goebbels traveled to Rome in 1933, where he visited major fascist cultural institutions and met with both Mussolini and Ciano [cite: 15].
In 1934, Ciano began studying the ministry created by Goebbels with the purpose of using it to introduce major changes within the Press Bureau.

== Internal Structure and Directorates ==

The General Directorate of Italian Press was created in 1929 within the press office by elevating the Italian Press Section to a general directorate and was included in the Undersecretariat for Press and Propaganda.
This directorate was responsible for issuing directives to newspapers through what were called 'veils' or press orders.
The General Directorate of Foreign Press was also created in 1929 within the press service and was elevated to the status of a general directorate from the section of foreign press.
The General Directorate of Propaganda collaborated with organizations working abroad, including CAUR, Dante Alighieri, and the Fascist Institute of Culture Abroad.
This directorate organized cultural events abroad and sent large quantities of propaganda material abroad.
Between 1934 and 1935, the Undersecretariat for Press and Propaganda defined its control over propaganda.
The General Directorate of Cinematography was responsible for film credit and for organizing film exhibitions and congresses.
The Undersecretariat extended its control to culture, which increased its influence on mass media and resulted in the expansion of the internal structure of the Undersecretariat.

The Direzione Generale per la Cinematografia (General Directorate of Cinematography) was created by decree on September 18, 1934, and was responsible for censorship and revision in the theatrical field as well as for authorizing the creation of new cinemas.
Fascism never created a state cinematography, and while the expansion of the Undersecretariat's control over cinema indicates that the regime finally recognized the importance of this new mass communication medium, this recognition did not produce a very significant change in how this cultural activity was managed, with the main activities of the General Directorate of Cinematography remaining censorship and revision.
The General Directorate of Tourism was created by decree on November 21, 1934.
The General Inspection of Theater and Music was established by a decree in April 1935, and it would have been premature to create a separate division for managing a medium still in an experimental phase.
The General Inspection of Broadcasting was created by decree on December 3, 1934, and this inspection also dealt with television.

== Elevation to Ministry and Expansion ==

Ciano and Mussolini realized that the Undersecretariat for Press and Propaganda was no longer adequate within a few months.
Galeazzo Ciano was appointed Minister of Press and Propaganda.
On June 24, 1935, the Undersecretariat for Press and Propaganda was transformed into the Ministry of Press and Propaganda.
The Ministry of Press and Propaganda retained the same internal structure as its predecessor, the Undersecretariat, and expanded this structure to include additional areas of culture and propaganda.
This extension was achieved by placing the Automobile Club, the SIAE (Italian Society of Authors and Publishers), the San Carlo Theatre, the La Scala Theatre, and the EIAR under the ministry's tutelage.
The ministry itself was also subject to the tutelage of the General Directorate of Posts and Telegraphs of the Communications Ministry.
The ministry was granted the power to appoint press attachés in the prefectures.
A law was passed on October 24, 1935.
The law granted special powers of seizure and suppression of newspapers to the general directorates of Italian and foreign press, which were initially assigned to the Ministry of the Interior.
The 'Ispettorato per il teatro e la musica' (Inspection of theater and music) was elevated in 1936 to the rank of 'Direzione Generale per lo spettacolo' (General Directorate for the performing arts).
The new directorate was charged with unifying and coordinating all organizations dealing with the performing arts.

The main activity of these organizations is censorship.

== Book and Press Censorship ==

A division for books, or Division III, was created within the General Directorate of Italian Press of the Ministry and was directed by Amedeo Tosti.
The division's staff consisted of eight journalists for Italian books and thirty-two for foreign books.
The Ministry of Press and Propaganda removed book censorship activities from the Ministry of the Interior upon the creation of this division, and the censorship criteria used by the division were the same as those used by the Ministry of the Interior.
Prefects were responsible for identifying books suspected of containing anti-fascist sentiments and for transmitting these suspected books to the Ministry.
The Ministry decided whether to seize the books, ban their reprinting, censor them, or request their revision, and the ministry ordered the prefects to seize these books.
The ministry itself sometimes reported certain suspect books to the prefects.

== Control of Cultural Unions and Theater ==

Mass theater is a type of theater capable of attracting peasants and workers through the organization of low-cost theatrical performances.
The ministry extended its control over cultural unions with the goal of removing the unions' ability to control their respective fields of competence.
The Union of Authors and Writers was forbidden from handling translations of foreign works and from handling censorship, and these tasks were assigned to the ministry.
The Confederation of Artists and Professionals had to obtain approval from the ministry before undertaking any cultural propaganda activity.
The ministry attempted to effectively group all productive forces of the sector under the Confederation of Artists and Professionals by requiring local clubs and cultural associations to provide the confederation with a list of their members.
In 1936, all writers participating in literary contests were required to register with the Union of Authors and Writers.

== War Propaganda ==

During the Ethiopian War (1935–1936), the Ministry of Press and Propaganda served as the main designer and disseminator of war propaganda.
The Ministry abandoned the integration propaganda established since 1926 and concentrated all its efforts on agitation propaganda aimed at countering British and French propaganda.

== Establishment and renaming ==

Galeazzo Ciano became the Minister of Foreign Affairs in June 1936, and Dino Alfieri, who was previously the Undersecretary of the Ministry, was appointed Minister of Press and Propaganda in June 1936.
Mussolini changed the name of the Ministry of Press and Propaganda to the Ministry of Popular Culture on May 27, 1937 [cite: 27].
Dino Alfieri was the Minister of Press and Propaganda at the time and was automatically appointed as the Minister of Popular Culture upon its creation.
There is strong political and institutional continuity between the new Ministry of Popular Culture and the former Ministry of Press and Propaganda [cite: 27].
The new ministry retained the same structure as the old Ministry of Press and Propaganda, and this structural continuity lasted throughout the year 1937 [cite: 27].

== Official rationale and rhetoric ==

The official explanation for the new name was that the expression 'popular culture' indicated and celebrated the broadening of the ministry's objectives.
Dino Alfieri suggested in a report to the Senate in early 1937 that the ministry aimed to advance in the simultaneous control of culture and propaganda, an aim that involved targeting the unification of the two sectors.
The masses were to be educated according to fascist principles and values, and this unification was required for the regime's cultural policy to become a concrete reality for the people and the masses.
Cultural activities should not be a privilege reserved for a few, and these actions aimed to realize the fascist revolution that had been discussed since the 1920s.
The goal was to define more precisely the nature and function of popular culture within the regime.
The same concept was expressed in a speech delivered by Pavolini on April 24, 1941, in which he stated that popular culture concerns the general body of citizens and applies simultaneously to both cities and villages.
Official explanations regarding the nature of popular culture and the name change of the ministry were merely rhetoric.
The expression 'popular culture' was primarily a substitute for the term 'propaganda'.
Mussolini and Ciano considered the drawbacks of the term 'propaganda' in the early 1930s, and Ciano stated that people no longer wanted to be propagandized but wanted to be informed instead.
They proposed to remove the word 'propaganda' from official vocabulary.

The popular culture program had been discussed since the early 1930s, and the discussion on the drawbacks of the term continued throughout the 1930s.
Directives were issued on June 3, 1939, and on February 5, 1942, which asked journalists to avoid using the word 'propaganda' when referring to government activity.
The official explanation that the Ministry of Popular Culture was supposed to launch a genuine popular culture program aimed at culturally uplifting the masses was pure rhetoric.
This fact is demonstrated primarily by the type of activities and functions performed by the ministry itself.

== Functions and operational reality ==

Theoretically, the ministry's work was to be divided into two types of functions: dynamic functions that aimed to unify culture and propaganda to implement a popular culture program, and static functions that aimed to perfect totalitarian control of culture.
In a speech to the Chamber of Deputies given in early 1937, Dino Alfieri highlighted issues and gaps in this control.
The actual activity of the ministry was characterized essentially by the fulfillment of static functions, while the dynamic functions were largely neglected.

== Press and propaganda apparatus ==

The goal of the agitation propaganda was to boost the morale of the population and to generate feelings of enthusiasm for the war.

== Racial policy and anti-Jewish campaign ==

A Bureau of Race was created and was responsible for racial propaganda throughout the country.
In 1939, the Bureau of Race changed its name to the Bureau of Studies and Racial Propaganda.
The Ministry of Popular Culture launched an anti-Jewish campaign in 1938 that was linked to the regime's xenophobic campaign and related to the theme of 'romanity'.
The Ministry attempted to convince Italians of the existence of a pure 'Italic race' that was fully realized in Imperial Rome but became contaminated by foreign influences after the fall of the Roman Empire.
The recovery of the values and principles of the pure 'Italic race' was considered possible only by purifying national culture and targeting foreign influences.
The 'Ufficio studi del problema della razza' was a bureau within the Ministry of Popular Culture that played a fundamental role in the anti-Jewish campaign.
This effort aimed to free magazines, cinema, theater, and all other cultural activities from Jewish influences.

Documentaries and exhibitions were produced and organized to convince Italians of the existence of an ideal Italic type.
The 'Race Exhibition' was one such event.

== Cultural policy failures and intellectual rebellion ==

Propaganda campaigns launched by the Ministry of Popular Culture and their linked cultural themes generally testify to the failure of the regime's cultural policy.
The regime claimed to have achieved a 'cultural revolution' that was a failure, and it was aware of its failure in the cultural field.
The goal of these campaigns was to give Italians at least the illusion and appearance of important social and economic changes.
The Ministry of Popular Culture had a repressive and reactionary attitude and aimed to increase its totalitarian control over culture.
This attitude led to the development of strong intellectual rebellion against the regime's cultural policy in the late 1930s.
The rebellion involved intellectuals who were established before fascism and a new generation of intellectuals formed under the fascist regime.

== Giuseppe Bottai's reform efforts ==

Giuseppe Bottai was a central figure in the intellectual rebellion against the regime's reactionary and repressive cultural policy and served as the director of the fascist-inspired journal Critica fascista.
Bottai was the minister of national education and successfully rebelled against the regime's cultural policy by opening his review to authors and intellectuals who declared themselves anti-fascist.
Bottai's most important battle was attempting to place control of national artistic life under the Ministry of National Education and to remove control of artistic activities from the Ministry of Popular Culture.
On July 4, 1938, Bottai gave a speech at a meeting of superintendents of antiquities and fine arts that took place under the Ministry of National Education.
In the speech, Bottai emphasized the urgent need for the regime to further centralize the country's artistic life under its control through a proposed single unitary body, the Ministry of National Education.
Between 1939 and 1940, the Ministry of National Education effectively extended its control over the country's artistic life.
One measure was the law of May 22, 1939, which increased the number of superintendencies within the ministry to fifty-eight.
Under this law, each superintendency was assigned a single area of competence and had full authority over its assigned area of competence.
The Office of Contemporary Art was created in January 1940 and depended directly on the General Directorate of Antiquities and Fine Arts, which belonged to the Ministry.

Italy entered the war a few months after the bureau was created.
The bureau could not develop a clear and specific action program.
The bureau represents the most important bureaucratic triumph of the Ministry of National Education over the Ministry of Popular Culture.

The debate between traditionalism and modernism in art resurfaced after the adoption of racial laws in 1938.
Bottai was one of the most ardent defenders of the racial laws and engaged in the debate between traditionalism and modernism in art while continuing his fight for cultural dialogue and freedom of expression.
Some antisemites and some members of the PNF (National Fascist Party) claimed that modernism was the product of foreign and Jewish influences and argued that the state should not worry about defending or preserving modernism.
Bottai stated this position more than once in Critica Fascista and argued that the regime should not ignore the defense and protection of modern art.
Bottai claimed that modernism in art was not the product of foreign influences and was not the product of decadent influences.
In February 1939, Bottai published in the journal Le arti and attempted to demonstrate the compatibility of modern art and fascist cultural policy.
Bottai stated that cultural dialogue can only benefit fascism because it awakens public interest and stimulates artistic creativity.
Bottai spoke at the Venice Biennale in 1938, opposed equating art with propaganda, and stated that the regime should not aim for an absolute fusion of artistic and political interests.

== Pavolini's repressive centralization ==

Alessandro Pavolini was appointed Minister of Popular Culture on October 31, 1939, and Dino Alfieri was appointed ambassador to Berlin in 1939.
The Ministry of Popular Culture's attitude towards culture became more aggressive and repressive, a change caused by Pavolini.
The goal was to achieve greater centralization of control over cultural activities, and the goal of this strengthening was to allow the ministry to focus all its efforts on war propaganda.
The Ministry of Popular Culture received special powers covering all matters concerning the press and other mass media.
In December 1941, the number of press attachés at the ministry increased from 10 to 15, and the number of general inspectors at the ministry was doubled.
The ministry suppressed avant-garde literary magazines between 1939 and 1943, including Campo di Marte, Oggi, L'Italiano, and Il Frontespizio.

== Expansion of censorship and publishing control ==

A law enacted on January 18, 1943, established the book division of the General Directorate of Press, which was tasked with controlling publications and distributing nulla osta (approval) for publications concerning international relations, military defense, and war events.
A law passed on April 19, 1943, allowed the ministry to grant permission to create new publishing projects and to revoke permission for existing publishing projects.

== Integration of the Institute of Culture ==

In 1940, the head of propaganda services for the Ministry of Popular Culture was added to the board of directors of the Institute of Culture, and a special unit of the Institute of Culture was created within the General Directorate of Propaganda of the Ministry.
Pavolini made the Institute of Culture a true branch of the Ministry of Popular Culture for the purpose of making it an instrument of war propaganda in the provinces.
Local branches of the Institute of Culture organized conferences, meetings, and art exhibitions, and used documentary films about war events.
Starting from the end of 1942, local branches used truck-mounted projectors to broadcast documentary films about war events in rural areas.

== New surveillance and mobilization units ==

The service was established within the Radio Broadcasting Inspection and a foreign radio and radiotelegraphic listening service was created in December 1939.
A civil mobilization office was created in 1940 and was established within the General Directorate of Administrative Services.

== Military coordination and power concessions ==

The Ministry of Popular Culture experienced an increase in control over culture during this period.
A conflict of interest with the high command would have been counterproductive for the war.
To avoid creating a conflict of interest with the high command, the ministry had to give up some of its powers.
The surrendered powers were transferred to the Press and Propaganda Office of the Supreme Military Command, which was created in 1940.
This concession occurred with an agreement in May 1942 between Pavolini and General Ugo Cavallero.
The Supreme Command Press and Propaganda Bureau was required to review all publications concerning military phases of the war in advance to prevent the dissemination of important military secrets.

== Shift to wartime agitation propaganda ==

Italy decided to enter the war in 1940.
Starting from 1940, the MinCulPop focused all its energy on creating and disseminating internal war propaganda.
A fundamental characteristic of this propaganda was that it was not integration propaganda, which the regime had used since 1926.
This propaganda was agitation propaganda instead.
The goal of this agitation propaganda was to exalt national sentiment, violence, and the dynamism of action.
This understanding is achieved by analyzing meeting minutes from meetings held between the Minister of Popular Culture and newspaper editors-in-chief.
The 'Race Exhibition' took place in Rome in April 1940.

== Early origins and structural evolution (1922–1937) ==

On September 6, 1934, a specific office was transformed into the Undersecretariat of State for Press and Propaganda by Royal Decree number 1434.

== Shift to mass propaganda and cultural integration ==

Until 1926, the regime's cultural policy was directed mainly at controlling high culture and intellectuals through the creation of new cultural institutions and the fascistization of existing institutions.
The fundamental goal of the regime's cultural policy in the 1930s was 'going towards the people'.
Mussolini believed that cultural policy was not solely a tool to control intellectuals and suppress anti-fascist opposition, but that the regime could use it to take root in the country's cultural life and to shape the moral and social consciousness of Italians.
Bridging fascist culture and the masses would have allowed Mussolini to gain a broad base of popular consensus, which would have given his regime and leadership greater longevity.
In 1926, the regime shifted from agitation propaganda to integration propaganda.
This possibility resulted from a significant expansion of functions concerning the Press Office of the Head of Government, as these changes began in 1926.
Promoting fascist propaganda cinema was an important task, and the decision provided impetus for the fascist regime.
The fascist regime sought to centralize more competencies within a single ministry.

== Press control mechanisms and wartime directives ==

Between 1939 and 1943, the Minister of Popular Culture gave general press instructions to newspaper editors during meetings, which were followed by the issuance of more precise and clear press orders called 'veline' that represented the concrete application of the instructions given by the Minister during meetings with journalists.
One theme was an invitation not to place excessive trust in victory and not to consider the victory as already won.
This theme reflects the regime's awareness of Italy's military unpreparedness, its economic inferiority compared to Nazi Germany, and its military inferiority compared to Nazi Germany.
This awareness is evidenced by a report dated May 17, 1941, in which Pavolini ordered journalists not to present war events solely from the German perspective and to prioritize the Italian perspective.

== Propaganda of sacrifice and national unity ==

Sacrifice was an important theme, and the spirit of sacrifice was meant to distinguish Italians while people had to be aware that sacrifice was necessary to win the war.
An invitation to sacrifice is evidenced by a pamphlet from October 30, 1940, which states that one should not think butter is bad for health or oil is indigestible and that these things are sacrifices that can be supported very peacefully.
War quickly became a symbol to which the values of sacrifice, poverty, and personal deprivations were entrusted.
These values favored the unification of individuals into communities and promoted the development of a sense of community among individuals.
For this reason, war became a fundamental element of the regime in building a sense of national belonging.

== Dissolution and the Badoglio transition ==

On July 25, 1943, the king stripped Mussolini of the powers he had granted him in 1922 and appointed General Badoglio to form a new non-fascist government.
Badoglio retained the Ministry of Popular Culture, suspended all its activities, and used it only to transmit orders aimed at controlling the press.

Mussolini was held as a prisoner on Gran Sasso in September 1943 and was liberated by the Germans. [62,63]
Mussolini announced the creation of a new neo-fascist state based in Salò on September 23, 1943, which was named the Italian Social Republic (RSI). [62,63]
Some officials moved to Salò encouraged by news of the new neo-fascist state, and Mussolini transferred the headquarters of the Ministry of Popular Culture to Salò. [62,63]
Mezzasoma aimed to achieve effective totalitarian control over culture and implemented a more rigid and uncompromising cultural policy to this end.

The decrees of November 1943 were issued by the Council of Ministers of Salò and created a General Directorate of Press and Internal Radio.
This new directorate included a foreign correspondent and merged the Italian and Foreign Press Directorates and the Radio Inspection.
November 1943 decrees sanctioned the creation of the General Directorate of Performing Arts, which combined cinema and theater.
Mezzasoma ensured that fascism addressed culture in a global sense for the first time, an approach that included not only its control and dissemination but also its production.
Newspapers were considered true state agencies.

The book industry was considered a true state agency, while the film and theater industries were directly controlled by the state to form a state-managed film and theater industry.
Mezzasoma's work enabled the Ministry of Popular Culture to achieve control over culture in the Republic of Salò.

== German dominance and the end of the RSI ==

Mussolini's new government was completely manipulated by the Germans, and the German ambassador Rudolf Von Rahn supervised all of Mussolini's activities.
Between 1944 and 1945, film censorship required authorization from the Germans, and political and cultural decisions were made by the Germans.
The Ministry of Popular Culture had no authority or autonomy vis-a-vis the Nazis.
The new fascist government was characterized by instability, yet it was still necessary to have an administrative structure for the development and control of propaganda despite this instability.
There was a need to continue carrying out propaganda actions and cultural activities, and propaganda was used for twenty years.
The Italian Social Republic (RSI) was established in 1943, and the Ministry of Popular Culture was located in Salò.

One of the main tasks was reading all major newspapers and periodicals published in the country.

== Propaganda themes: The Duce and New Italy ==

The integration propaganda was based on two general themes: the myth of the Duce, which involved constructing a popular image of Mussolini, and the idea of the New Italy, which involved constructing an idealized image of Italy.
This image depicted a stable, well-ordered, and vigorous Italy where society led a sober and moralistic life that embodied fascist ideals and values while rejecting the values of pre-fascist Italy, which was characterized by its bourgeois liberalism.
The press office was involved in constructing the myth of the Duce by building an image of a man with superior qualities compared to the norm and seeking to demonstrate that the Duce was close to the lives of the masses and adhered to rural life values.
The image of Mussolini was constructed to portray him as a man with almost unreal qualities, absolute authority, and a symbol of vigor and masculinity, an image construction achieved by the Press Office.
Newspapers were instructed to omit news about the Duce's potential illnesses, his birthday due to his advancing age, and his private life, while the directive stated that Mussolini was not only the head of government.

The directive stated that Mussolini was also the Duce, and all speeches by Mussolini had to be published on the front page.
Journalists were prohibited from praising other political figures, a prohibition that applied to anyone outside of Mussolini or the King.
News about the Duce's athletic achievements were disseminated, including photographs depicting Mussolini riding a horse, swimming, and directing military maneuvers.
The image of Mussolini as a man close to the lives of the masses was created primarily through the publication of photographs showing him in work clothes among farmers, driving a tractor, and operating a flamethrower.
The construction of the idea of New Italy was achieved by the Press Office and was based on a negative value attributed by the regime to bourgeois liberalism of pre-fascist Italy.
These traditions were viewed as customs belonging to an outdated bourgeois society, and liberal and bourgeois values were viewed as symptoms of a corrupt and decadent society.
These values were presented to the masses as outdated and obsolete, with the goal to convince the masses that the New Italy's ordered and stable society should totally reject them.

Integration propaganda was a very important aspect.
The press office had to mediate between novelty and tradition in building the myth of the Duce and the idea of New Italy.
This mediation resulted in propaganda that presented themes of the new and social/moral modernization brought by fascism on one side.
On the other side, this propaganda remained linked to traditional values of the agricultural and rural world.
These traditional values were the basic values for the majority of Italians.

== Social engineering and family policy ==

The regime sought to strengthen the traditional family and promoted the value of motherhood as a symbol of social stability.
The regime attempted to impose a traditional view of the woman's role on Italians, expecting women to remain tied to their duties as wives and mothers.
There was a ban on publishing images of semi-nude women and excessively thin women, and beauty contests were eliminated.
Prizes were awarded to larger families.

The 'new man' was an individual endowed with a new culture and represented the highest expression of the 'fascist revolution' and the 'new Italy' that fascism wanted to build.
The concept of 'romanity' is linked to the concept of the 'new man'.

Bottai made a speech at the Venice Biennale in 1938 where he opposed equating art with propaganda and stated that the regime should not aim for an absolute fusion of artistic and political interests.
Intellectuals rebelled against the regime's cultural policy, which was reactionary and repressive, and this rebellion involved all fields of intellectual activity including the press, literature, theater, and cinema.

The magazine Primato, which was founded on March 1, 1940, and edited by Bottai, played a fundamental role in the fight against the regime's policy.
The last issue of Primato was published in 1943.
Bottai's magazine explicitly criticized the regime, advocated for cultural dialogue, opposed equating culture with propaganda, and expressed trust in a new generation of intellectuals who rebelled against the regime's repressive attitude in the cultural field.
Literary journals of the avant-garde were important on a literary level.
Letteratura was a journal edited by Bonsanti that hosted voices of anti-fascist authors.
Campo di Marte was a journal edited by Gatto and Pratolini that took a more explicit stance against the cultural policy of the regime.
The Ministry of Popular Culture suppressed Campo di Marte one year after its birth.
A meeting of playwrights that was significant in the theatrical sphere took place in May 1938.
Approximately thirty playwrights attended the meeting.
The attendees were members of the Confederation of Artists and Professionals.
The meeting was presided over by Marinetti.

Playwrights stated that Italian theater should be freed from the censorial activity of MinCulPop and approved a motion of the day.
The motion stated that theater should not be an instrument of propaganda presenting an illusory and apparent reality and should represent the concrete reality of the country.
The Teatro delle Arti was directed by Anton Giulio Bragaglia and was the most important form of rebellion against the repressive cultural policy of MinCulPop in the theatrical field.
The Teatro delle Arti was not completely free from the regime's censorship but managed to bring works by foreign authors like O'Neill and Dostoevskij to Italian theaters.
The Teatro delle Arti replaced the illusory reality constructed by the regime with the concrete reality of the country in the theatrical field.
There were various forms of intellectual rebellion against the fascist cultural policy.
These forms of rebellion are clear evidence of the regime's failure to achieve its cultural ambitions.
The rebellions demonstrate that fascism had not yet achieved effective totalitarian control over national culture by the late 1930s.
This awareness is evidenced by a report dated May 17, 1941.
In this report, Pavolini ordered journalists not to present war events solely from the German perspective and to consider primarily the Italian perspective.
Sacrifice was an important theme.
The spirit of sacrifice should have characterized Italians during wartime.
Italians were expected to be aware that sacrifice was necessary for victory.
This call to sacrifice is evidenced by a directive dated October 30, 1940.
No one should be told that butter is bad for health.

No one should be told that oil is indigestible, and people were instructed to state that sacrifices were endured very calmly.
War quickly became a symbol to which the values of sacrifice and poverty were entrusted, values that would favor the union of individual people into communities and thereby make war a fundamental element for building a sense of national belonging.
The conflict was interpreted as a war between gold and blood and as a struggle between matter and faith, with propaganda presenting it as a conflict between two opposing groups.
One group valued wealth above all else and represented materialism, primarily including Anglo-Saxon peoples, the British Empire, and the United States, while the other group valued self-sacrifice, poverty, and a sense of national belonging based on these values and consisted of Italians and their allies.
The peoples of gold were often represented negatively by adopting images from classical mythology, including the concept of generating wealth even from misfortunes, which was represented through the figure of the mythical King Midas.
Antisemitism was a background theme that came from the ministry's war propaganda.

Journalists were ordered not to cover Moravia or his works, and newspapers were forbidden from publishing Jewish advertisements.
The Ministry of Popular Culture strengthened and revived the cult of personality of the Duce, used wartime propaganda, and accompanied this revival with a tendency not to value the work of military hierarchs and commanders engaged in the war.
The Ministry of Popular Culture fought against voices from foreign radio stations and launched a violent campaign against those who listened to foreign voices.
The Ministry of Popular Culture failed to completely eliminate the influence of foreign voices, which began appearing in Italian radio broadcasts starting in 1942.
The Ministry of Popular Culture concentrated all its efforts on war propaganda.
