# Rudi Blesh

Rudolph Pickett Blesh (January 21, 1899 – August 25, 1985) was an American jazz critic and enthusiast.

Rudi Blesh was born in Guthrie, Oklahoma.
Blesh studied at Dartmouth College and worked as a jazz critic in the early 1940s.
He wrote for the San Francisco Chronicle in the early 1940s and started writing for the New York Herald Tribune in 1944.
He was a prolific promoter of jazz concerts, particularly New Orleans jazz, and hosted a jazz radio program, This Is Jazz, in 1947.
(These shows have been reissued by Jazzology Records.)

Blesh in collaboration with Harriet Janis (mother of actor and jazz band leader Conrad Janis) wrote They All Played Ragtime which was published in 1950 by Alfred A.
Knopf.
A promotional record consisting of "Maple Leaf Rag" recorded to piano roll by Jelly Roll Morton in 1907 and an interview with the co-authors was sent to radio stations.
They All Played Ragtime proved to be a popular book and is credited as the cause for a renewed public interest in ragtime music.
Blesh founded Circle Records in 1946, which recorded new material from aging early jazz musicians as well as the Library of Congress recordings of Jelly Roll Morton.
Together they sparked renewed interest in the music of Joseph Lamb, James P.
Johnson, and Eubie Blake, among others.

Blesh retired from writing in 1971.
He taught jazz history at various colleges and wrote liner notes to jazz albums almost up until the time of his death.
In 1976, he was nominated for a Grammy Award for his liner notes to Joplin: The Complete Works of Scott Joplin performed by Dick Hyman.

He died on August 25, 1985, in Gilmanton, New Hampshire, from a myocardial infarction, aged 86.
He was survived by his daughter, Hilary.

== Radio and recordings ==

Blesh promoted traditional Oldtime Jazz on his radio program, a musical revival that occurred long before the 1972 film The Clou.
Blesh released recordings by Louis Armstrong, Sidney Bechet, Kid Ory, and Wild Bill Davison on the Circle label.

Shining Trumpets (A History of Jazz) honored traditional Oldtime Jazz.
