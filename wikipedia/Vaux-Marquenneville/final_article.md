# Vaux-Marquenneville

Vaux-Marquenneville (; ) is a commune in the Somme department in Hauts-de-France in northern France.

The commune is situated south of Abbeville, on the D93 road.
The name Vaux derives from "vale" or "valley" and Marquenneville comes from the domain of someone called Marca.
The inhabitants are known as Vallois in French.

== Location and administrative divisions ==

The communes and municipalities of Vaux-Marquenneville are located in and belong to the Somme department.
The municipality is part of the Baie de Somme - Picardie maritime natural regional park.
These sections belong to the Hallencourt inter-municipal community.
The Heritage Section corresponds to CIS 10 and 14.
Vaux-Marquenneville depends on the local territorial brigade of Hallencourt.

== Position relative to major cities ==

The municipality borders the area of Oisemont directly and is located to the north-northeast of it, approximately 3.5 kilometers away.
The municipality is located approximately 10 kilometers west of Hallencourt and approximately 8 miles south of Abbeville.
The municipality is located approximately 38 km west of Amiens and approximately 135 km north of Paris.

== Neighboring communes ==

Fresnes-Tilloloy is located to the west, Neuville-au-Bois to the south, Doudelainville to the northwest, Forceville-en-Vimeu to the southeast, Frucourt to the northeast, Citerne to the east, and Oisemont to the southwest.

== Road network ==

Vaux is crossed by the departmental road RD 93 from south to north between Oisemont and Frucourt, heading towards Abbeville.
The commune is also crossed by the departmental road RD 53 E from southeast to northwest, which comes from Neuville-au-Bois and heads towards Airaines.
Vaux is further crossed by the road to Fresnes-Tilloloy.

The municipality belongs to the Picardie Maritime region.

== Climate ==

In 2010, the commune had a strict oceanic climate based on a study by CNRS that relied on data covering the period from 1971 to 2000.
In 2020, Météo-France published a climate typology for metropolitan France according to which the commune is exposed to an oceanic climate and is in the Eastern Cotentin coastal region.
The Eastern Cotentin coastal region is characterized by low sunshine, high air humidity, and frequent strong winds, with relative humidity exceeding 80% in winter.
The data covers the period from 1991 to 2020.

== Land Use and Economy ==

The land use of the commune is based on the European Corine Land Cover (CLC) database.
Agricultural territories are the most important feature of the commune's land use, with 99.6% of the area being agricultural in both 1990 and 2018.
Arable land accounted for 74.5% of the area, heterogeneous agricultural zones for 22.3%, and pastures for 2.8%.
Urbanized areas accounted for 0.3% of the area.
The main activity of the village is based on livestock farming and polyculture.

In 1790 the commune came under the management of the department of the Somme and in 1793 the town of Vaux (after 1801: Vaux-Marquenneville) was integrated into the arrondissement of Abbeville and the canton of Saint-Maxent, and after 1801 the canton of Hallencourt.

The town was occupied, as were many French towns and villages, by the German army during World War II.
In 1943 work began to build a V1 missile launch ramp at "Le Mont" in Marquenneville.

A German army deserter hid in a hangar under a pile of wheat and after fruitless search by the Wehrmacht, the soldier, with the help of the French Resistance escaped from France to Spain and return to his own home.

In June 1944.
the V1 ramps were destroyed by No. 485 Squadron RNZAF.
The bombardment lasted one and a half hours (from 15h45 to 17h15).

The village was liberated by a Canadian tank regiment in 1944.

Vaux-Marquenneville received the Croix de Guerre with Bronze Star from the Secretary of State for War who said: "This courageous village, badly damaged during the Second World War, was delivered by faith and hard work".

== Etymology and early toponymy ==

The name is composed of the Latin word 'villa'.
A map of Vaux-Marquenneville from 1757 exists (Cassini map).

== Medieval lordship and Templar presence ==

Vaux was a property of the Templar Order in the Middle Ages.
Pierre de Vaux was a squire (écuyer) who held the position in 1370.
In 1490, a different Pierre de Vaux, who was likely the great-great-grandson of the first known Pierre de Vaux, transferred the lordship to Jean du Marquais.

== Seigneurs of Vaux ==

The De Riencourt family were lords of the place from 1527 until the Revolution.
François Ferdinand de Riencourt was the last lord of Vaux.

== Administrative divisions before 1789 ==

Until 1789, the lordship of Vaux belonged to the bailiwick of Bailleul and was part of the election of Amiens.

== Revolutionary changes and municipal merger ==

After 1789, the commune depended on the Kingdom of France.
The change to Vaux-Marquenneville was recorded in the Bulletin des lois of 1801 and was due to a merger with the neighboring village of Marquenneville.
The municipality was formed by the merger of two villages, one of which is called Vo.
