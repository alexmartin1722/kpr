# Toyota GD engine

The Toyota GD engine series is a diesel engine produced by Toyota which appeared in 2015. It replaced the Toyota KD engine series as a diesel engine series mainly oriented to body-on-frame vehicles. The GD engine featured Economy with Superior Thermal Efficient Combustion (ESTEC) technology. Toyota claims they have a maximum thermal efficiency of 44 percent, "top class" at the time of introduction.

The GD engine series is produced in three countries: in Japan, in Bangalore, India by Toyota Industries Engine India (TIEI), and in Chonburi, Thailand by Siam Toyota Manufacturing (STM).

== Development and introduction ==

Toyota Automatic Loom Works developed the engines after being commissioned by Toyota Motor Corporation to do so.
The announcement was made on May 11, 2015, at the Vienna International Motor Symposium.
It was announced that the 8th generation Hilux would be equipped with the engine for the first time.
The announced engine displacements are 2.8 L and 2.4 L, which are smaller than the previous 3.0 L and 2.5 L displacements.
The 1GD-FTV 2.8 and 2GD-FTV 2.4 engines were introduced to the market in 2015 for use in off-road vehicles.
Toyota Automatic Looms manufactures the Toyota GD engine.
The engines of this series will be available in 150 countries by 2020.

== Technical specifications ==

The Toyota GD engine is water-cooled and continues to use a DOHC valve train mechanism.
This is the first time Toyota has used a timing chain for camshaft drive in a diesel engine developed with its own DOHC mechanism.
The component was first adopted as a commercial product under the TNGA (Toyota New Global Architecture) platform.
TSWIN technology (Thermo Swing Wall Insulation Technology) was used in the engines, and the 1GD-FTV engine achieves a thermal efficiency of 44% due to TSWIN technology.
SiRPA stands for silica-reinforced porous anodized aluminum, and the technology involves using a material in the walls of the combustion chamber.
The material is an anodized aluminum alloy that is porous and contains silicon as an additive.
The material is known by the acronym SiRPA and has low heat capacity.
The material retains heat generated by fuel combustion inside the combustion chamber, heats up easily, cools down quickly, and has low thermal conductivity.
SiRPA was used in the production of pistons.
The reduction in heat losses was approximately 30%, and heat losses to the cooling system were reduced.

The specific fuel consumption was reduced by 15%.

== Emissions and fuel systems ==

TSWIN aims to achieve high-thermal-insulation diesel combustion.
The engine adopts a urea SCR system for the first time in Toyota's diesel engines.
The engine complies with EURO 6 emissions regulations and post-new long-term vehicle exhaust gas regulations.
The configuration adapts based on local exhaust emission regulations and diesel fuel quality.
The system can combine a DPF (Diesel Particulate Filter), a urea-free catalyst that does not require urea water, and a urea SCR (Selective Catalytic Reduction) system.
