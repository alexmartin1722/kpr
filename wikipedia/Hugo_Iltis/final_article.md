# Hugo Iltis

Hugo Iltis (April 11, 1882 – June 22, 1952) was a Czech-American biologist.

Iltis was born on April 11, 1882, in Brno, Moravia, Austria-Hungary.
His family was of Jewish descent, and the family name translates as "polecat".
He was the son of the town physician Dr. Moritz Iltis.
He became a citizen of the newly established Czechoslovak Republic in 1919.

He attended the lower grades and the German-language gymnasium in Brno and then went on to study biology and botany at the University of Zurich, Switzerland, from 1900 to 1903 as an assistant to Arnold Dodel-Port and later Alfred Ernst (the successor of Dodel-Port in 1902).
He studied botany at the University of Prague under Hans Molisch from 1903 to 1905 where he received his Ph.D. in 1905.

In 1906, he served as secretary for Naturforschender Verein in Brno, which was the society through which Gregor Mendel published his papers.
In 1910, he raised funds for the Mendel Memorial in Brno, and served as secretary for the International Committee for the Mendel Memorial.
He gave the commemorative speech at the unveiling of the memorial.
He was also the secretary for the Mendel Centenary in 1922, a celebration of the hundredth anniversary of Mendel's birth.

He taught biology (with the civil service title "professor") at the German-language gymnasium in Brno from 1905 to 1938, and he also held an appointment as a Privatdozent for botany and genetics at the Deutsche Technische Hochschule (German Polytechnical Institute) in Brno from 1911 to 1938.
He was the founder and director of the Masaryk People's University (Masaryk Volkshochschule) in Brno, an adult education evening school, from 1921 to 1938.
This Volkshochschule was the largest institution for adult education in Czechoslovakia with an enrollment above 2000.
He founded the Mendel Museum in Brno in 1932 and curated it to 1937.
The museum contained many valuable manuscripts and relics of the life and work of Mendel.

A socialist, Iltis spent much of his time from 1930 to 1938 combating the racist biology (eugenics) of the Nazis.
He was a key organizer and host of the 1932 Brno congress of the World League for Sexual Reform.

With the help of Franz Boas and Albert Einstein and the Emergency Committee in Aid of Displaced Foreign Scholars, the Iltis family received a United States visa in the fall of 1938.
Hugo left Czechoslovakia in December 1938 on the last plane out and traveled to England to lecture.
His wife Anni Iltis and their two boys left behind their life in Brno and joined him in January 1939 in France following a harrowing train ride through Germany and France.
They sailed for the U.S. from the port Cherbourg on the passenger ship RMS Aquitania.

Initially, he taught for 5 weeks at the International School, run by Peter Ray Ogden.
Following a chance meeting with Dean Edward Alvey in the grocery store, he was offered a professorship in biology at Mary Washington College in Fredericksburg, Virginia, where he taught for approximately 12 years.
He was also the founder and curator of the Mendel Museum of Genetics, now housed at the University of Illinois Archives.
He died at Fredericksburg on June 22, 1952.
His younger son was the plant morphologist and taxonomist Hugh Iltis.
His elder son was the entomologist Fred Iltis.

Later in life, to his great surprise, he learned from his wife's sister Lisi Liebscher that his wife Anni Iltis was a distant cousin of Gregor Mendel.

He was a fellow in the A.A.A.S., a member of the Genetics Society of America, the American Genetics Association, the Virginia Academy of Science, and the American Association of University Professors.

He was listed in: Köpfe Europas, Wer Ist's?, Who Knows What?, and American Men of Science.

Archives of his work are stored in the University of Wisconsin Library Special Collections.

== Early life and education ==

Hugo Iltis was an Austrian botanist and phycologist of Jewish-German descent.
Brno was part of the Margraviate of Moravia at the time of his birth.
The purpose of the move was to study natural sciences.
The studies were completed at the Karl-Ferdinand University of Prague, where he obtained a doctorate and conducted important studies at the botanical institute in the capital.

== Scientific research and experiments ==

Hugo Iltis was a naturalist and a refugee in the United States.
He conducted experiments at the Prague Botanical Institute that focused on root length growth of aquatic plants.
He found that root growth was accelerated in darkness compared to daylight, with the acceleration factor ranging from 1.5 to 7.5 times.
Iltis did not conduct experimental research himself.

== Discovery and biography of Gregor Mendel ==

Hugo Iltis is mentioned on the Mendel Museum website and was the first and most significant biographer of Gregor Mendel for decades.
Iltis came from an established Jewish family and met Gregor Mendel during his student days.
Gregor Mendel's person subsequently influenced Hugo's life, and Mendel's personality decisively influenced Iltis's life.
In 1899, Hugo read a natural history journal from Brno that contained an old research article by Gregor Mendel, which Hugo discovered while reading the journal.
The biography was considered a standard work on Mendel for a long time.
Iltis expressed an opinion about Mendel in his work, claiming that Mendel was a rationalist and that Mendel became a priest only to be able to research without disturbance.
The biography described Mendel as a rationalist.

== Mendel Museum and collection ==

Iltis purchased significant quantities of original Mendel documents in his hometown for the museum.
He moved his collection from Brno into the museum.
The materials are known as the Iltis Mendeliana Collection.
Iltis escaped with his wife, two sons, and the inventory of the Mendel Museum.
Fred Iltis lived from 1923 to 2008.
He donated a large quantity of original documents to the Mendelian Museum.
Iltis founded the Iltis-Mendel Museum in Fredericksburg.
The collection is titled the Iltis Mendelania Collection.
The collection is located in Urbana.

== Teaching and adult education ==

Anne Liebscher was one of Hugo Iltis's students and later became his wife.
Iltis published the quarterly journal *Licht ins Volk* from 1927 to 1938 within the framework of the Volkshochschule.
He was primarily responsible for acquiring a building for the Volkshochschule and raised most of the financial funds for it.
Adult education was not widely spread at that time.
Iltis's primary profession was as a high school teacher.
The supporting ministry was responsible for school affairs and popular culture.
Hugo Iltis is associated with the Hunt Institute for Botanical Documentation.

== Cultural and political activities ==

Iltis was a member of the board of the German Society for Science and Art, which advocated for the preservation of German culture in Brno.
He was also a member of the German Society of Sciences and Arts (DGWK) [cite: 4].
Iltis defended the German roots of the region and opposed the growing Czech influence in the region [cite: 4].
He published a festschrift for the celebration that occurred from September 22 to 24, 1922 [cite: 4].

== Scientific debates and Lamarckism ==

Iltis was closely friends with Paul Kammerer, who conducted experiments on the midwife toad to attempt to prove the inheritance of acquired traits in Lamarck's sense and argued against Darwinism based on these experiments.
Some of Iltis's works attempted to rehabilitate Lamarckism against Darwinism, an attempt limited to at least some species including the midwife toad.
From the mid-1920s onwards, Iltis aligned with other anthropologists and debated Fritz Lenz, Arthur de Gobineau, Hans F.
K.
Günther, and Houston Stewart Chamberlain.
One of the accepted eugenic parameters was marriage prohibitions, and another was social hygiene.

== Emigration and later life ==

Hugo Iltis later became a Czechoslovakian botanist.
Brno was then part of Czechoslovakia.
This opportunity allowed Iltis to escape to safety shortly before Germany invaded Czechoslovakia.
The New York Committee for Aid to Foreign Refugees helped Iltis emigrate.
Iltis worked for the New York Committee for Aid to Foreign Refugees until 1944.

His son, Hugh Hellmut Iltis, is an emeritus professor of Botany at the University of Wisconsin-Madison who is well known for his discoveries in corn domestication.

== Conflict with racial theorists ==

Fritz Lenz, an antisemitic neodarwinist, heavily attacked Iltis in 1929 due to his relationship with Kammerer and his Jewish heritage.
Iltis began dealing with this topic in the mid-1920s, and the work 'Volkstümliche Rassenkunde' became widely distributed.
Racist fallacies, previously held by laypeople such as Arthur de Gobineau and Houston Stewart Chamberlain, entered German science after the end of the war, with Hans F.
K.
Günther as the main person who spread these theories within German science.
Iltis described Günther's teachings as unscientific and politically dangerous.
Iltis wanted to counter these teachings with a neutral and critical presentation of the state of science.

== Views on race and racism ==

Iltis made a preliminary remark regarding his own description of human races, stating that almost every author defines racial boundaries differently and that sharp boundaries between races do not exist.
He considered a typological subdivision to be sensible and distinguished a European form group, a Mongoloid form group, a Negroid form group, an Australoid form group, and dwarf races.
Iltis further subdivided the European form group by describing a Nordic type, a Mediterranean type, an Alpine type, a Dinaric type, and an Eastern race.
Iltis considered the postulate of a Jewish race to be outdated based on the work of Felix von Luschan, noting that Jews are a mixture of several European races.
Iltis rejected the theory that racial mixing is harmful, a theory that originated with Gobineau.

Hugo Iltis was one of the first authors to coin the term 'racism' and defined it in a relatively broad manner that included colonialism and the imperialistic division and exploitation of the world.
His main work on racism was a book in which he argued for defining the concept of race purely in physical terms and attributed the ability to develop high cultures to all major races.
Iltis attributed the cultural superiority of Europeans in modern times to the 'horrifying history' of colonialism and imperialism, which, according to his portrayal, began with the conquest of America.
He simultaneously adhered to a 'non-evaluative' race science.
