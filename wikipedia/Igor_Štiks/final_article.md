# Igor Štiks

Igor Štiks (born 17 September 1977 in Sarajevo) is a novelist and scholar. His novels The Judgment of Richard Richter and A Castle in Romagna have earned him multiple awards; the former has been translated into 15 languages.

Igor Štiks was born in Sarajevo, Bosnia-Herzegovina, in 1977, when Sarajevo was part of Yugoslavia.
He became interested in literature and reading at the age of 13 or 14, an interest influenced by the outbreak of war which made the world of adults completely incomprehensible to him.
In 1992, Štiks left Sarajevo and moved to Zagreb, where he lived.
He graduated in 2001 from the Faculty of Philosophy in Zagreb with a degree in philosophy and comparative literature.
He earned his PhD at the Institut d'Études Politiques de Paris and Northwestern University, where he studied and worked as an assistant.
He obtained a doctorate in 2009 and became a research fellow at the University of Edinburgh in 2009.
He later worked and taught at the University of Edinburgh and the Faculty of Media and Communications in Belgrade.
He has also lived in Paris, Chicago, Edinburgh, and Graz.
His first novel, A Castle in Romagna (Dvorac u Romagni), won the Slavić prize for best first novel in Croatia and was nominated for the IMPAC International Dublin Literary Award for 2006.
His second novel The Judgment of Richard Richter, originally published as Elijah's Chair (Elijahova stolica), won the Gjalski and Kiklop Awards for the best novel in Croatia in 2006 and has been translated into fifteen languages including German, Spanish, French, Dutch, Italian, Polish, Czech, Hungarian, Slovenian, Bulgarian, Macedonian, Finnish, Ukrainian and Arabic.
In 2017 he published his third novel originally titled Rezalište.
In his scholarly work, Štiks investigated the topic of citizenship and nationalism in Balkans.
Besides many scholarly articles and edited volumes, Štiks published a monograph, Nations and Citizens in Yugoslavia and the Post-Yugoslav States: One Hundred Years of Citizenship (2015).
Along with Jo Shaw, he edited the collections Citizenship after Yugoslavia (2013) and Citizenship Rights (2013), and, with Srećko Horvat, Welcome to the Desert of Post-Socialism (2015).
Štiks was honored with the prestigious French distinction Chevalier des arts et des lettres for his literary and intellectual achievements.
In addition to winning the Grand Prix of the 2011 Belgrade International Theater Festival for his stage adaptation of Elijah's Chair, Štiks wrote two more plays, Flour in the Veins and Zrenjanin.
All three plays were put on stage by one of the leading post-Yugoslav theater directors Boris Liješević.
In 2017, Štiks signed the Declaration on the Common Language of the Croats, Serbs, Bosniaks and Montenegrins.

Štiks is married to Dr. Jelena Vasiljevic, who is a research associate at the Institute for Philosophy and Social Theory at University of Belgrade. They live in Belgrade and have a son.

== A Castle in Romagna ==

The novel *Dvorac u Romagni* was published in 2000 and connects the world of the Italian Renaissance with the Informbiro period.
The setting of the novel is the island of Rab, and it features alternating narratives.
One narrative follows an Italian writer from the 16th century, while the other follows a Croatian priest from the 20th century.
The novel *Dvorac u Romagni* was translated into German, Spanish, and Turkish.

== The Judgment of Richard Richter ==

The story features a 50-year-old writer who searches for his real father and identity in the early 1990s, a search that leads him to Sarajevo where a war begins at that time.
A German translation was published by Ullstein Verlag, a Spanish translation by Destino, a French translation by Galaade, a Dutch translation by De Bezige Bij, an Italian translation as Mentre Alma dorme, a Polish translation by W.A.B., a Czech translation by Mlada fronta, a Hungarian translation by Magveto, a Slovenian translation by Beletrina, a Bulgarian translation by Panorama, a Macedonian translation by Ili-Ili, a Finnish translation by Mansarda, and a Ukrainian translation by Knigi XXI.
The English edition was added to the Kindle store in August 2017 and became the best-selling e-book in the historical fiction category within a few weeks.

== The Cuts ==

The plot of Štiks's third novel, *Rezalište*, takes place during a dinner in a small Balkan town where a nameless family scattered around the world gathered after not being together for 25 years.
