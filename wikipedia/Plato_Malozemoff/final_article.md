# Plato Malozemoff

Plato Malozemoff (born Platon Alexandovich Malozyomov, ; 1909–1997) was a Russian-American engineer, manager, and businessman.

Malozemoff was born in Saint Petersburg, Russia, on August 28, 1909, and immigrated to the San Francisco Bay Area as a child.
Malozemoff was raised in Oakland, California.
He attended the University of California, Berkeley as an undergraduate and did his graduate studies at the Montana School of Mines, where he studied under metallurgist Antoine Marc Gaudin.

== Family background and father's career ==

Plato Malozemoff's father was Alexander Platonovich Malozemov, a mining engineer who was in exile in Barguzin at that time.
His mother was Elizaveta Andreevna, who traveled from Siberia to St.
Petersburg.
The father managed a British-owned gold mine on the Obi River and later managed all mines of Lena Goldfields in Siberia.

== Emigration to the United States ==

After the father received information that he might be arrested in his homeland, he went to the United States in 1919 to purchase equipment and did not return from the trip.
The family fled illegally, crossed the Soviet-Mongolian border, and met Malozemov Sr. in China.
They traveled to San Francisco in 1920 and purchased a house in Oakland.

== Education ==

Platon Malozemov graduated from the University of California, Berkeley in 1931 and from the Montana School of Mines in 1932.

== Brother and his work ==

Plato Malozemoff's brother was Andrey Alexandrovich Malozemov, an American historian born in 1910 who died in 1952.
Andrey Alexandrovich Malozemov authored the monograph 'Russian Far Eastern policy 1881-1904: with special emphasis on the causes of the Russo-Japanese War', a work titled 'Russian Far Eastern policy, 1881-1904' that focuses on the period from 1881 to 1904 and gives special emphasis on the causes of the Russo-Japanese War.
The work was originally published in Berkeley in 1958.

Unable to secure an engineering position after earning his master's degree, Malozemoff held jobs provided by the Works Progress Administration.

In 1945, Malozemoff took an entry-level engineering position with the Newmont Corporation in Colorado.
Quickly rising up the corporate ranks, Malozemoff became president of the company in 1954.
At the time it was valued at $147 million.
He expanded it into a $2.3 billion firm via acquisitions and international expansion by the time he left in 1986.
He was inducted into the National Mining Hall of Fame in 1994.

The UC Berkeley College of Engineering has since established a named professorship in honor of Malozemoff, the Plato Malozemoff Professor of Civil and Environmental Engineering.

== Early career and international projects ==

Platon Malozemov worked as a metallurgist for Pan American Engineering Company for 5 years.
His father persuaded him to work on a joint geological survey project in Argentina to search for tungsten.
Alexander Malozemov, Platon Malozemov, and Engineer Arthur Corri traveled to Argentina in 1938.
The group traveled to Cordoba, Argentina.
Brown Brothers Harriman and Pan American Engineering Company financed the project.
Platon Malozemov became the construction director of a factory in Chilesito in 1941.
Mining operations at the mine were halted due to the war.

== Costa Rica venture ==

Abangares is located on the coast of Costa Rica.
He invested his own money in a new venture together with his father.
They leased a gold mine in Abangares.
The project failed.
Platon Malozemov returned from Costa Rica in 1943.

== Government service ==

Platon Malozemov served as a market expert for non-ferrous metals and worked in a government price regulation office.

== Leadership at Newmont ==

Malozemov later became the head of the board of directors and managed the regional company.
The regional company was valued at 147 million dollars in the mid-1950s.
Under Platon Malozemov's management, the regional company began to conquer the market.

== Acquisitions and partnerships ==

Malozemov cooperated with many mining enterprises and acquired shares in these mining enterprises.
Partner companies included Palabora Mining Company, Atlas Consolidated Mining Development in the Philippines, Evraz Highveld Steel and Vanadium Limited in South Africa, Carlin Gold Mining Company in Canada, and Foote Mineral Company in the United States.

== Recognition ==

He was included among the business leaders of America.

== Board service and leadership ==

He served on the board of trustees of the American Museum of Natural History from 1975 to 1990.
Platon Malozemov served as chairman of the board of directors of the Tolstoy Foundation.
