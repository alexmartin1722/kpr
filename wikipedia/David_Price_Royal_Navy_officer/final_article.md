# David Price Royal Navy officer

Rear Admiral David Powell Price (1790 – 31 August 1854) was a Royal Navy officer of the 19th century, who served as Commander-in-Chief, Pacific from 1853. He was also known for being a primary commander during the Siege of Petropavlovsk within the Crimean War before allegedly committing suicide before the bombardments of the fort.

== Early life ==

David Powell Price was an esquire born in Killikum, Wales, into the family of a landowner named Rice Price.

Joining the navy on in 1801, he fought at Copenhagen that year and then in the West Indies on when the Peace of Amiens broke down in 1803.
He then served in in 1805 and 1806 under its captain Sir Samuel Hood, fighting in the battle off Rochefort on 25 September 1806 and in its capture of the 50-gun Russian battleship Sevolod on 26 August 1808.
He was then appointed acting lieutenant on Ardent in April 1809 and during summer 1809 was captured and released by the Danes twice during the Gunboat War.
He was confirmed as lieutenant in September 1809, continuing in Ardent right up to February 1811.
He was then transferred to the brig off France's northern coast, managing to bring out an armed brig and three store ships on 19 August 1811 when Hawk drove a convoy and its escort on shore near Barfleur, though in a later attempt to cut two more brigs out of Barfleur harbour he was severely wounded and unable to serve again for almost a year.

His next appointment was to the 74-gun off Cherbourg, then from September 1812 on off Toulon (under Henry Bourchier, who had been his captain on Hawk).
In December 1812 he gained his first command, the bomb vessel , with which he sailed for North America in summer 1814 and fought during the War of 1812 on the Potomac, at Baltimore and near New Orleans.
He was wounded on 24 December 1814 near New Orleans and saw action the following month, bombarding a fort on the Mississippi as a diversion.
At the cessation of hostilities he returned to England, where he was promoted to post rank on 13 June 1815.
His next command was (1834–38) in the Mediterranean (receiving the Order of the Redeemer for his services to the Greek government).
From 1838 to 1844 he retired to Brecknockshire, serving as a Justice of the Peace there.

In 1844 he married Elizabeth Taylor, daughter of John Taylor and niece of Admiral William Taylor, and in 1846 was promoted to superintendent of Sheerness Dockyard, remaining there until promotion to rear admiral on 6 November 1850 and then to the post of the Royal Navy's Commander-in-Chief, Pacific, in August 1853.
Arriving there just before the declaration of the Crimean War, he proved tactful, courteous but indecisive and difficult in his dealings with working with his French colleague Auguste Febvrier Despointes.
The French and British fleets slowly advanced across the Pacific, spending a long time at the Marquesas Islands and Honolulu (where the English and French squadrons met in July 1854 and worked to reduce American influence).
On 25 July 1854 the combined force sailed to meet two Russian frigates reported to be in the area, finding them dismantled at Petropavlovsk.
An attack on them was planned for 31 August 1854 but on that morning Admiral Price was killed by a discharge of his own pistol.
Command transferred to Sir Frederick Nicolson, who postponed the attack until 4 September, on which day the Russians decisively beat it back.

N.N.Muravyov wrote at his letter to the admiral-general of the Russian fleet, Grand Duke Konstantin Nikolaevich about the battle in Petropavlovsk: ... the English Admiral Price was killed in front of the Petropavlovsk port on his frigate and was buried in the Tarino Bay... == Early service and promotion ==

He joined the crew as a boy after several years of schooling and joined the service on January 1, 1801.
The captain of the 'Ardent' was Thomas Berty.
He was promoted to midshipman at the age of 13.
The crew participated in combat operations under Copenhagen subsequently.

== Service aboard Centaur ==

The ship was named Centaur and was commanded by Samuel Hood.
The crew participated in combat operations near La Rochelle fortress.

== Service aboard Ardent ==

David Price was assigned to the ship 'Ardent' again, which was commanded by Robert Honiman.
He led the capture of a Danish felucca at Rogervik on Romsø Island.
He replaced the wounded Lieutenant James Shi, who was wounded in the arm.
A battle occurred after which he was captured while on a captured enemy ship.
The Danes exchanged him for his countrymen who had been captured by the British.

== Operations aboard Hawk ==

The ship 'Hawk' encountered a large French convoy and ran aground on rocks.
Coastal batteries fired heavily during the engagement.
Three boarding parties participated in the battle, one of which was commanded by David Price.
The boarding parties used boats to capture the vessels, including a 16-gun brig named 'Neron' and three transports.
The capture of a French schooner was made from a launch under heavy fire from coastal batteries.
The event took place on October 21, during which he nearly fell into captivity.
He was stabbed with a bayonet during a hand-to-hand combat three months after the previous event.

== War of 1812 and the Vulcan ==

Pryce returned to service in September 1812 and was appointed to command the floating battery 'Vulcan'.
The purpose of leading the 'Vulcan' to North America was to join Alexander Cochrane's fleet, where Pryce served under the command of Rear-Admiral Plunket Malcolm.
A battle with Americans took place during which Pryce was shot in the thigh, prompting Admiral Malcolm to write a letter regarding the injury.
The ship 'Vulcan' covered the retreat of British troops, shelled Fort St.
Philip, and then participated in the siege of Boyer's Fort.
Boyer's Fort was located in Mobile Bay, which is now known as Fort Morgan.
The ship 'Vulcan' returned to Portsmouth on May 31, 1815, and Pryce received half pay during this period.

== Later commands and honors ==

Captain Price was appointed commander of the 50-gun HMS Portland, which was part of the Mediterranean Squadron, on May 1, 1834.
In 1837, King Otto I of Greece awarded Price the Order of Saviour for personal bravery and skilled command.
King Otto I ordered a portrait of Price to be painted.
Captain Price received a congratulatory letter from Sir Edmund Lyons and a ceremonial sword from the Viceroy of Egypt, Muhammad Ali.

== Crimean War and death ==

David Price commanded the 98-gun ship Ocean starting November 10, 1846, and the 80-gun ship Wellington starting March 1, 1848.
A naval operation tactic was selected that involved the sequential capture of Russian ports.
The fleet, consisting of three British warships and four French warships, arrived at the Hawaiian Islands on July 17.
This Anglo-French squadron of eight ships reached Petropavlovsk-Kamchatsky on August 29.
Preparations began on August 30 after the city assault plan was agreed upon, but they were not completed in time due to news of the death of the commander-in-chief David Price.
His 2,500 marines were defeated against 900 residents of Petropavlovsk-Kamchatsky.
The person was buried on September 2, 1854.
Thomas Berty was a veteran of the American War of Independence and the French Revolutionary Wars who later sailed on small ships in the West Indies.
