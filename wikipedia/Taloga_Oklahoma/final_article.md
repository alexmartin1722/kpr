# Taloga Oklahoma

Taloga is a town in Dewey County, Oklahoma, United States. The population was 288 at the time of the 2020 census.

It is the county seat of Dewey County. The town lies near the southern bank of a bend of the Canadian River, along U.S. Route 183, approximately thirty-seven miles north of Clinton. According to the Encyclopedia of Oklahoma History and Culture, the name is of Indian origin and means either "beautiful valley" or "rocking water".

The Taloga Wind project outside of town, dedicated in March 2012, can generate up to 130 megawatts of power, which is enough to meet the needs of about 35,000 homes. All electricity produced by the project is provided to Oklahoma Gas and Electric Company under a 20-year power purchase agreement.

== History ==

The settlement was founded in April 1892 after the Cheyenne-Arapaho Reservation was opened to non-Indian settlers. [cite: 1,2,3,4]
Almost all members of the Dalton Gang registered under false names when the settlement was founded to receive land allotments.

== Economy ==

Oil reserves were developed in the area, with development beginning in the 1920s.
The production of oil and gas contributed to the prosperity of the place, with this contribution beginning in the 1950s.
Agriculture is an important economic sector in the region.

== Energy ==
