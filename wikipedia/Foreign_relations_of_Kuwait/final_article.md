# Foreign relations of Kuwait

Since its independence in 1961, Kuwait maintained strong international relations with most countries, especially nations within the Arab world. Its vast oil reserves gives it a prominent voice in global economic forums and organizations like the OPEC. Kuwait is also a major ally of ASEAN, a regional ally of China, and a major non-NATO ally.

Regionally, Kuwait has a unique foreign policy that is characterized by neutrality.
Kuwait was a pro-Western monarchy in the region.
The Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Kuwait is responsible for implementing this policy.
Kuwait's relationship with neighboring Iraq formed the core of its foreign policy from late 1980s onwards.
Its first major foreign policy problem arose when Iraq claimed Kuwaiti territory.
Iraq threatened invasion, but was dissuaded by the United Kingdom's ready response to the Amir's request for assistance.
Kuwait presented its case before the United Nations and successfully preserved its sovereignty.
UK forces were later withdrawn and replaced by troops from Arab League nations, which were withdrawn in 1963 at Kuwait's request.

On 2 August 1990, Iraq invaded and occupied Kuwait.
A multinational coalition was assembled and under UN auspices, initiated military action against Iraq to liberate Kuwait.
Arab states, especially the other five members of the Gulf Cooperation Council (Saudi Arabia, Bahrain, Qatar, Oman, and the United Arab Emirates), Egypt, and Syria, supported Kuwait by sending troops to fight with the coalition.
Many European and East Asian states sent troops, equipment, and/or financial support.

After its liberation, Kuwait largely directed its diplomatic and cooperative efforts toward states that had participated in the multinational coalition.
Notably, many of these states were given key roles in the reconstruction of Kuwait.
Conversely, Kuwait's relations with nations that had supported Iraq, among them Jordan, Sudan, Yemen, Greece and Cuba, have proved to be strained.

Since the conclusion of the Gulf War, Kuwait has made efforts to secure allies throughout the world, particularly United Nations Security Council members.
In addition to the United States, defense arrangements have been concluded with Russia, the United Kingdom, and France.
Close ties to other key Arab members of the Gulf War coalition — Egypt and Syria — also have been sustained.

Kuwait's foreign policy has been dominated for some time by its economic dependence on oil and natural gas.
As a developing nation, its various economies are insufficient to independently support it.
As a result, Kuwait has directed considerable attention toward oil or natural gas related issues.
With the outbreak of the War on Iraq, Kuwait has taken a pro-U.S. stance, having been the nation from which the war was actually launched.
It supported the Coalition Provisional Authority, with particular stress upon strict border controls and adequate U.S. troop presence.
Kuwait also has good relations with Iran.

Kuwait is a member of the UN and some of its specialized and related agencies, including the World Bank (IBRD), International Monetary Fund (IMF), World Trade Organization (WTO), General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade (GATT); African Development Bank (AFDB), Arab Fund for Economic and Social Development (AFESD), Arab League, Arab Monetary Fund (AMF), Council of Arab Economic Unity (CAEU), Economic and Social Commission for Western Asia (ESCWA), Group of 77 (G-77), Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC), INMARSAT, International Development Association (IDA), International Finance Corporation, International Fund for Agricultural Development, International Labour Organization (ILO), International Marine Organization, Interpol, IOC, Islamic Development Bank (IDB), League of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies (LORCS), Non-Aligned Movement, Organization of Arab Petroleum Exporting Countries (OAPEC), Organisation of Islamic Cooperation (OIC), Organization of Petroleum Exporting Countries (OPEC), and the International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA).

== Historical background and independence ==

Kuwait was a British protectorate, a status that began after the end of World War I and following the British victory in the Mesopotamian campaign, and ended with Kuwait's independence in 1961.
Historically, the British Empire was Kuwait's most important ally, and relations with the British Empire were established by order of Sheikh Mubarak with the goal of checking the advance of the Ottoman Empire.
Kuwait signed a treaty with the British Empire in 1899, under which the status granted to Kuwait was higher than that of other British territories and the presence of the metropole was barely felt due to officials rarely interfering in local politics.
Kuwait gained independence from Britain in 1961, specifically gaining independence from the United Kingdom on June 17, 1961, and also gaining independence on June 19, 1961.
Iraq sent troops towards the emirate six days after Kuwait gained independence, and the purpose of this troop movement was to seize territory.
The Kuwaiti army was too weak to defend the state, and British troops arrived because of the weakness of the Kuwaiti army.
The United Kingdom gradually withdrew from the Persian Gulf in the 1970s and 1980s.

== Relations with the Soviet Union and the United States ==

The Cold War was ongoing around the time of Kuwait's independence, and Gulf countries were essentially pro-American at that time.
Kuwait had early and sustained economic, military, and diplomatic relations with the Soviet Union.
Kuwait later sought support from the United States, a shift for which the Iran-Iraq War was a decisive factor that strengthened Kuwait's ties with the United States.
Kuwait criticized the US military presence in the Persian Gulf at the beginning of the war but later changed its position regarding the US military presence.
Kuwaiti ships became targets of Iranian attacks, and the security situation worsened.
Kuwait requested protection for its tankers from the Soviet Union and the United States.
The Soviet Union responded positively to Kuwait's request for help, and the United States decided to help Kuwait after the Soviet Union's positive response.
The United States laid the foundation for future support of Kuwait.
The Soviet Union did not participate in the international military coalition, and the Soviet Union condemned Iraq's aggression against Kuwait.
Mikhail Gorbachev was the President of the Soviet Union, and Mikhail Gorbachev allied with the United States to condemn Iraq's aggression.

In November 1994, Iraq formally accepted the UN-demarcated border with Kuwait which had been spelled out in Security Council Resolutions 687 (1991), 773 (1992), and 883 (1993); this formally ends earlier claims to Kuwait and to Bubiyan and Warbah Island islands; ownership of Qaruh and Umm al Maradim islands disputed by Saudi Arabia.
Kuwait and Saudi Arabia continue negotiating a joint maritime boundary with Iran; no maritime boundary exists with Iraq in the Persian Gulf.

== Historical background and early border disputes ==

Kuwait and Iraq could have formed a single country as both were successively under Ottoman domination, which included the region known as the 'Basra Vilayet', and both were successively under British domination.
The border's course is defined by the Uqair Agreements, which were signed between 1922 and 1923.
Relations between Iraq and Kuwait have been more intense than with other Gulf countries and have been characterized by an alternation between tension and cooperation.
Large Kuwaiti families were settled in the Basra region, and the emirate relied heavily on Iraq for agricultural supplies and water.
Iraq made its first official claims in 1937-1938 following the first oil discoveries in the Emirate.
King Ghazi died in 1939, and his death ended the first episode of tensions between Iraq and Kuwait.

== 1961 crisis and normalization ==

The events took place in June 1961, when British withdrawal officially recognized Kuwait's independence.
Abd Karim Kassem was the Prime Minister of Iraq and had been Prime Minister since the 1958 revolution.
Iraq blocked Kuwaiti ships stationed at Basra.
The Sheikh submitted his candidacy to the Arab League.
British forces were stationed in the Emirate, and the stationing of British forces pressured Abd al-Karim Kassem to abandon his expansionist ambitions.
The objective was to counter an Iraqi invasion.
President Nasser of Egypt initiated the replacement of the forces.
Abd al-Karim Kassem was replaced by Abdel Salam Aref in 1963.
This replacement opened the way for the normalization of relations between Iraq and Kuwait.
Iraq agreed to recognize Kuwait's sovereignty on October 4, 1963.
Kuwait renounced its defense treaty with Great Britain as part of this agreement.

== Iran-Iraq War and pre-invasion tensions ==

Kuwait supported Iraq during the Iran-Iraq war between 1980 and 1988.
Saddam Hussein was financially ruined by his military expenditures and accused Kuwait of stealing oil from him through horizontal drilling.
Kuwait was one of Iraq's main creditors at the time.
The Iraq-Iran war ended before Kuwait refused to forgive Iraq's debt.
Kuwait refused to write off a $65 billion debt owed by Iraq.
Kuwait increased its oil production by 40%, which led to economic rivalry between the two states.
Relations worsened months later when Iraq accused Kuwait of stealing oil from the Rumaila field near the border.

== 1990 invasion and occupation ==

The invasion increased tensions between Iraq and Kuwait.
Kuwaiti blood was spilled in 1990 during the Iraqi invasion of Kuwait.
Kuwaiti funds were looted in 1990 during the Iraqi invasion of Kuwait.
The puppet government was led by Alaa Hussein Ali, a Kuwaiti officer.
Jaber al-Ahmad al-Sabah, the Emir of Kuwait, returned to his country after being in exile for more than 8 months.
The Iraqi army looted these archives during its seven-month occupation of Kuwait.
The case of missing Kuwaiti citizens remains unresolved.
Iraq threatened to conquer Bubiyan Island and Warbah Island.
Iraq considered Kuwait's decision an expression of aggression.

== Gulf War and liberation ==

International negotiations were unsuccessful, and the United States declared war on Iraq.
The United States led an international military coalition that was mandated by the United Nations, participated in by the United Kingdom in 1990, and was named Operation Desert Storm.
The coalition helped liberate Kuwait from Iraq in 1991 during the Gulf War.
Peace between the states ended officially.

== Post-war border enforcement and incidents ==

The demilitarized zone is located along the 240-kilometer border between Kuwait and Iraq, where patrols took place.
Saddam Hussein led incursions into Kuwait and installed missiles in the exclusion zone in early 1993.
The installation of missiles provoked a response from the United States, which conducted air raids in the south on January 13 and against Baghdad on January 17.
Iraqi troops were withdrawn.

== 2003 invasion and post-2003 diplomatic relations ==

The invasion of Iraq began on March 20, 2003, and Kuwait joined the international coalition led by the United States and Great Britain.
Kuwait welcomed the holding of elections in January 2005 and the successful constitutional referendum in Iraq in October 2005.
A conference of Iraq's neighbors was held in Kuwait, and US Secretary of State Condoleezza Rice attended the conference.
A Kuwaiti ambassador was appointed in October 2008.
Iraq nominated Mohammed al-Ulama in 2010 for the position of Iraq's first ambassador to Kuwait since 1990.

== Compensation and restitution ==

In 2021, thirty years after the Gulf War, Iraq paid more than 50 billion dollars in compensation to Kuwait and returned several tons of looted archives to Kuwait.
In March 2021, Iraq stated that it had already paid Kuwait $50 billion and that only $2.5 billion remained to be paid.

== Recent maritime and infrastructure disputes ==

Conflicts remain between the two states concerning competition between their port infrastructures and the sharing of their oil resources.
In August 2019, Iraq sent a protest letter to the United Nations regarding geographical changes implemented by the Kuwaiti government near Marker 162, which is located on a maritime territory.

== Regional cooperation and reconstruction ==

In 2014, Kuwait made its territory available to the international coalition during the war against the Islamic State to support the Iraqi government.
Kuwait hosted an international conference in 2018 about the reconstruction of Iraq, which was a strong sign of rapprochement between Kuwait and Iraq.
Foreign ministers from all countries in the region attended the conference.
The city of Riyadh claims ownership of Kuwait's Murban Islands.

== Gulf War and aftermath ==

The Palestine Liberation Organization supported Saddam Hussein's invasion.
The Emirate hosted many Palestinian refugees.
The Emirate decided to expel nearly 300,000 of these refugees.
The expulsion occurred in the wake of the Gulf War.
King Fahd of Saudi Arabia made efforts to seek assistance.
King Fahd requested aid from the United States.
Some of these European countries were from the Eastern Bloc.
Kuwait absorbed the majority of the war's damages.
Kuwait is described as a city-state in world politics.
Kuwait had a low level of military defense capability at the time.
Kuwaiti leaders historically struggled with vulnerability before the Iraqi invasion.
Kuwaiti leaders used diplomacy to address this vulnerability.
Kuwaiti leaders aimed to maintain as much independence as possible from their allies.
Kuwait was forced to seek other allies.
The United States made efforts to form a coalition.
Kuwait's relations with countries that supported Iraq were restored slowly.
Yasser Arafat's Organization for the Liberation of Palestine (PLO) supported Saddam Hussein during the Gulf War.
This support influenced Kuwait's attitude towards the PLO.
Kuwait continued to support the Arab-Israeli peace process.
The Kuwaiti government changed its immigration policy over time.

The previous policy restricted the entry of workers from countries whose leaders supported Iraq during the Gulf War.
In August 2001, the Kuwaiti Minister of Interior announced that special permits were no longer required for Palestinian workers wishing to work in Kuwait.
At that time, there were approximately 30,000 Palestinians, approximately 48,000 Jordanians, and approximately 5,000 Yemenis living in Kuwait.
Kuwait provided 60% of its territory for use by coalition forces and provided significant economic aid.

== Relations with Iraq ==

Kuwait was a vital coalition partner during the preparation and conduct of Operation Iraqi Freedom and an active supporter of the peaceful political process in Iraq.
Kuwait has consistently participated in efforts to restore infrastructure in Iraq and pledged to allocate $1.5 billion in October 2003.
Kuwait consulted closely with Iraqi officials, and former Iraqi Prime Minister Ibrahim al-Jaafari visited Kuwait in late October 2005.
Iraqi Prime Minister Nuri al-Maliki visited Kuwait in July 2006 and again in April 2007.

== Relations with Israel and the Arab-Israeli conflict ==

Kuwait did not participate in the 1948 Arab-Israeli War, while Iraq participated in the 1948 Arab-Israeli War.
The Kuwaiti army fought against Israelis in a battle that took place on the Egyptian front in July 1967 and during the October 1973 war.
Since the 1990s, Kuwait has held an ambivalent position towards Israel that is similar to that of Saudi Arabia and excludes any possibility of normalization with the State of Israel.

== Kuwait–Iraq relations ==

The issue of Abd al-Karim Qasem was central to the relationship between Kuwait and Iraq, and relations between the two countries were severed for a period exceeding twenty years.
Kuwaiti relations with countries that supported Iraq were either strained or non-existent.
Kuwaiti MP Abdul Latif Al-Omairi opposed the forgiveness and write-off of Iraqi debts, which amount to approximately 16 billion dollars in loans provided by Kuwait to Iraq during the Saddam Hussein era, with most given during the Iran-Iraq War (1980-1988).
Al-Omairi stated that the debt owed by Iraq to Kuwait is a right of the Kuwaiti people and that no one has the right to negotiate this debt.
While some Kuwaitis are calling for forgiveness of debts and compensation, Al-Omairi believes these voices should be ignored because they do not represent the Kuwaiti people, noting that Iraq possesses significant oil wealth and that Kuwait and Kuwaitis have never waived these loans at any time.
On November 8, 2008, Kuwaiti MP Mulla Al-Mulla proposed that Kuwait should allow Iraq to pay its debts via natural gas, a statement reported by Arab Times.

This method would allow Kuwait to recover its money from Iraq.
On April 25, 2007, Kuwaiti MP Saleh Ashour issued a statement strongly supporting the government in Baghdad and calling for the reopening of the Kuwaiti embassy in Baghdad.
Kuwaiti MP Ghanem stated that it was still too early to reopen the Kuwaiti embassy in Baghdad and believed this issue should be postponed until security conditions in Iraq improve.
On December 28, 2008, Kuwaiti MP Makhloof Al-Azemi protested.

== Kuwait–Jordan relations ==

== Kuwait–Yemen relations ==

Yemen was a member of the United Nations Security Council in 1990 and in 1991.

Kuwait cancelled aid programs, expelled thousands of Yemeni workers, and cut diplomatic communications.

== Kuwait–United States relations ==

The start date is 1964 [cite: 26].
The United States opened a consulate in Kuwait in October 1951, which was later upgraded to an embassy.
The United States supports Kuwait's efforts in multilateral diplomacy to build more cooperation among Gulf Cooperation Council countries.
Strategic cooperation between the United States and Kuwait increased in 1987 when a naval protection system was implemented to guarantee freedom of navigation in the Arabian Gulf.
Eleven Kuwaiti tankers had their flags changed to United States flags.
The strategic partnership between the United States and Kuwait was significantly intensified after Iraq invaded Kuwait.
The United States led the UN Security Council, which demanded Iraq withdraw from Kuwait and authorized the use of force if necessary.
These operations liberated Kuwait.
The United States developed multinational military operations including Operation Desert Shield and Operation Desert Storm.
Operation Iraqi Freedom began in 2003.

They monitored and enforced Iraq's compliance with UN Security Council resolutions.
The United States provided military and technical assistance to Kuwait after its liberation through both Foreign Military Sales (FMS) and commercial sources.
The US Military Cooperation Office in Kuwait reports to the US Embassy and manages the Foreign Military Sales (FMS) program.
There are currently more than 100 open FMS contracts between the US Army and the Kuwaiti Ministry of Defense with a total value of $8.1 billion.
The US Army has organized major purchases for Kuwaiti defense forces, including Patriot missile systems, F-18 fighter jets, M1 Abrams main battle tanks, and AH-64D Apache helicopters.
The Kuwaiti navy underwent significant modernization with US boats.
Kuwaiti attitudes toward American products have been favorable since the Gulf War of 1991.
Kuwait publicly abandoned the second and third levels of the Arab boycott against Israel in 1993, which had affected American companies.
Kuwait is the fifth-largest market in the Middle East region.

The United States is currently the largest supplier of goods and services to Kuwait, with the total value of US exports to Kuwait at $2.14 billion.
American companies have a competitive advantage in many fields requiring advanced technology, including oil field equipment and services, electric power generation, distribution equipment, wired and wireless communication equipment, consumer goods, and military equipment.
Kuwait is an important partner in the ongoing US-led campaign against international terrorism by providing military, diplomatic, and intelligence assistance, logistical support, and efforts to prevent terrorist financing.

== Kuwaiti parliamentary statements on Iran ==

On July 13, 2008, Kuwaiti MP Jassim Al-Kharafi gave an interview to Kuwaiti state-owned television in which he publicly accused the West of provoking Iran regarding the nuclear issue and stated that there are provocative Western statements to which Iran responds in the same way.
Al-Kharafi stated that the issue of this dialogue requires sensitivity rather than escalation and that Iran should not be treated as one of the US states.

== Relations with Oman ==

Kuwait is a major investor in Oman.

== Relations with Israel and the Palestinian cause ==

A large part of the population, which has significant social and economic weight, refuses any normalization with Tel Aviv and reaffirmed this refusal following agreements between Israel and the United Arab Emirates and Bahrain. A normalization decision with Israel cannot override unfavorable public opinion. In August 2020, the Parliament reiterated its support for the Palestinian cause, considering it to be 'the main cause of Arabs and Muslims' in a press release signed by 41 out of 50 members of the Assembly. The monarchy does not recognize the existence of the State of Israel and always refers to Israel as a 'Zionist entity' and as 'Occupied Palestine'. Media in the country refer to Palestinians killed by the occupation army as 'martyrs'.

== Role of the Parliament in foreign policy ==

Major foreign policy decisions are made by the parliament.
In Gulf monarchies, foreign policy decisions can override unfavorable public opinion.

== Domestic protests regarding the Gaza conflict ==

Kuwaiti MPs Musalim Al-Burak, Marzouq Al-Ghanem, Jumaan Al-Harbi, Ahmed Al-Milfi, Mohammed Haif Al-Mutairi, Ahmed Al-Saadoon, and Nasser Al-Saleh protested, while Walid Al-Tubtabai protested in front of the National Assembly building.
The protesters opposed Israeli attacks on the Gaza Strip, burned Israeli flags, and held signs reading "No to hunger, no to submission".
Protests against Israeli attacks on Gaza took place on January 3, 2009, with participation from Nasser Al-Sanai, Waleed Al-Tubtabai, and Adnan Abdulsamad.
These protests were held before the National Assembly.
A protest occurred on January 8, 2009, after the Friday prayer, and Jamal Harbesh protested in front of the National Assembly.

Protesters urged Arab leaders to take a stronger stance against Israeli attacks, called for the opening of the Rafah crossing, and sought to end the siege imposed by Israel on Gaza residents.

== Kuwait and India ==

India and Kuwait have friendly relations that are traditional.
Geographic proximity, historical ties, trade links, and cultural connections support the relationship between India and Kuwait.
A large number of Indian expatriates reside in Kuwait and continue to support and nurture the long-term relationship between India and Kuwait.
India was a natural trading partner and a destination for higher education.
The Indian rupee was legal tender in Kuwait until 1961.
High-level visits from India to Kuwait included Dr.
Zakir Husain, the former Vice President of India, in 1965; Prime Minister Indira Gandhi in 1981; and Shri M.
Hamid Ansari, the former Vice President of India, in 2009.
High-level visits from Kuwait to India included Sheikh Sabah Al-Salem Al-Sabah as Crown Prince and Prime Minister in November 1964; Sheikh Jaber Al-Ahmad Al-Jaber Al-Sabah as Emir of the country in 1980; Sheikh Jaber Al-Ahmad Al-Jaber Al-Sabah as Emir of the country again in 1983 during the Non-Aligned Movement summit; and Sheikh Sabah Al-Ahmad Al-Jaber Al-Sabah as Emir of the country in 2006.

There are approximately 600,000 Indians in Kuwait, forming the largest expatriate community and serving as the top priority community among expatriates in Kuwait.
Kuwait considers India a rapidly growing economy.
The Indian community includes a large number of unskilled expatriates and semi-skilled workers, alongside professionals such as engineers, doctors, and accountants.
Scientists, software experts, management consultants, and architects are part of the Indian community, as are skilled workers such as technicians and nurses.
There has been a recent increase in the number of qualified Indian experts working in technology fields, particularly in software and finance sectors in Kuwait.
India also requires semi-medical staff with a good reputation in the healthcare sector.

There are approximately 300 associations within the Indian community in Kuwait, of which 128 associations are currently registered with the embassy.

== Kuwait and China ==

== Denmark ==

On November 6, 2006, the Kuwaiti parliament voted 22 in favor and 15 against to approve cutting diplomatic relations with Denmark, a decision triggered by cartoons depicting the Prophet Muhammad published in the newspaper Jyllands-Posten.
The parliament allocated approximately $50 million (39.20 million euros) to defend the image of the Prophet in the West, though all votes were non-binding and the Kuwaiti cabinet was not obligated to comply.
Kuwaiti MP Abdul Samad voted in favor of severing diplomatic relations, arguing that diplomatic and commercial relations with Denmark must be cut and that they should not eat Danish cheese.
MP Al-Rashid voted against severing diplomatic relations, arguing that Muslims should remain positive and claiming it was individuals, not governments, who insulted Prophet Muhammad.
In February 2008, MP Abdullah Al-Rumi called for an end to the boycott of Kuwait.
The newspaper reported that Al-Rashid said Christians were being insulted in many of their mosques in Kuwait.

== France ==

France recognized Kuwait's independence two months after its proclamation.
Kuwait became an important oil supplier for France.
France adopted a position favorable to Arab countries starting in 1967, which allowed France to engage in strong cooperation with Arab countries and enabled France to sign contracts in the field of armaments.
France liberated Kuwait in 1991 as part of Operation Daguet, acting alongside the United States, with the French army participating in the military coalition that liberated Kuwait.
France concluded a defense agreement with Kuwait in 1992, which was renewed in 2009.
The agreement allows France to maintain a military presence in the Gulf, allows France to intervene against ISIS in 2015, and stipulates that France and Kuwait conduct joint military exercises.
Kuwaiti military personnel are trained in France.
Economically, Kuwait is the third-largest supplier to France among Gulf countries.
Contracts for arms purchases between Kuwait and France totaled 1.1 billion euros in 2017.
