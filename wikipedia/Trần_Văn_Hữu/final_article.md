# Trần Văn Hữu

Trần Văn Hữu (9 March 1896 – 17 January 1984) served as president of the government of Cochinchina from 1948 to 1949, then as Prime Minister of the State of Vietnam from 1950 to 1952.

He was born in 1896, in Long My village, Chau Thanh district, Vinh Long province (now Thanh Duc commune, Long Ho district, Vinh Long province) into a wealthy landowner family.
His house is in the same village as Trần Văn Hương (later Prime Minister of the Republic of Vietnam), while Phạm Hùng's house (later Prime Minister of the Socialist Republic of Vietnam) is located opposite the other side of the Long Hồ River (belonging to Vietnam).
Long Phuoc village ).
His father held the position of incense in the village.
At a young age, Tran Van Huu studied the French program and received his secondary education at Lycée Chasseloup Laubat.
Growing up, he went to France to study and graduated with a degree in agricultural engineering, having also studied in Tunisia with a specialization in the tropics.
When he returned home, he worked at a real estate bank, specifically the Crédit Foncier de l'Indochine, where he held an inspector position.
Tran Van Huu worked in the 1920s at the agriculture department of French Indochina.
Tran Van Huu became a significant landowner in Cochinchina and an influential person in the colony's upper class.
He married a French citizen of Vietnamese descent.
Trần Văn Hữu acquired French nationality.
He was a Francophile, a monarchist, and a Catholic believer.

On September 23, 1945, after the August Revolution broke out and the Democratic Republic of Vietnam was established, the French army opened fire to occupy Saigon, starting the Indochina war.
With the goal of restoring colonial rule in Vietnam and Indochina, France established a pro-French government "Autonomous Cochinchina".
Tran Van Huu started participating in politics from here.
In July 1946, as a member of the French delegation, he boarded the French ship Dumont Durville to attend the Fontainebleau conference.

In December 1946, in Cochinchina a pro-French cabinet led by Dr. Le Van Hoach as Prime Minister was established, and Tran Van Huu was awarded the position of Minister of Finance.
Due to the unstable political situation at this time, many civil servants quit their jobs, or left for resistance zones.
In response, Tran Van Huu decided to increase wages for civil servants, calling them back to work for the government.
However, the activities of the government of Le Van Hoach proved ineffective.
Another government, led by Lieutenant General Nguyen Van Xuan, was established.

In 1947, before returning to the country to establish a government, former emperor Bao Dai invited many figures such as Mr. Ngo Dinh Diem, doctor Phan Huy Quat, doctor Le Van Hoach, Tran Van Huu, Nguyen Ton Hoan... to meet.
in Hong Kong.
In October 1947, Tran Van Huu was promoted to Deputy Prime Minister in the new administration.
He reorganized the administrative apparatus from central to local levels, bringing many Vietnamese into power in an effort to create a real government image of the Vietnamese people.
However, his efforts did not bring much fruit because in reality, the French colonial government, both civilian and military in Vietnam, remained the decisive voice.

== Early career and colonial service ==

After his studies, he entered the service of the colonial administration and worked as an agricultural engineer from 1915 to 1929.
He obtained French citizenship in 1925 and moved into the colonial finance sector in 1929.
In 1935, he ran for election for the Colonial Council of Cochinchina.
He joined the Indochinese Democratic Party (Dang Dan Chu) in 1939.
Colonial authorities reported on his cooperation, and he did not cooperate with the Japanese or the Viet Minh during World War II.

== Formation of the State of Vietnam ==

The state Trần Văn Hữu led was a client state, and he was one of the politicians involved in a plan by French High Commissioner Georges Thierry d'Argenlieu to build a pro-French Cochinchina separated from the north.
The government he served under was a Provisional Government officially named the Provisional Government of South Vietnam, which was colonial in nature.
This state was to be led by Emperor Bảo Đại, and the State of Vietnam was founded within the framework of the French Union.
The government of Trần Văn Hữu was intended to be a transitional step towards the reunification of Vietnam, but colonial and Cochinchinese autonomist factions blocked the reunification process.
Nguyen Van Xuân left his position and took the head of the Provisional Central Government of Vietnam.
Cochinchine was reunified with the rest of Vietnam, and Emperor Bảo Đại took office the following month.
Bảo Đại became the head of state of Vietnam, and the State of Vietnam had Bao Dai as its head of state.
The State of Vietnam established its formal government in July 1949 [16,17].

== Appointment and cabinet formation ==

Tran Van Huu replaced Nguyen Phan Long in April 1949, as Nguyen Phan Long was considered too pro-American and ambiguous towards the communist Viet Minh.
Tran Van Huu formed a cabinet of southerners in Southern Vietnam (Nam Ky) [cite: 6].
Nguyen Van Tam joined the cabinet as the head of the General Security Service and later became the Minister of the Interior [cite: 6].
A student and schoolchildren struggle occurred in Saigon in October 1949, during which the students demanded the use of Vietnamese instead of French in schools.
Tran Van Huu held the position of Governor of South Vietnam and replaced Nguyen Phan Long as Prime Minister.
Tran Van Huu took office on May 6, 1950, an appointment that occurred three months after the student suppression incident.

== Military and security policies ==

Trần Văn Hữu was granted special powers by Bảo Đại to combat the Viet-Minh guerrilla.
He held the portfolios of defense and foreign affairs.
As Minister of Interior, he established the General Staff Department of the army and opened a school to train Vietnamese officers to replace French officers.
He contributed to building the State of Vietnam army and armed sectarian forces such as Cao Dai, Hoa Hao, and Catholic groups as well as the Binh Xuyen military force.
The Vietnamese government leader had to manage the armed communist opposition and strengthen the Vietnamese National Army.
High Commissioner Jean de Lattre de Tassigny undertook efforts to strengthen the army for the conflict.
Bảo Đại and the French judged it necessary to entrust the government to a specialist in repression.

== Diplomacy and international recognition ==

Trần Văn Hữu attended the Conference of Pau in his capacity as Prime Minister and called for greater autonomy for the states of the French Union.
In early September 1951, he led a delegation of the State of Vietnam to a conference of 51 nations held in San Francisco, USA, which focused on contributions to defeating Japan in World War II.
The Vietnamese delegation attended the conference by invitation from the US government.
At the conference, he expressed support for Western allied countries, demanded war reparations from Japan, and asserted Vietnam's sovereignty over the two archipelagos of Truong Sa and Hoang Sa.
Vietnam signed for the peace-building effort and confirmed its long-standing sovereignty over the Spratly Islands (Trường Sa) and the Paracel Islands (Hoàng Sa).
On June 29, 1950, during Trần Văn Hữu's tenure as Prime Minister, France officially recognized the State of Vietnam as a sovereign member of the French Union based on the Pau Agreement.
On this date, the United States and the United Kingdom formally recognized the State of Vietnam and established diplomatic relations with it, while several other countries also formally recognized the State of Vietnam.

Trần Văn Hữu, a Minister and diplomat of the State of Vietnam, made a statement at the 1951 San Francisco conference.

== United States relations and aid ==

Tran Van Huu attempted to weaken the French position by approaching the USA.
The first US embassy was established in Saigon, where Donald Heath served as the ambassador.
The United States established a military mission in Vietnam and increased aid to Vietnam.
Under Mr.
Tran Van Huu, the United States became more deeply involved in Vietnam.
The United States launched a Vietnamese language program on the Voice of America (VOA).
The United States signed an aid agreement with the State of Vietnam government on September 7, 1951.

== Domestic governance and political stance ==

The head of the Vietnamese government advocated for real independence for his country and believed that independence was the only way to end the Indochina War.
Tran Van Huu presented a new cabinet on February 16, 1951.

== End of tenure and retirement ==

Trần Văn Hữu ended his political career and called for negotiations with the Viet Minh.
He was removed from office after the French side demanded his removal.
His term as Prime Minister of South Vietnam began on April 27, 1950, and he left the position in June 1952.
Nguyễn Văn Tâm replaced Trần Văn Hữu as Prime Minister.
He served as Minister of Foreign Affairs of the State of Vietnam, holding these positions concurrently from May 1950.
His retirement occurred after the founding of the Republic of Vietnam.
The Geneva Accords were signed in 1954.

== 1950 student protests and suppression ==

The student struggle received support from the intellectual community and the press in Saigon, where 125 intellectuals spoke out together to demand the release of arrested students.
A protest involving approximately 2,000 people took place on January 9, 1950, with protesters demanding that Tran Van Huu fulfill his promise to release 12 students who had been arrested during a previous strike and stating they would only disperse if their demands were met.
Tran Van Huu met with student and youth representatives in front of the Governor's residence at approximately 12:30 PM, threatening to disperse the protest by 1:30 PM and warning that force would be used if it was not dispersed by that time.
When the students and schoolchildren did not disperse, Tran Van Huu ordered approximately 500 soldiers and police to suppress the protest with violence, resulting in the death of a student named Tran Van On, injuries to many other people, and the arrest of many other people.

January 9th is known as the Vietnamese Student and Youth Day, a date established by the Socialist Republic of Vietnam government.
There is a report on the 1950 student suppression incident published in the newspaper Tuoi Tre.

He lived in France after Ngô Đình Diệm came to power in 1954 in South Vietnam and worked to undermine the Diệm regime.
Hữu served as leader of the Committee for Peace and Renewal of South Vietnam, an organization that lobbied for peace and the neutralization of Vietnam in the Cold War.
As part of this mission, in 1966 he visited Pope Paul VI and the United Nations Secretary General U Thant.

Because of his lobbying efforts and past political standing, Hữu was seen as an ally of the National Liberation Front (NLF) in Paris.
In 1969, the leadership of the NLF proposed Hữu as a possible minister of a new NLF government.

== Return to France and refugee status ==

The Geneva Conference on peace in Indochina took place in 1954, and the pro-French faction was defeated by the pro-American faction in southern Vietnam.
Tran Van Huu began to enjoy political refugee status in France.
He returned to Saigon from France in 1955 when the Ngo Dinh Diem administration was struggling and dealing with the National United Front of All Forces.
The Diem-Nhu administration viewed Mr.
Tran Van Huu's presence as a thorn or irritant.
Secret police blocked Tran Van Huu's car when he was traveling from Saigon to Tay Ninh to meet Cao Dai leader Pham Cong Tac.
The secret police forced him to turn his car around and return to Saigon while making threatening gestures.
As a result, he had to board a plane and returned to France by air.
Tran Van Huu's political role in Vietnam ended.

== Political activities and diplomatic efforts ==

Tran Van Huu had hopes of participating in politics, which were revived when the Ngo Dinh Diem administration was in danger.
On July 22, 1962, Tran Van Huu met with a group of high-ranking officials from the Hanoi government in Switzerland to discuss the situation in Vietnam.
Nguyen Huu Tho, a lawyer and Chairman of the National Liberation Front of South Vietnam, sent a letter.
In early November 1963, General Tran Van Huu sent a telegram to General Duong Van Minh praising him for successfully leading a coup d'état that overthrew the Ngo Dinh Diem regime, an event Tran Van Huu believed liberated southern Vietnam from a medieval-style dictatorship.
An interview with Tran Van Huu was broadcast by Swiss Radio on November 1, 1963.
During this period, Tran Van Huu frequently visited an American Protestant church in Paris, where a priest conveyed his message to US President John F.
Kennedy, and he volunteered to establish a transitional government in Saigon.

Tran Van Huu's proposal was completely ignored by President Kennedy, his political activities were limited to that level, and his political activities had no real impact in Vietnam.

== Trần Văn Vũ ==

Trần Văn Vũ was born in 1895.
Emperor Bảo Đại appointed Trần Văn Vũ Prime Minister of South Vietnam on April 27, 1950.

Trần Văn Vũ died on January 19, 1984, in Paris, France.
