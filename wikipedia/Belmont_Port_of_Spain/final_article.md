# Belmont Port of Spain

Belmont, in north-east Port of Spain, in the Republic of Trinidad and Tobago, is located at the foot of the Laventille Hills; it was the city's first suburb. In the 1840s–'50s, parts of the area were settled by Africans rescued by the Royal Navy from illegal slaving ships. In the 1880s–'90s, the population swelled rapidly, and the characteristic Belmont street pattern of narrow, winding lanes developed. The black professional class built large homes in Belmont, as they were excluded from the more expensive neighbourhoods such as St. Clair and Maraval; Belmont became known as "the Black St. Clair". Many of these large homes have been renovated and converted to business use, but some remain in family hands. Belmont currently is a lower-middle to middle-class residential neighbourhood. It was the birthplace and early home of many important Carnival designers and bandleaders. Belmont has 9,035 inhabitants.

== History ==

Belmont is named after a country house that dates from the 19th century and belonged to the Barry plantation-owning family, whose surrounding land also belonged to them.
The Barrys rented out the house and land to the respective governors of the island between 1803 and 1828, with Thomas Hislop serving as the first tenant.
After a devastating fire occurred in Port of Spain in 1808, Thomas Hislop conducted government affairs from his country house for two years.
Woodford named the country house 'Belmont'.
The pastureland south of the house was also given a name, and free settlers moved onto it in the 1840s after they were no longer tolerated within the city limits of Port of Spain following abolition, which at that time ended at the St.
Ann's River.
This emerging place was known by the name Freetown in its early years.
Belmont lacked urban planning and grew uncontrollably due to this lack.

The uncontrolled growth of Belmont led to a chaotic street network.
Belmont was incorporated into Port of Spain on January 1, 1899.

== Demographics and culture ==

The inability to buy homes in white neighborhoods was due to race-based value systems, and Belmont was primarily a Black residential area until the 20th century.
Belmont served as a center of Afro-Trinidadian culture, and other Black residential areas contributed to this cultural center.
The Carnival, Calinda, Calypso music, and steelbands originated from these areas.
Belmont is home to several steelbands that are known regionally or internationally, including Belmont 5th Dimension, Crescendoes Musicale, and Pan-Demonium.
Several carnival clubs are based in Belmont, and the Trinidad Theatre Workshop is based in Belmont.

== Infrastructure and economy ==

Belmont is known for a high crime rate that is characteristic of the 21st century.
No significant companies are located in Belmont apart from small businesses.
Major traffic arteries could not form in Belmont due to the complex network of streets in the area.
The Lady Young Road and the Laventille Road are important feeder roads.
The Port of Spain General Hospital, which is the largest hospital in Trinidad, is located in southwest Belmont.
Educational institutions in Belmont include St.
Francois Girls College, Belmont Boys Secondary School, Escallier Anglican Primary School, St.
Francis Girls Roman Catholic School, Belmont Secondary School, and Providence Girls Secondary School.
Holy Name Convent is a Catholic school that provides primary and secondary education.
The Belmont Church of Christ is located in Belmont.
The Colts FC football club was based in Belmont and played in the 1960s.

The league in which the Colts FC played was the highest level of Trinidadian football at that time.
