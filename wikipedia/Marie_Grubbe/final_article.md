# Marie Grubbe

Fru Marie Grubbe (1643–1718) was a member of the Danish nobility who drew a lot of attention by her many extramarital affairs. She has been the inspiration for books, plays and operas.

Daughter of the statesman and nobleman Erik Grubbe (1605–92) and his wife Maren Juul (1608–47).
She was married to Ulrik Frederik Gyldenløve, Count of Laurvig (1638–1704), Governor-general of Norway, the son of King Frederick III of Denmark, in 1660–70, the noble Palle Dyre (d. 1707) in 1673–91, and to the coachman Søren Sørensen Møller in 1691.

As the heir of her father, she was arranged to marry Ulrik Frederik Gyldenløve, Count of Laurvig (1638-1704), the illegitimate son of King Frederick III of Denmark by her relative Regitze, widow of the illegitimate son of King Christian IV of Denmark and Karen Andersdatter.

The Countess of Laurvig followed her husband to Oslo in 1664.
She had extramarital relationships with her husband's secretary Joachim Lambert, the Frenchman Blanquefort and her brother-in-law Stygge Høeg.
In 1667, the spouses separated.
As adultery under Danish law was punishable by death, she thanked her spouse for her life, as he had chosen not to have her killed.
She and the Count were divorced by permission of the King in 1670, and the former Countess was allowed to remarry and keep her fortune.
In 1673, her father arranged a new marriage with Palle Dyre.
In 1690, when she had an extramarital relationship with the younger coachman Søren Sørensen Møller, her father asked the king for permission to disinherit his daughter and to have her locked up for the rest of her life.
The king asked her spouse for his wishes, and he answered that he only wished for a divorce.
She was put under house arrest until the divorce was completed, after which she was disinherited and lost her right to remarry in Denmark.
She then married her lover Søren Sørensen Møller in Holsten in Germany instead.
The couple lived in poverty until they were given a lease on Grønsund Ferry House on Falster by the Queen dowager Charlotte Amalie of Hesse-Kassel, which enabled them to make a living from operating the ferry service to Møn and the associated inn.
After the death of the queen dowager in 1714, she lived on charity.
She once said to Ludvig Holberg, that she was happier with her third spouse than she had been with any other.

Some years ago, she was found to have been buried in Allerslev, under the name of Marie Grubbens.

== Early life and family background ==

Marie Grubbe, also known as Marie 'Maren' Grubbe, was born on August 25, 1643, in Havreballegård near Aarhus.
Her father, Erik Lauridsen Grubbe, was a Danish landowner and a county official (länsman).
Marie's mother gave birth to seven children, of whom all siblings except Marie and her sister Anne died as infants.
Marie was her father's last legitimate child, though he had at least one additional daughter from this relationship.

== Upbringing and court introduction ==

At the age of 14, Marie was sent to Copenhagen to live with her paternal aunt, Regitze Grubbe, who was married to Hans Ulrich Gyldenløve (born 1615, died 1645) and had been widowed for a long time.
Regitze was close to the Danish court and introduced her niece to the Danish court.

== First marriage to Ulrik Frederik Gyldenløve ==

Marie Grubbe married on December 16, 1660, and her influence was the main reason for the marriage.
The marriage was a secret one concluded the year before, and Gyldenløve had to dissolve his marriage to Sophie Urne, the mother of his two sons.
The marriage was unhappy from the beginning, and Gyldenløve left for a long trip abroad only a few weeks after the wedding.
Marie stayed in Denmark, and she accompanied her husband on a trip through the country.
Gyldenløve became Vice King of Norway in 1664, and she promised not to use his name.

== Separation and divorce from Gyldenløve ==

Marie Grubbe's sister Anne married Stygge Høeg, who was a friend of Gyldenløve and the son of the Imperial Chancellor Just Høeg.
Marie's father made several attempts to reconcile the couple, but the husband initiated the separation and Gyldenløve sent Marie back to her father in 1667.
Gyldenløve urged for a divorce, and the woman thanked her husband in the letter.
Gyldenløve paid Marie Grubbe 12,000 Reichstalers, which was derived from her maternal inheritance that Marie Grubbe had brought into the marriage.

== Travels and second marriage to Palle Dyre ==

Marie Grubbe undertook extensive travels after her divorce, visiting Paris and Nuremberg.
She was married to Palle Clausen Dyre, a landowner.
Marie Grubbe's sister was divorced, married Jørgen Arenfeldt in her second marriage, and died around 1680.
In 1685, Marie Grubbe moved with her husband to her father's estate.
They lived at Akershus.

== Conflict over inheritance and arrest ==

Dyre wanted to have the will authenticated by the King, but Arenfeldt, Dyre's widowed brother-in-law, sued against this action.
Marie and her husband had mistreated Erik Grubbe, who was 85 years old in 1690.
Dyre supported Erik Grubbe's request, and Erik Grubbe and Dyre approached the King together.
Søren Sørensen Møller was formerly a coachman of Marie's father and later became the landsteward of Tjele.
The affair took place while Marie's husband was traveling.
She was detained at her father's estate, which was located in Tjele.

== Divorce proceedings and outcome ==

A court hearing took place in January 1691 where servants of the father's estate testified as witnesses.
Palle Dyre submitted several character references provided by pastors and officials that attested to his innocence regarding the breakdown of the marriage.
Marie Grubbe protested in writing against her husband's actions, stating that the arrest prevented her from defending herself.
Marie Grubbe was divorced on March 23, 1691, and Dyre kept her dowry because of her adultery [cite: 1].
She inherited nothing from her father in 1692.

== Later life with Søren Møller ==

Dyre had several children, was wealthy, remarried, and was killed in February 1707 during a dispute over gambling.
Møller obtained a ferryman's position on the island of Falster near Stubbekøbing, which provided a moderate livelihood for the couple.
They lived a wandering life and survived on temporary jobs.
The conversation took place in 1711.

== Reputation and legacy ==

Marie Grubbe's life path was considered unconventional and scandalous by her contemporaries.

== Later life and death ==

Søren Møller was arrested in 1711 due to manslaughter charges stemming from an incident during a dispute at an inn.
He died in prison in 1714.
Marie Grubbe was at least ten years older than Søren Møller and outlived him for several years.
During the time she outlived him, she lived on poor relief.
Marie Grubbe likely died in the summer of 1718 in Allerslev.

== Divorce proceedings ==

The documents primarily concern divorce proceedings from 1690/91.
Grubbe gained control over her own love life.

Marie Grubbe has inspired many artists since Ludvig Holberg.
She has inspired the books Kysse-Marie by Juliane Preisler (1994), Blichers Brudstykker af en Landsbydegns Dagbog by Steen Steensen (1824), Fru Marie Grubbe by J. P. Jacobsen, (1876) and Hønse-Grethes Familie by H. C. Andersen (1869), the opera Marie Grubbe by Ebbe Hamerik (1940) and a play by Sven Lange.
In 1989/1990, a three-episode TV mini-series with the title Marie Grubbe has been produced in GDR-Polish-Hungarian-Danish co-production, directed by Christian Steinke, starring Austrian actress Mijou Kovacs as Marie Grubbe.

== Ludvig Holberg's account ==

Ludwig Holberg, who was young at the time, visited Marie Grubbe on Falster after having fled from Copenhagen due to the plague.
Holberg recorded Marie Grubb's life story through conversations with her that took place on Falster in 1711 or 1712.
Ludvig Holberg was the first to describe Marie Grubb's life story and published it in 1748 as number 89 of his letters.
The story is titled 'Hønse-Grethes Familie' (English: 'Hen-Grete's Family').

== Jens Peter Jacobsen's novel and adaptations ==

Jens Peter Jacobsen presented the first Danish naturalistic novel in 1876.
The novel contains inaccuracies and errors in some historical dates, including the age given for Møller when he met Marie Grubbe.
Jacobsen's novel was adapted into an opera in 1940.
The film was based on Jacobsen's novel.

== Other literary works ==

Marie Grubbe became a subject in European literature.
Wilhelmine Corinth published the novel *Die Fährfrau* in 1983, which depicts the passionate life of Marie Grubbe.
Lone Hørslev published the historical novel *Dyrets år* in 2014, a book about the life of Grubbe.
August Fjelstrup wrote an entry about Marie Grubbe.

== Film and television ==

Marie Grubbe was portrayed in films.
Her life story was adapted into a film in 1990.
