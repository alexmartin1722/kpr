# Marcel Cohen

Marcel Samuel Raphaël Cohen (February 6, 1884 – November 5, 1974) was a French linguist. He was an important scholar of Semitic languages and especially of Ethiopian languages. He studied the French language and contributed much to general linguistics.

Marcel Cohen was born in Paris.
He studied at the Lycée Condorcet.
He attended Antoine Meillet's lectures at the Collège de France and the École pratique des hautes études.
In 1905 he registered at the École des langues orientales and graduated in 1909.
He Studied Amharic (under Mondon-Vidailhet), French linguistics, Sanskrit, Ge'ez and South Arabian.
He wrote his thesis on the Arabic dialect of the Jews of Algiers (Le parler arabe des juifs d'Alger).
Between March 1910 and June 1911, he undertook a journey to Ethiopia in which he collected much material on Ethiopian languages.
He succeeded Mondon-Vidailhet, who had just died, as chargé de cours d'Abyssin at the École des langues orientales.
In 1916 he became Assistant Professor and in 1926 a full Professor.
In 1919 he also became professor of Ethiopic at the École pratique des hautes études.
He had several students who became distinguished éthiopisants, such as: Wolf Leslau, Stefan Strelcyn and Joseph Tubiana.

== Early life and education ==

Marcel Cohen was born in the 9th arrondissement of Paris into a Jewish family.
He studied at the Sorbonne and under Ferdinand Brunot, Sylvain Lévy, Joseph Halévy, and Mario Roques.
Cohen also studied under Mario Roques at the École pratique des hautes études and had Joseph Vendryès as a teacher.
Meillet encouraged Cohen to study Semitic languages, and he learned Ge'ez from Joseph Halévy.
He learned spoken Arabic at the École des langues orientales and graduated in Maghrebi Arabic from the École nationale des langues orientales.
Cohen passed the grammar agrégation exam in 1908.
He obtained a diploma in Maghrebi Arabic the year after passing the Agrégation.

Cohen fought in the Resistance during World War II.

== Academic career and research ==

Marcel Cohen was elected to the chair of Ethiopian studies at the EPHE in 1919 and later became a director of studies for ancient South Arabia.
Two years later, he was appointed professor of Amharic.
He taught Amharic at the École nationale des langues orientales and was the director of Ethiopian and South Arabian studies at the École pratique des hautes études.
He obtained his habilitation in 1924 in Paris.
Cohen continued teaching at EPHE until 1967 [cite: 3].
He was responsible for the collection of World Languages, a responsibility he shared with his mentor Antoine Meillet.
He was the driving force behind the Bulletin of the Société de Linguistique de Paris for several decades.
Marcel Cohen conducted fifty years of research covering linguistics, ethnography, sociology, criticism, and pedagogy.

Marcel Cohen played a major role in reorganizing the CNRS after World War II [cite: 7].

== Political engagement ==

Marcel Cohen was a convinced communist who admired the Russian Revolution and was engaged with the Communist Party of France.
He joined the French Communist Party in 1920 and remained a member of the party.
Cohen wrote regularly and many articles for the communist daily newspaper L'Humanité.
In the 1930s, he was a co-founder of the Workers' University.
He engaged as an antifascist, and his engagement was against the Italian occupation of Ethiopia.

== Military service and World War II ==

Cohen served as a machine gunner on the Balkans during World War I, where he was wounded and received the Croix de guerre, ending his service with the rank of lieutenant.
He lost his professorship in December 1940 due to antisemitic regulations during the German occupation.
He went underground in 1941 due to the racial laws of the Vichy regime.
He joined Henri Rol-Tanguy's staff during the liberation of Paris and regained his professorship after liberation in October 1944.

== Honors and death ==

Cohen was an honorary doctor of the University of Warsaw, the University of Manchester, and the University of Prague.
He received an honorary doctorate on June 10, 1960, which was awarded by the University of Warsaw.
He also received awards, one of which was the Legion of Honour.
Marcel Cohen died in Viroflay and in Cugand, Vendée.

== Early Works and Habilitation ==

His habilitation thesis consisted of two works, one of which was titled 'Couplets amhariques du Choa' and contains Amharic couplets from the Choa region, a dialect he researched on-site.
The work 'Le parler arabe des Juifs d'Alger' was published in 1908 and is one of the first large-scale systematic studies of linguistic variation within a social group.
This work foreshadowed the development of sociolinguistics.
He received the Volney Prize in 1912 for his work 'Le parler arabe des Juifs d'Alger'.
The work titled 'Le Langage de l'École Polytechnique' was published in the 'Mémoires de la Société linguistique de Paris'.

== Abyssinian and Semitic Linguistics ==

The book *Essai comparatif sur le vocabulaire et la phonétique du chamito-sémitique* compares vocabulary and phonetics of Chamic-Semitic languages and was reprinted in 1969 with the ISBN 2-85265-016-9.
He published a monumental Treatise on the Amharic language in 1936.
The report on the linguistic mission in Abyssinia was edited with Antoine Meillet.
The book *Langues chamito-sémitiques* was published in 1952 by CNRS.
The work *Les langues du monde* was republished in 1952, 1981, and 2000.

== Sociolinguistics and the French Language ==

His writings were linguistic commentaries on the French language that were collected in a series of volumes.
He published an innovative dictionary titled *Dictionnaire du français vivant* with co-publishers Maurice Davau and Maurice Lallemand.
The dictionary was published in Paris in 1972 and was republished in 1975 and 1979.
The book *Pour une sociologie du langage* was published in Paris in 1956.
The work titled *Materials for a Sociology of Language* consists of two volumes published in Paris, with the first volume appearing in 1971 and the second in 1978.
A Spanish edition of the work was published in 1974.
The book *Le Français en 1700 d'après le témoignage de Gile Vaudelin* was published in Paris in 1946.
The book *Grammaire française en quelques pages* was published in Paris in 1948 and 1964.
The book *Cinq cents ans de phrase française* was published in Paris in 1954.

Marcel Cohen was interested in sociolinguistics and authored works on issues in sociolinguistics.
He proposed to study the French language and its evolution as a social fact [cite: 8].
The work 'Pour une sociologie du langage' was published in 1956 by Albin Michel.
The book 'Notes de méthode pour l'histoire du français' was published in Moscow in 1958 and was first published in 1956 under the title 'Pour une sociologie du langage'.

== Linguistic research ==

Marcel Samuel Raphaël Cohen was a French Jewish linguist who studied synchronistic linguistics and engaged in sociolinguistics.
He was a comparative linguist and a specialist in Hamito-Semitic languages, particularly in Ethio-Semitic languages.
Cohen made a significant contribution to proving the relationship between 'Hamito-Semitic' languages.
He is known for his work on materials for a sociology of language, which were prepared as early as 1949 and inspired all subsequent French sociolinguistics.
He was a pioneer in the study of slang and in the study of children's language.
He introduced many unexplored fields into modern linguistics and had a considerable influence on the development of this discipline.
He was one of the most prolific French-language linguists and one of the most innovative French-language linguists.
