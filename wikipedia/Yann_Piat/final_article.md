# Yann Piat

Yann Annabelle Piat (née Marie; 12 June 1949 – 25 February 1994) was a French politician. She served in the National Assembly from 1986 to 1994, first with the extreme right party National Front, then with the centrist party Union for French Democracy. She was assassinated on February 25, 1994.

Yann Piat was born on June 12, 1949, in Saigon, French Cochinchina.

== Birth and parentage ==

Yannick Annabelle Marie, whose birth name was Yannick Marie, was born in Saigon, which was part of French Indochina at the time, during the Indochina War.
Her mother was Luce-Marie Millet, a female auxiliary of the French Army who was imprisoned, while her father is unknown.

== Early National Front career and 1986 election ==

She was a member of the National Front (FN) until 1988 and became the departmental secretary of the party in Landes.
She was elected as a deputy for Var in 1986 during the 1986 legislative elections.
The candidate ran in the third constituency of the Var department and defeated Gaston Biancotto, a socialist candidate.
Gaston Biancotto was eliminated in the second round, and the candidate became the only deputy representing the Fronte.
She was a candidate for the presidency of the National Assembly in 1986 and was supported by the National Front.
The vote took place at the National Assembly, where she won seats in the first round of voting and lost two seats in the second round of voting.

== 1988 re-election and party departure ==

In 1988, Yann Piat joined the Union for French Democracy (UDF) and received support from Jean-Marie Le Pen.
She was the only National Front (FN) parliamentarian re-elected in 1988, receiving 53.71% of the votes.
Her re-election was due to an agreement with the Union for French Democracy (UDF) that involved withdrawing a right-wing candidate from the Piat constituency, and in exchange, the Var branch of the FN withdrew all its qualified candidates for the second round.
The candidate confirmed their seat in the National Assembly during the 1988 legislative elections.
She gradually drifted away from her godfather and moved closer to the Union for French Democracy (UDF).
She left the party in October 1988 following a dispute with Jean-Marie Le Pen.
Jean-Marie Le Pen insulted Michel Durafour, who was then the Minister of Public Service, by using the term 'Durafour-crématoire' in response to the minister's call to 'exterminate the National Front'.

== 1993 election and UDF affiliation ==

She was considered for inclusion in the UDF-RPR list intended for the regional elections of March 1992, but was ultimately excluded from the list. In exchange for this abandonment, she secured the right-wing nomination for the 1993 legislative elections after abandoning her regional ambition. This information is attributed to Hervé Gattegno. She ran under the UDF label in the 1993 election, which involved three candidates.
She received 42.40% of the votes and was re-elected to the legislative assembly in the third constituency of Var.

== Conspiracy theories and legal disputes ==

Investigative journalists André Rougeot and Jean-Michel Verne relaunched the conspiracy theory trail in the book titled 'L'Affaire Yann Piat' and 'Des assassins au cœur du pouvoir'.
The authors claim the deputy knew too much about real estate dealings involving politicians and about Varois organized crime.
The authors cite the statements of a 'general' presented as a former member of the Military Intelligence Directorate.
According to these statements, François Léotard and Jean-Claude Gaudin were the instigators of the murder.
François Léotard was referred to by the nickname 'Encornet' in these statements, and Jean-Claude Gaudin was referred to by the nickname 'Trottinette'.
The 'general' was quickly revealed to be a fraud and described as a mythomaniac and paranoid individual.
The 'general's name was Jacques Jojon, who had already been convicted of crimes.
Jacques Jojon admitted having contact with investigative journalist André Rougeot but denied being André Rougeot's main informant.
