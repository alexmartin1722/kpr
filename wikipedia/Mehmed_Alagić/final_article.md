# Mehmed Alagić

Mehmed Alagić (8 July 1947 – 7 March 2003) was a Bosnian general in the Army of the Republic of Bosnia and Herzegovina.

== Early life ==

Mehmed Alagić was born in Fajtovci near Sanski Most, RS of Bosnia and Herzegovina, Yugoslavia.

Mehmed Alagić graduated from the Yugoslav People's Army (JNA) military academy in Banja Luka in 1970.
He later taught at the Reserve Officers' School and served as its head, which was also located in Banja Luka.
Mehmed Alagić completed studies at the Command and Staff School, a high military school for officer staff, in 1986.
After completing his studies, he became an operational affairs officer for the 36th Mechanized Brigade.
In 1986, he became commander of the 36th mechanised brigade and in 1989 commander of the Zrenjanin brigade as part of the Novi Sad Corps.
The infantry brigade was located in Zrenjanin.
He was appointed as the chief of staff of an infantry brigade.

Alagić left the JNA on February 27, 1991, and his resignation was a result of the breakup of Yugoslavia.
On 13 December 1993 he served in the 17th Krajina Brigade of the 3rd Corps of the Army of the Republic of Bosnia and Herzegovina.
On 8 March 1993 he became commander of the Operational Group (OG) Bosnian Krajina within the 3rd Corps.
He became commander of the 3rd Corps on 1 November 1993 and held this position until February 26, 1994.
The 7th Corps was established on February 26, 1994, and Alagić became its first commander.
On February 26, 1994, he took over the command of the newly established Seventh Corps of the Army of the Republic of Bosnia and Herzegovina.

In March 1996, as a member of the SDA party, Alagić was elected for the mayor of Sanski Most municipality and a member of the Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina's Parliament.

In 2001 he was indicted for war crimes at the ICTY, but died before the trial commenced. Subsequent to his death, the case was terminated. His co-defendants were convicted and served jail time.

In December 2019, Sarajevo Canton initiated a process to name a major street in the city in General Alagić's honour.

== ICTY indictment and trial ==

The trial took place in The Hague, where Mohamed Alagić died.
According to the Hague indictment, he was charged with murder, cruel treatment, wanton destruction of cities, towns, and villages not justified by military necessity, plundering public or private property, intentional killing, inhumane treatment, unlawful imprisonment of civilians, extensive destruction of property not justified by military necessity, violence against life, destroying or intentionally damaging institutions dedicated to religion (violation of laws and customs of war), and intentionally causing great suffering or serious injury to body or health.
Alagić was held criminally responsible for war crimes committed by soldiers under his custody.
The International Court in The Hague suspended the trial against Alagić on March 21, 2003.
The ICTY issued an order to close the case on March 21, 2003, after Alagić had died before the order was issued.
