# Lady Emmeline Stuart-Wortley

Lady Emmeline Charlotte Elizabeth Stuart-Wortley (née Manners; 1806 – 20 October 1855) was an English poet and writer, best known for her Travels in the United States, etc. During 1849 and 1850. She was editor of The Keepsake volumes for 1837 and 1840.

She was a daughter of John Manners, 5th Duke of Rutland, and his wife, the former Lady Elizabeth Howard. On 17 February 1831, married Hon. Charles Stuart-Wortley-Mackenzie, a son of James Stuart-Wortley-Mackenzie, 1st Baron Wharncliffe. They had a daughter, Victoria, Lady Welby.

After the death of her husband in 1844, she began to travel as a wealthy Victorian widow, along with her daughter, Victoria. In October 1855, she died of dysentery in the area between Antioch and Beirut, while travelling through Ottoman Empire regions following the history of the early Christians.

== Early life and family ==

Emmeline Charlotte Elizabeth Stuart-Wortley was born on May 2, 1806.
She was the second daughter of John Manners, 5th Duke of Rutland.
Elizabeth Howard was the daughter of Frederick Howard, 5th Earl of Carlisle.
She was previously courted by Prince Leopold of Saxe-Coburg.
Charles Stuart-Wortley was the second son of James Stuart-Wortley and was a politician.
Emmeline Charlotte Elizabeth Stuart-Wortley belonged to the Romantic era.

== Children ==

One of the children was Archibald Henry, who lived from 1832 to 1890 and married Lavinia Rebecca Gibbins.
One of the children was Adelbert William John, who died in 1847.
One of the children was Victoria Alexandrina, who lived from 1837 to 1912 and married William Earle Welby-Gregory, 4th Baronet.

== Travels in Europe ==

They made multiple trips to the Netherlands, Italy, and Russia.
She traveled through Southeast Europe in the summer and autumn of 1838, and her travels included Serbia, Bulgaria, and other European territories of the Ottoman Empire.
She traveled to Italy and France in 1848 and experienced the February Revolution during her trip.
She began a journey in October 1851, and the destinations included Lisbon, Madeira, Spain, and Morocco.
They traveled to Northern Europe in 1853.

== Travels in the Americas ==

She traveled through America from spring 1849 to summer 1850.
They visited Canada, Mexico, Cuba, Panama, Ecuador, and Jamaica.
They met the Swiss-American scientist Louis Agassiz and US President Zachary Taylor.

== Later travels and death ==

Her husband died from complications of a riding accident, and her second son, Adelbert, died in 1847.
She intensified her travels after the deaths of her husband and son.
She departed for the Near East with her daughter in January 1855 and arrived near Jerusalem on May 1st.
She traveled through Constantinople and Cairo.
She broke her leg when a mule stepped on her.
She continued traveling to Antioch despite the injury.
She fell ill in the desert, and her daughter also fell ill.
Her companions left her for dead.
Her daughter was taken to Beirut and was saved after her death.

== Writing style ==

Her travel reports described her travels and experiences in an enthusiastic tone and included descriptions of the countries she visited.
