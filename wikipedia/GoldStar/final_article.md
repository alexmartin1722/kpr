# GoldStar

GoldStar was a South Korean electronics company established in 1958. The corporate name was changed to LG Electronics and LG Cable on February 28, 1995, after merging with Lucky Chemical. LG Cable was spun off from LG Electronics and changed its name to LS Cable in 2005.

== History ==

Goldstar was founded by Koo In-hoe, who is the founder of the LG Group, under the name Goldstar (Geumseong).
The company successfully entered the electronics sector and established its first overseas branch in New York City, USA, in March 1968.
A first sales office was established in the United States in August 1978, an achievement that occurred in 1978 when the company's exports were worth over $100 million.
A promotional exhibition hall named Technopia was launched in 1985.
The project started as a division of labor within the Hyundai Group.

GoldStar manufactured a wide variety of products, including radios, televisions, air conditioners, MSX home computers, LCD games, videocassette recorders, video and audio cassette tapes, microwave ovens, electronic typewriters, integrated circuits, escalators, elevators, injection molding machines, dehumidifiers, fans, and tractors.
The company started selling domestically produced radios one year after its establishment.
The first Korean telephone was introduced in November 1962.
The first Korean refrigerator was introduced in January 1965.
The first Korean black-and-white television was introduced in August 1966.
The first air conditioner from Korea was introduced in September 1968.
GoldStar televisions became a commonly sold brand of consumer television sets in the United States in the 1980s.

GoldStar Precision was a division manufacturing electronic test equipment such as multi-meters and oscilloscopes and industrial electronics.
The name was changed to LG Precision with the merger, with many of the same products and model numbers being produced but with new branding.
The measuring device division was acquired by EZ Digital in 1999, again with identical or similar model numbers.
The industrial products continue under the company name LIG Nex1.

== Financial performance and milestones ==

Goldstar reached the peak of its growth and profitability in the late 1980s and earned a profit of over $2 billion.
The company was saved from bankruptcy and achieved first place in the electronics industry.
Following this success, the company achieved first place in Korea in December 1991 and exported $2 billion worth of home appliances in December 1991.

GoldStar tractors began in 1975, as a division of Hyundai, in cooperation with Yanmar of Japan.
In 1977, they began cooperation with Fiat of Italy, and in 1983, was acquired by GoldStar.
Both companies, Lucky Chemicals and Goldstar Co.
Ltd, merged and formed Lucky-Goldstar in this year.
That same year, they began cooperation with Mitsubishi, and re-established cooperation with Fiat in 1984.
The company produced excavators.

Tractors were sold under the GoldStar and Fiat-GoldStar brands.
Fiat-GoldStar was a brand of tractors sold in cooperation with the Fiat company of Italy.
The Fiat-GoldStar name was discontinued when GoldStar changed its corporate name to LG Cable.

== Rebranding and dissolution ==

The company was dissolved on March 27, 1995, with the establishment of LG Electronics, an event triggered by the launch of the Challenge 2005 plan.
The company was re-established through a spin-off on April 1, 2002, and the surviving legal entity changed its name to LG EI.
In the following year, LG EI was absorbed and merged into LG CI, after which LG EI ceased to exist.
LG Electronics exported electronic products, and some of these exported products retained the old 'GoldStar' logo until 2002.
Some of LG Electronics' electronic products used the old Goldstar logo until 2002, including microwaves, window air conditioners, dehumidifiers, and audio equipment.
Only microwaves, window air conditioners, dehumidifiers, and audio equipment carried the old GoldStar logo, while the remaining electronic products were sold through LG Electronics.
Products sold in the domestic market also used mixed branding until 2002.
LG Electronics decided to unify its logo, and the unification process started in August 1996.

GoldStar also produced some models of computer monitors and optical disc drives.
The company produced electrical wires.
The company produced optical disk drives through a technical partnership with Japanese Hitachi Works.
The company produced elevators through a technical partnership with Japanese Hitachi Works.
