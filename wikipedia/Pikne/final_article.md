# Pikne

Pikne (also Piken or Pikker: the long one) is the god of lightning in Estonian mythology. In Finnish, lightning is sometimes called Pitkäinen, which is similar in meaning. It is likely that both are taboo euphemisms.

There was an Estonian satire and humor magazine called Pikker.

== Etymology and names ==

Pikne is also known as Piken, meaning 'the extended', Perkons (Kõu), Negaisu (Äike), Vecāko (Vanemb), and Debesu tēvu (Taevaisa).
The form 'Picker' later spread in Estonian pseudomithology and folklore literature, though mythologists consider it incorrect and state that the correct form is the South Estonian form 'Picken' mentioned later in the prayer.
In Russian translation, Pikker is called Pikne.

== Mythology and attributes ==

Pikne was possibly the weather god and is the god of thunder and rain.
Pikne throws lightning bolts.
Pikne uses the magic flute to drive away demons and to make it rain.
Pikne is considered a promoter of agriculture.
Pikne blocked the rain for a long time.
Pikne has a daughter named Ilmatütar [2,3,4].
Ilmatütar is the goddess of bad weather and the personification of the sky and air [2,3,4].
Uku is the god of thunder and lightning [2,3,4].
Kõu is considered the god of thunder [2,3,4].
Pikker is the god of thunder and lightning.

== Related deities and figures ==

Maan-Emo is a figure in mythology and is the goddess of female fertility and hunting luck.
Vanapagan is a figure in mythology, is a giant, and is more stupid than evil.

== Comparative mythology ==

The worship of Pikk was documented among other Finno-Ugric peoples and among the Wotians.

In the Middle Ages, the pagan priests made animal sacrifices to Pikne.
These reports come from medieval Estonian folk belief.
The most famous priest of Pikne (literally: thunder priest) was the seventeenth-century Jürgen of Wihtla (), who uttered the following prayer:

Take it Pikne, the bull we are offering with two horns and four hooves for ploughing and harvesting

Pikne was protector of the holy river Võhandu in Võru County, and punished people who built mills there by sending them no rain.
The incident along with the prayer was recorded by the pastor Johann Gutslaff in his work Kurtzer Bericht und Unterricht Von der Falsch-heilig genandten Bäche in Lieffland Wöhhanda (published in Tartu, 1644).

This prayer has been used by the Estonian composer Veljo Tormis in his 1974 choral work Litany to Thunder (text rendered into the Võro dialect of contemporary Southern Estonian and developed by the writer Ain Kaalep).

According to the myths collected by Matthias Johann Eisen, Pikne is the brother of Kõu and the son of Uku.
The evil underworld god Vanatühi stole his whistle or bagpipes.
Without blowing it, the gods couldn't help the farmers who were praying for rain.
Uku was angered and sent Pikne back to Earth, where he worked as a farmhand.
He visited a wedding, where he pretended to be a musician and regained his magic instrument (compare the Eddaic myth of Trymskvida'').

== In literature and folklore ==

Pikne is mentioned in the Estonian national epic *Kalevipoeg* in the third song, where the sons of Kalev are on a hunt and Linda is kidnapped.
After the sons of Kalev return, Kalev's younger son sits by the sea and has a dream in which his mother appears young and cheerful, swinging on a swing and singing.
In the dream, the lands of Kungla are to see a brocade skirt, Pikne is to see the patterns, and the stars are to see the embroidered apron.

== Scholarly sources and publications ==

Matthias Johann Eisen, an Estonian folklorist who lived from 1857 to 1934, collected and edited myths.
The work titled 'Kõu ja Pikker' was authored by Tarmo Kulmar.
The litany titled Pikse litaania was published in 1974, and a litany previously written by Johann Gutslaff exists.
A prayer to the thunder god Perkons in the Estonian language was included in a book by Johan Gutslaf, an Estonian-German pastor and writer.
