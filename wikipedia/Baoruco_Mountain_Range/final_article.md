# Baoruco Mountain Range

The Bahoruco Mountain Range—Sierra de Bahoruco (or Sierra de Bahoruco) is a mountain range located in the far southwestern region of the Dominican Republic. It is within Pedernales, Independencia, Barahona, and Bahoruco Provinces. A large part of the area is protected within the Sierra de Bahoruco National Park (Parque Nacional Sierra de Bahoruco), also a Biosphere reserve.

Its name is mentioned for the first time by Bartolomé de las Casas in his "Brief History of the Indies", who takes it from the phonetic sound used by the Taino Indians to describe that region.

It is rich in deposits of salt and gypsum, and also has a lagoon in the southern part (Oviedo lagoon) and between this mountain range and the Neiba mountain range is Lake Enriquillo, the largest in the Antilles.

The mountain range is a massif of volcanic origin from the Cretaceous period (from 145 to 66 million years ago) and later covered by a layer of calcareous sediment of marine origin, this means that the entire mountain range was once submerged under the sea and in mostly inhabited by corals that left their sediments.
The Sierra de Bahoruco is part of an underwater mountain range that runs across most of the Caribbean tectonic platform.
The mountain range is formed by a complex of tectonic faults resulting from the collision of the Caribbean and Atlantic plates, rising and leaving the sea.
This means that we can find rocks at more than 2000 meters high that at first glance can be seen remains of ancient corals that were in the sea.
The Sierra de Baoruco consists of a limestone plateau.
Geological forces pushed the Sierra de Baoruco northward.
The fluvial sediment comes from the Baoruco River.

== Geography ==

The Baoruco Mountain Range emerges south of Hispaniola Island and its emergence created the Barahona Peninsula in the Dominican Republic.
The mountain range is near the border with Haiti and continues into Haiti, where it transitions into the Massif de la Selle.
The highest part of the mountain range rises towards the west and penetrates into the Republic of Haiti, where it is known as the Massif de la Selle (Macizo de la Silla).
The Sierra de Baoruco is situated near the border with Haiti and constitutes an extension of the Massif de la Selle, which crosses Haiti.
The mountain range extends the La Selle chain, which is a continuation of the mountain massif.
The mountain chain extends to the Caribbean Sea.
The mountain range is 40 kilometers wide.
There are sharp ridges in the north that protrude above the Hoya de Enriquillo.
There are rolling slopes in the south that descend towards the Barahona peninsula.
There are three mountains.

The Sierra de Baoruco is located west of the capital city Santo Domingo.
There are three peaks higher than 2000 meters, including the highest peak, Loma del Toro, which has an elevation of 2367 meters.
Two of the mountains are unnamed, with one standing 2275 meters high and the other 2085 meters high.
Hoyo de Aceitillar is a remnant of an ancient lake located in one of the highest parts of the mountain range.

== Climate ==

A hot steppe climate prevails in the area.
The annual rainfall exceeds 600 millimeters.
May is the warmest month and December is the coldest month.
August is the rainiest month and February is the driest month.

== Ecology ==

The slopes are covered with pine trees.
Small patches of cloud forest are located in the eastern section.

== Mineral Resources ==

There are deposits of salt and gypsum in many places, including Salinas Barahona.
One of the salt deposits is located at Salinas Barahona.
One of the gypsum deposits is located at Laguna de Oviedo.

== Hydrology ==

The Baoruco Mountain Range contains a salt lake called Laguna de Oviedo.

== Conservation ==

The protected area is classified as UICN category II and covers an area of 1092 square kilometers.

Granberry and Vescelius (2004) suggest a Macoris etymology for the name Bahoruco, comparing it with baho-ro-eku 'within the jungle' in the purportedly related Warao language of the Orinoco Delta.
The Sierra de Bahoruco is named after the part located in the Dominican Republic.

The Sierra de Bahoruco are noted for the blue colored concretions called larimar, which formed in the range's volcanic vesicles.
Larimar is the Dominican name for a mineral assemblage of acidic silicate hydrates of calcium and sodium.
Larimar is a specific type of pectolite (occasionally as ratholite), a pyroxenoid mineral.
