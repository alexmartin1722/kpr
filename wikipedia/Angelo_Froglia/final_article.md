# Angelo Froglia

Angelo Froglia (23 March 1955 – 11 January 1997) was an Italian painter and sculptor mostly known for being involved in the scandal of the sculptures falsely attributed to Amedeo Modigliani in 1984.

Angelo Froglia was born in Livorno.
He attended the Accademia di Belle Arti in Florence.
The person obtained a diploma from an art high school.
In 1971 he returned to Livorno where he rented a studio and started painting.
In 1974 he participated to the 10th edition of the Rome Quadriennale.
In 1977, during the Years of Lead, he took part in the armed struggle and ended up in jail.
The individual was a member of Revolutionary Action (Azione Rivoluzionaria).
The individual committed an attack in 1978.
The target of the attack was the Livorno headquarters of Cisnal.
The individual served three and a half years in prison for the attack.
He was released in 1981 and moved back to Livorno where he resumed painting.

In 1984 Froglia sculpted a false Modigliani's head and threw it in the town's canal.
The sculpture was initially deemed as authentic before Froglia and others exposed the hoax.
Froglia also made a video about the event entitled "Peitho e Apate ... della persuasione e dell'inganno (Cherchez Modi)" and was given an award at the Turin Film Festival in 1984.
From 1985 he produced a great number of paintings and organised exhibitions in Italy and abroad, although his health was undermined by an excessive use of recreational drugs.
Angelo Froglia resumed working intensively from 1985.
Although Froglia never considered his role in the Modigliani scandal as a "hoax" but rather as an artistic performance, he grew tired of being associated with it.
Shortly before his death, Froglia told his friend and art critic Massimo Carboni that, "Time does not count, I work within my possibilities.
The important thing is the conviction that you put into it."

Angelo Froglia died following a long illness on 11 January 1997.

== The Modigliani scandal ==

The event exposed Angelo Froglia to the world.
Bettino Craxi had a fake Modigliani head that was part of his private collection in Hammamet.
The fake Modigliani head found by Angelo Froglia is included in the publication *Due pietre ritrovate di Amedeo Modigliani*, which was issued by Books & Company Editore, has the ISBN 978-88-7997-124-9, was endorsed by famous national art experts and critics, and became the catalog of the 'Modigliani hoax'.

== Later developments ==

In September 2010, a head was put up for sale on eBay with a starting price of 200,000.00 Euros.
A documentary film titled 'Le vere false teste di Modigliani' was produced in 2011 and reconstructs the story of the fake Modigliani heads.

== Cultural impact ==

Alice Barontini wrote a work titled 'Alla ricerca di Modi' about Angelo Froglia and a performance that challenged the critics.
The subjects are students who attend the Academy of Fine Arts of Florence.
