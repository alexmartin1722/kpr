# Pinkhus Turyan

Pinkhus Grigorievich Turjan () was the party organizer of the 269th separate sapper battalion in the 12 Army on the Southwestern Front. He was awarded the title Hero of the Soviet Union for his role in the Dnieper crossing.

He was born in Lebedin, in a working class Jewish family.
He graduated 7 classes becoming a member of CPSU in 1918.
Prior to joining the army forces he occupied administrative positions in the Kiev area.
Joins the army in July 1941.
During the crossing of the Dnieper river on 26–28 September 1943 in the district of the village of Petro-Svistunov he led the crossing of the 269th battalion of the 12th Army of the Soviet Union on the ferries, participating in battles for the bridgeheads and repelling counterattacks.
After the war he lived in Kiev working in construction management.

== Early life and education ==

Pinhas Grigoryevich Turyan was born on April 20, 1896, in Lebedin, Ukraine.
Lebedyn is located in the Cherkasy Oblast and is now part of the Shpola district in Cherkasy Oblast.
He completed 7 classes of an incomplete secondary school, which was at the level of incomplete secondary school.

== Pre-war career and political activity ==

He worked in a sugar factory and served as an administrative and economic employee in the Kyiv region.

== Military service before World War II ==

He participated in the civil war and fought against the White Guard.
He participated in the war with Poland and fought against the Poles.

== World War II service ==

In 1941, he was conscripted into the Red Army and later rejoined the Red Army.
Between September 26 and 28, 1943, he participated in the crossing of the Dnieper River in the Zaporizhzhia region, where Petro-Svyistunove is located in the Vilnian district of the Zaporizhzhia region.
He was wounded during these battles.

== Post-war life and death ==

He was transferred to the reserve in 1945 and remained in reserve status starting from that year.
Pinchus Grigorjewicz Turjan, also known as P.
H.
Turian and Pinhas Grigoryevich Turyan, died on August 30, 1976, in Kyiv.
He was buried in Kyiv at the City Cemetery 'Berkytsi' in plot number 77.

By decree of the Presidium of the Supreme Soviet on 19 March 1944 for his courage and heroism in crossing the Dnieper River and for holding the bridgehead on the right bank of the Dnieper, captain he was awarded the title Hero of the Soviet Union, the Order of Lenin, the Hero of the Soviet Union Gold Star, the Medal 'Golden Star', the Order of the Red Star, and medals.
His name is recorded at the Ukrainian Museum of the Great Patriotic War.

== Legacy ==

The Hero's name was inscribed on a stele located in the Ukrainian Museum of the Great Patriotic War.
