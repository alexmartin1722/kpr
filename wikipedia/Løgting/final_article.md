# Løgting

The Løgting (pronounced ; ) is the unicameral parliament of the Faroe Islands, an autonomous territory within the Danish Realm.

The name literally means "Law Thing"—that is, a law assembly—and derives from Old Norse lǫgþing, which was a name given to ancient assemblies. A ting or þing has existed on the Faroe Islands for over a millennium and the Løgting was the highest authority on the islands in the Viking era. From 1274 to 1816 it functioned primarily as a judicial body, whereas the modern Løgting established in 1852 is a parliamentary assembly, which gained legislative power when home rule was introduced in 1948. Together with the Manx Tynwald and the Icelandic Alþing, the Løgting is one of the oldest surviving parliaments in the world, all three holding lineages to the old Norse assemblies of mainland Europe.

Today, the Faroe Islands comprise one constituency, and the number of MPs is fixed at 33. The first election with this new system was held on 19 January 2008, after the Election law was changed in late 2007, prior to which the membership of the Løgting varied from 27 to 32. The 7 constituencies had 27 seats and up to 5 supplementary seats. That Election Act came into force in 1978, and the eight general elections between 1978 and 2004 all resulted in 32 members.

The Løgting is elected for a period of four years. Election of the Løgting can take place before the end of an election period if the Løgting agrees on dissolving itself. The Løgmaður (Prime Minister) issues a proclamation of the forthcoming election and appoints the day of election, which must take place, at the earliest, six weeks after the proclamation.

== Name and etymology ==

Løgting is the original name of the Faroese parliament, and its full official name is Føroya løgting.
The Danish name for the parliament is Lagting, a term also used to refer to the parliament of the Åland Islands and in Sweden, Norway, and Denmark.
Lögþing is used in Iceland.

== Composition and Function ==

The settlers established democratic self-government in their Thing, which was formed by free men and met at regular intervals to choose tribal leaders, especially kings.
The general rule was one man, one vote, though deliberations were dominated by the most influential members of the community.
The parliament met twice a year and met outdoors until 1557.
All free men of the archipelago gathered in this assembly to resolve community affairs through their deliberations.
The thing later became a Løgting, which gathers representatives from each local community.
These local assemblies were composed of free men.

== The Løgsøgumaður and Law Recitation ==

The task of the Løgsøgumaður was to announce the passed laws.
The Løgsøgumaður served as a kind of "walking memory" of the Thing.
The Løgsøgumaður had to memorize all decisions and recite them when needed.
The laws had to be memorized by the løgsøgumaðurin.

== Judicial Authority and Punishments ==

The Faroe Saga, an early source, reports that the Faroese Ting passed death sentences in which condemned men were either hanged or beheaded and condemned women were mostly drowned.
The Løgting usually imposed fines that were paid as tribute to the king.

== Christianization and Key Figures ==

It is historically documented that the Faroese Thing adopted Christianity starting in the year 999.
Sigmundur Brestisson was a chieftain who had been baptized in Norway and was able to overcome initial resistance to adopt Christianity.
Sigmundur Brestisson attempted to convince the ting in 999, which was already residing at Tinganes at that time, to introduce Christianity as the main religion.
Tróndur í Gøtu was an opponent of the reform.
The Saga of the Faroe Islands is one of the Icelandic works describing the Christianization of the archipelago and is dated to the turn of the 12th and 13th centuries.

== Demographics and Settlement ==

The surroundings of Tórshavn remained uninhabited as late as the 13th century.
The Faroese population reached a peak of up to 4,000 people during the Viking Age.

The status of the Faroes changed under king Magnus VI of Norway, who introduced the Norwegian Landslog (Land's Law) in 1274.
By this time, The Faroese ting had become an assembly of representatives of the 6 local vártings, with only judicial authority.
This was called lǫgþing in Old Norse, according to the High Courts of Norway.
Its president, the Løgmaður, was the presiding judge, and was, from then on, appointed by the king.
Its members were called Løgrættumenn (approximately translating to "jurymen"), appointed by the King's Provost on the Faroes.

On 24 June 1298 the Faroes gained its first form of constitution, the Seyðabrævið ("Sheep Letter", concerning sheep breeding): the earliest such document the islands know today.

Around 1380, the Faroes, together with Norway, came under the Danish throne, but the islands preserved their special status as former Norwegian territory.
Regardless of these developments, the Løgting preserved a certain influence on the legislature and the administration of the islands until the introduction of the absolute monarchy in 1660 under Frederick III.
From that date the influence and authority of the Løgting had become again reduced, and the institution was finally abolished in 1816.
At the same time, the judicial authority of the Løgting was transferred to other courts, such as the newly inaugurated Court of the Faroes.

== Early Norwegian integration and legal foundations ==

The Faroe Islands were formally part of Norway in 1035, and there was only irregular communication with the government in Trondheim at that time.
Historians believe that older rules of the Løgting were preserved, and these rules were likely modeled after the Gulating Law in Norway.
This situation continued until 1274, when King Magnus Lógbøtari, a Norwegian king, introduced the Landslóg.
The beginning of Norwegian rule on the archipelago made few changes to the system of governance, and the Faroe Islands officially became part of the Norwegian realm in 1280.

== The 1274 Landslóg and transformation of the Løgting ==

The Laws Act reform of 1274 preceded the event where the Althing ceased to function as a legislative assembly after 1280.
The Løgtings exercised only partial legislative functions until that point.
The Landslóg transformed the historical Althing into the current Løgting.
The name changed around 1400.
This status lasted until the final takeover by the Norwegian Crown.

== Judicial structure and personnel ==

The Løgmaður was ultimately a representative of the King from that point on.
The Fúti had the competence of a prosecutor, and the primary responsibility of the Fúti was to ensure the state revenues of the Crown.
The Løgting had a secretary called the Sorinskrivari, who served as the chairman of the Løgting.
The Løgting was the highest court of the Faroe Islands and served as an appellate court for judgments of the Várting.

== Procedures, oaths, and quorum ==

It is believed that the decisions of the Løgting were recorded in writing.
The Løgrættumenn were required to take an oath after the Landslóg, which required them to act according to their own conscience, the laws, and God in all proceedings.
Often only 12 of the 36 Løgrættumenn were present, and in lesser cases, 6 members were sufficient to be quorate.

== The Kalmar Union and later status ==

Queen Margaret I of Denmark united the kingdoms of Denmark, Sweden, and Norway in 1380.
The Faroe Islands were included in this union through a personal union and were considered part of the Norwegian realm within the Kalmar Union.
This status continued after the dissolution of the Kalmar Union during the period when the entities were within Denmark-Norway.
Copenhagen was the capital of the Great Realm.

When Denmark received a free and, for that period, democratic constitution in 1849, this signalled the end of the special status the Faroes had held within the kingdom of Denmark.
This was enacted without consulting the Faroese population.
At that time many of them wished to see the Løgting reinstated, one reason being that they were not satisfied with the situation that the highest Danish government official, called the Amtmaður, was the sole advisory authority on the Faroes on matters of Faroese legislation.
Among those who campaigned for political rights of the Faroes was Niels Winther (1822–1892).
When the Act of 23 March 1852 was passed it meant that the Faroese Løgting was to be reconstituted, although not as a legislative assembly, but as an advisory body, an amtsráð.
The reconstituted Løgting held its first assembly on Ólavsøka in 1852, and thus revived the traditions of the former institution which had been abolished in 1816.

Even though at that time the Løgting had only limited political authority and influence, the institution gradually gained in significance as the representative of the Faroes to the Danish government and parliament (the rigsdag) and as a political forum where politically conscious Faroese islanders could gain experience of politics.
The Løgting became the political platform for the Faroese nationalist movement.
One of the chief objectives behind the demand for political home rule which its supporters put forward was that the Løgting should have legislative powers.

In the assembly established in 1852, the Amtmaður, the highest Danish government official, was ex officio the president of the Løgting.
However, this rule was amended in 1923, so that the president was, from then on, elected by the members of the Løgting.
Since 1927, the Løgting protocols are written in Faroese, and in 1935 the Løgting was authorised to levy taxes.

== Early institutional history and absolutism ==

The Løgting first occupied a permanent building in 1557 on Tinganes, and another Ting house was built later where the Ting met until 1816.
The Danish-Norwegian Crown recognized the Løgting in 1569 as the legitimate representation of the Faroese people.
The introduction of absolutism was the second major break in the history of the Løgting, after which all power subsequently transferred to the King.
The Løgting was expanded to 48 seats, with 8 Løgrættumenn per Syssel, while re-election of members was prohibited and a term limit restricted parliamentarians to one election period.
Historians believe the Løgting became increasingly incompetent, and royal officials gained power as a result.
The Fúti served as the King's representative, and the Sorinskrivari served as the chair of the Thing.
Frederick III of Oldenburg was the reigning monarch in 1660, and Christian V of Denmark issued a document called Norska lóg.

The document Norska lóg defined the competencies of the Faroese parliament, which was composed of 48 representatives, each of whom could serve only one term.
The powers of the Fútir increased at that time.

== The Gabel period and administrative shifts ==

King Christian V bequeathed the Faroe Islands to Christopher von Gabel in 1655, and Christopher von Gabel received the Faroe Islands as a fiefdom.
The Gabel period is considered the darkest period in Faroese history.
The Faroe Islands returned to direct royal rule in 1709.
The Faroe Islands became an administrative unit of Denmark in 1720, and this change had no consequences at the time.
The Faroe Islands were administered as part of the Norwegian colony of Iceland from 1720 to 1776.
After 1776, the Faroe Islands were administered again from Zealand.

== The Treaty of Kiel and dissolution of the union ==

The personal union between Denmark and Norway was dissolved when the Treaty of Kiel was signed in 1814, separating Denmark and Norway from each other.
The Kiel Peace was signed on January 14, 1814.
After the dissolution of the personal union, Greenland, Iceland, and the Faroe Islands came under Danish rule.
The Faroe Islands remained part of Denmark after the separation.

== Abolition of the Løgting and royal administration ==

The last death sentence in the Faroe Islands was executed in 1706.
In 1797, many of its rights were revoked to the Danish crown.
The Løgmaður was abolished in 1816, while the Sorínskrivari was retained and became the sole legal authority.
The Faroe Islands no longer belonged to the Danish district of Zealand.
The Amtmaður decided which Danish laws applied to the Faroe Islands and exercised power on behalf of the king.
The Løgting was abolished two years later.
The Governor was responsible for determining Danish laws for the Faroe Islands.
Since that time, the king appointed an administrator.

== The 1849 constitution and loss of autonomy ==

The March Revolution of 1848 took place in Denmark.
The Folketing was established as the parliament.
Any autonomy was abolished in 1849.
Denmark became a parliamentary monarchy in 1849.
Royal administrators took full power of the former Løgting.

== Campaign for reinstatement and the 1852 Act ==

The Faroese people began to fight to restore the old order under the leadership of Niels Winther.
One such attempt occurred in 1844 and involved 18 members.

== Composition and leadership of the reconstituted Løgting ==

The reconstituted Løgting consisted of 18 members, including the Próstur, who was the highest authority of the Faroese People's Church at that time.
Carl Emil Dahlerup served as the first chairman until 1861.

Members of the Løgting were no longer as experienced [cite: 5].

The lack of experience increased the influence of Danish officials [cite: 5].
The Løgting had 18 elected members, two of whom were the Amtmaður and the Próstur.
The number of members in the Løgting was increased to 22 in 1906 [cite: 5].

== Reforms in the 20th century ==

The first change was introduced in 1906, when the number of people sitting in the parliament was increased to 22.
In 1923, the number of seats was changed to 20, and there was the possibility of filling an additional 10 seats.
In 1923, the High Court Judge lost his seat in the parliament [cite: 5], and the Próstur lost his seat.
The Governor and the Court Preacher had a seat in the parliament [cite: 5].
In 1923, the Amtmaður did not automatically become the Løgmaður despite remaining a member of the Løgting, and the Amtmaður was elected from among the members of the assembly.
The position of Amtmaður (Amtmann) has existed since then and existed until 1948.
Since the adoption of the constitution, two Faroese representatives have always been represented there.
Since that change, all documents issued by the Faroese parliament are written in the Faroese language.
The Faroe Islands Court operated on a similar basis to the Løgting.

During World War II, when the Faroes were occupied by the United Kingdom on 12 April 1940 and relations with Denmark were interrupted, the Løgting actually functioned as a legislative assembly, and the Faroes had their own government consisting of the Amtmaður Carl Aage Hilbert and ministers appointed by the Løgting.
Denmark was occupied by Germany during World War II in an operation named Operation Weserübung.
The UK acted to preempt Germany, and the occupation of the Faroes took place three days after Germany's actions.
The Allies could not allow the strategic point in the Atlantic to be occupied, so they occupied the archipelago through a peaceful occupation.
The British were not perceived as unwelcome intruders by the Faroese, nor were they perceived as new masters.
The Løgting gained full power on the archipelago, and this period lasted until 1945.
In this period, the Faroese proved able to govern themselves.
The Faroese developed concrete demands for independence of their islands from Denmark or for more rights within the kingdom.
At the end of the war, the independence movement (mainly under influence of the new Fólkaflokkurin) was so powerful that none of the political parties were willing to return to the pre-war situation where the status of the Faroes had practically been that of a Danish county (Færøernes amt).
The occupation ended on September 16, 1945.
A constitutional crisis occurred.
Jákup í Jákupsstovu, a social democratic dissenter, declared the independence of the Faroe Islands on September 18.
The Fólkaflokkurin party declared the independence of the Faroe Islands.
There was a dispute regarding the nature of the vote, which concerned whether the vote was consultative or constitutive.
King Christian X dissolved the Faroe Islands parliament.
Denmark annulled the declaration of independence on September 20.
In 1946, the Lagman Thorstein Petersen declared the Faroe Islands free.

Long and laborious negotiations followed between the Danish government and the representatives of the Løgting.
After the war, the Faroese began to act more actively in pursuit of expanding their autonomy.
The Faroese people made repeated requests.
A plebiscite was decided to be held shortly after the war.
The Faroe Islands held a plebiscite.
The authorities of Denmark agreed to let the Faroe Islands decide their own fate.
Finally a public vote was held on 14 September 1946 where the electorate was to choose between a Danish proposition of Home rule and full secession from Denmark.
The result of the referendum was ambiguous.
This election is not considered a referendum, as the parliament was not bound to follow the decision of the vote.
The result was a marginal majority of 161 votes for secession from Denmark (48.7% in favour, 47.2% against, 4.1% blank or spoilt).
The republican coalition majority in parliament interpreted the results as a resolve by the Faroese people for full Faroese independence from Denmark and started the process of secession as well as establishing proper governing bodies for an independent Faroese nation.
The Government of Denmark contested the legality of this process, and on 25 September the King of Denmark signed a document dissolving the Faroese parliament and a new election was held a few months later.
The Løgting elections took place in November 1946.
This election resulted in a significant majority of 2,000 votes for the parties favoring a union with Denmark, and a new unionist coalition was formed.
Based on their growth in votes they chose not to pass the secession, but as a compromise, the Home Rule Act was constituted and came into force on 1 April 1948.
Negotiations for the Autonomy Act followed the elections.
The Autonomy Act was signed on March 31, 1948.
The act actually became legally effective on April 3.
However, the first date is conventionally cited.
The principle of self-determination was put into effect on April 1, 1948.
As it was then, this election is still shrouded in controversy today, and there exist two popular stances in this discussion.
On one hand, some people argue that there was a resolve in favor of independence, as there actually was a factual majority for secession, even if it was a small one.
On the other hand, other people argue that the majority was far too small, as there were in fact only 161 more votes for independence, and this side specifically argues that only a qualified majority can be large enough grounds to pass a vote of such social and political consequence.
Under the Home Rule Act, the Faroese Løgting is the legislative authority in special Faroese matters, defined as særanliggender, while other areas are administered by the Danish national authorities as common matters, fællesanliggender.
Initially, the Løgting gained only a majority of legislative powers on the archipelago.
The amount of competencies increased over time.
An example of this influence is the rejection of EU membership in a public referendum.
The Faroe Islands parliament gained the right to appoint its own administration.
The election of the Faroese government occurs without external interference.
This authority is defined in the Self-Government Act of April 1, 1948.
The scope of the concept was not specified since 1948.
With the passing of a new statute in 1995, parliamentarism was legally adopted and at the same time the structure and functions of the Løgting were modernised.
The principle of self-determination is supported by an act from 1994.
The Principle of Self-Determination states that legislative power in the Faroe Islands shall be exercised by 32 members of parliament, the prime minister, and the government.
A proper Faroese Constitution is planned and underway.

After Home Rule had come into force the parliamentary work of the Løgting changed fundamentally.
Before Home Rule the Faroese Parliament had only been a consultative body, whereas now the Faroese Parliament has legislative power within all the branches taken over from the Danish Parliament (Det danske Folketing).
According to the Home Rule Act the various branches of legislative power are divided into an A-sector and a B-sector.
The several branches within the A-sector can be taken over by the Løgting, if either the Løgting or the Danish Government so wish.
The branches within the B-sector can only be transferred to the Løgting if the Faroese Government (Føroya Landsstýri) and the Danish Government agree on the terms.

Matters regarding defence and foreign policy are outside the scope of Home Rule.
The Danish Folketing has legislative power in all areas except those which have been taken over by the Løgting.
The Faroese have two seats in the Danish Folketing.
Within the framework of Home Rule the Løgting provides for constitutional affairs and for the order of business.

According to the Faroese Home Rule Act the organization of internal affairs is solely within the province of the Faroese Parliament.
An act concerning this matter was passed on 26 July 1994.
According to section No. 1. of this act the division of legal power concerning matters taken over by the Home Rule is now shared jointly between the Faroese Parliament and the Prime Minister, executive power rests with the Government whereas judicial power in such matters rests with the Danish courts.
The Parliament is elected for a period of four years, and the maximum membership is 32 members who are elected in public, secret, and direct elections.
The government consists of the Prime Minister (løgmaður) and not fewer than two ministers (landsstýrismenn).

The Prime Minister is appointed indirectly by the Parliament.
The Chairman of the Parliament after having had talks with the party leaders submits a proposal for a new Prime Minister, a vote is taken, and if a majority of the members reject the candidate then the proposal is rejected, otherwise the candidate is accepted.
The Prime Minister appoints the ministers.
Neither the Prime Minister nor a minister may hold their seats if a vote no confidence is put forward and 17 MPs are opposed.
The Prime Minister has at any time the power to call an election.
The Prime Minister and the ministers are not permitted to hold seats in the Parliament.

== Legal framework and sector division ==

The responsibilities of the Parliament are regulated by several documents, including the Autonomy Statute of 31 March 1948, the Fámjin Treaty of 29 March 2005, and the Takeover Act of 4 April 2005.
The A-sector includes all areas of responsibility that the Løgting can assume, while the B-sector includes all matters delegated jointly by the Faroese and Danish governments to the Løgting.
A bilateral agreement called the Fámjin Treaty was signed in 2005, which expanded the competencies of the Løgting significantly and significantly enhanced the position of the Løgting.
The Self-Government Act granted the Faroese Parliament authority in several matters related to Färöer internal politics.

== Reserved powers and constitutional limits ==

The Løgting is generally not responsible for justice, as matters of national defense, justice, and foreign policy are the responsibility of the Kingdom of Denmark.
According to Article 1 of the Autonomy Statute, the Løgting has no interference in the constitution of the Danish State, Faroese citizenship and nationality, the Supreme Court of the Danish State, or monetary and exchange rate policy.
Laws introduced by the Folketing do not take effect immediately in the Faroe Islands if Faroese politicians decide that a given act is sent to the Løgting.

== Transferred responsibilities ==

Culture, prison administration, family matters, healthcare, the environment, education, and research are responsibility areas of the Faroe Islands.
Economic policy is a central task of the Løgting, and the economic policy of the Faroe Islands is primarily determined by their non-membership in the EU.
The Faroe Islands coordinate their negotiations with the EU and always consult the Danish government during these negotiations.

== Government formation and parliamentary oversight ==

The Løgting elects the government of the Faroe Islands.
Members of the government do not have voting rights in the Løgting, and a substitute from the party list represents them during this period.
Any member of the Løgting can question the government and hold it accountable whether in the plenary session or in committees.
A member of the Løgting can request accountability, submit written questions, or submit oral questions.
Questions can be directed to any member of the Faroese government or to the royal commissioner.
The objective of this procedure is to obtain as much information as possible to make a political decision, and the procedure includes the drafting of a conclusion.
The Commissioner of the Realm has no right to vote.

== Legislative initiative and forms ==

A motion must be made to initiate the legislative procedure, and this motion can be made by the Prime Minister, a member of the government, or a member of the Løgting.
Legislative initiative can take five different forms, including submitting a bill draft by one of the MPs, decisions of the Danish Parliament that directly concern the Faroe Islands, parliamentary resolutions, written motions, oral questions, unprepared questions, and a vote of no confidence.
Legislative work on a bill begins upon the submission of a proposal for its enactment, at which point the initiation of the process is officially announced by the chairperson.
The first reading does not involve any discussion on the content of the proposed bill.

== Legislative procedure and readings ==

The bill proposal enters the parliament and is debated during the readings, during which amendments can be made.
The legislative process involves two to three readings, beginning with a debate in general terms during the first reading.
Every member of parliament may keep the draft for one day to examine it more deeply, so something can happen with the draft no earlier than the second working day, at which time the first reading begins.
After the first reading, the chairman submits the draft bill to a ministry or ministries of their choice, which are tasked with providing suggestions and amendments.
A special report is prepared from the work of the ministers, and the commission submits a report to the plenary.
The President of the Løgting refers the proposal to the corresponding committee, after which a second reading takes place.
During the second reading, all proposed amendments are considered and all issues presented in the proposal are discussed in detail.
The first vote occurs, and if a proposal is defeated, it is discarded.
If a proposal is not defeated, it proceeds to a third reading, which includes respective debates and voting.

The report describes the changes proposed by the ministers in the bill.
Bills are usually returned to ministers between the first and second reading.
In such cases, the bill is sent back to the relevant ministries after the second reading, where the ministries review the content of the bill again more carefully.
The bill then returns to parliament.
The second reading can take place no earlier than the third day after the first reading and cannot take place earlier than two days after an amendment is introduced by ministers.
Each member of parliament has the right to submit their own amendment until the end of the ongoing debate.
The government as a whole can make decisions about changes until the end of the parliamentary debate.

== Royal assent and veto power ==

Laws passed by the Løgting must be confirmed by the Prime Minister, a requirement for the laws to come into effect.
The proposal is sent to the prime minister, who confirms it as a parliamentary act and publishes the act in the Faroese official gazette, after which the law comes into force.
The Prime Minister has the power of veto over legislative proposals but rarely exercises this veto power.
Exercising the veto can result in a motion of censure by the Løgting, and a successful motion of censure would remove the Prime Minister from office.

== Parliamentary resolutions and recommendations ==

The Løgting has the authority to issue a recommendation to the Danish parliament (Folketing) when the Folketing passes laws concerning the Faroe Islands.
The decision of the Løgting is not published and does not come into force.
This procedure is similar to that used for Faroese law proposals.
The Prime Minister is responsible for informing the kingdom's commissioner.
Parliamentary resolution proposals are discussed in only two readings, and after the second reading, the proposals are voted on in the plenary session.
Parliamentary resolutions are not laws, are not proclaimed by the prime minister, and have no effect on the government or citizens.
In some cases, the Autonomy Act stipulates that resolutions must pass through the Løgting before the Faroese government concludes important treaties with other countries.

== Censure motions and accountability ==

Censure motions involve a reading, a debate, and a vote.
These steps occur after a vote of no confidence against the prime minister or a member of their government.

Laws are published in Kunngerðablaðnum.

The act was published in the Official Gazette (Kunngerð) number 128 on December 19, 2003.
A proposal that has been raised generally requires three deliberations to be adopted.

The Løgting has 7 standing committees which in accordance with the order of business of the Faroese Parliament are elected for the duration of the election period unless the members of the Parliament agree on electing the committees anew.
Every member of the Løgting belongs to one of the 7 permanent committees.
Members can be reassigned to a different committee if the plenary decides to redistribute them.
Representatives are assigned a mandate for this period.
There is no debate in committees.
There is no voting in committees.
The composition of the councils is proportional to the seats occupied in parliament.
Standing Committees: The Finance Committee.
As provided by section 44, subsection 2 of the Home Rule Act, the committee grants supplementary approval and in addition it makes recommendations to the Faroese Parliament on matters of finances, economy, taxes, and duties.
Committee on Foreign Affairs.
As provided by section 54 of the Home Rule Act the committee makes recommendations to the Faroese Government on foreign affairs, trade matters, and defence matters, and in addition recommendations on relations with Denmark.
Committee on Fisheries and Industry.
Its tasks are to make recommendations to the Parliament on fisheries matters, shipping matters, matters concerning the fishing industry, industrial matters, matters concerning fish farming, agricultural matters, matters concerning the environment, matters concerning communication and transport, matters concerning energy and oil industry, trade matters and furthermore matters concerning commercial companies and registration matters etc.
The Welfare Committee.
Its tasks are to make recommendations to the Parliament on social matters, matters concerning the labour market, and matters concerning housing.
Committee on Judicial Affairs.
Its tasks are to make recommendations to the Parliament on judicial and municipal matters.
Committee on Governmental Affairs.
As provided by section 38 of the Home Rule Act the committee's task is to have judicial supervision with the Prime Minister and the ministers and to see to it that they observe the rules of law.
The committee has authority to summon the Prime Minister or ministers to explain in detail items concerning any political question.
One committee deals with Culture.
There is a Culture Commission.
One committee deals with the Nordic Council.
Two delegates to the Nordic Council are elected by the Løgting.
One committee deals with Prisoner Care.
The council advises on petrochemical industry.
The control committee is an exception to the standard size.
The control committee consists of only 3 members.

== Legislative process ==

The government can make these decisions even after the second reading has begun.
If the project is approved, it undergoes a third reading, which may take place on the third working day after the second reading.
This timeframe can be extended if the ministry decides to introduce its own amendments.
Amendments are allowed when the proceedings begin during the third reading.
The debate ends if the bill is not passed.
When the final vote concludes, the Prime Minister of the Faroe Islands must inform the royal administrator of the result.
The Prime Minister of the Faroe Islands signs the law to approve it, which is the final step to recognize a law as legally binding.
The Prime Minister has the right to veto a bill.
If the Prime Minister vetoes a bill, the parliament can vote a motion of no confidence against him.
After approval, the law is published in an official newspaper.

The parliament is typically split into four main parties, each of which typically get around 20% in elections: the conservative Union Party, the Social Democratic Party, the liberal-independentist People's Party, and the democratic socialist Republic, who each take a unique position on left/right and independence/unionism axis.
In addition, there are three smaller parties with representation: the classical liberal Progress, the liberal New Self-Government, and the Christian conservative Centre party.

At the elections each party has a certain letter, which is also used on posters for the campaigns.

== Electoral system and mechanics ==

The first two political parties were founded in 1906.
Deputies are elected by popular vote.
Seats are allocated using a proportional representation system, a multi-member list voting method, and the D'Hondt method.
The electoral threshold is one-thirtieth of the valid votes cast, which is approximately 3.03%.
All parties that meet the electoral threshold are eligible for seats.

== Party list letters and designations ==

The People's Party is assigned the letter A, while the Alliance Party is represented by B and the Social Democratic Party by C.
Sjálvstýri has the list letter D, which also represents the Autonomy Party, and the letter E represents the Republican Party.
The Progress Party is represented by F, the Centre Party by G, and the Workers' Union by H.
The letter I represents the Business Party (1936), the Christian People's Party, and the Faroe Islands Progress and Fishing Party (1958–1988).
J represents the Separatist Party, K represents the Unaffiliated, and L represents the Progress Party (1988) and the Students' Party (2008).
M represents the Free Union, N represents the Social Separatist Party, and O represents the Faroese Party.
For example, the People's Party (Partia Ludowa) is assigned the letter A.

== Historical development of parties ==

Parliamentary elections in the Faroe Islands took place before 1906, and all participants in these pre-1906 elections were non-partisan.
In 1906, the National Movement entered its political phase, and the first two political parties were Sambandsflokkurin and Sjálvstýrisflokkurin.
Elections in the Faroe Islands became party-based after the formation of the first two political parties.
Javnaðarflokkurin joined in 1928, Fólkaflokkurin joined in 1940, Tjóðveldisflokkurin joined in 1950, and Framburðsflokkurin joined in 1958.
Framburðsflokkurin existed until 1998, and Miðflokkurin joined in 1994.
Sjálvstýri was previously known as Sjálvstýrisflokkurin until 2015.
Sjálvstýrisflokkurin, Tjóðveldisflokkurin, Javnaðarflokkurin, and Sambandsflokkurin are current parties in the Løgting.

== Parliamentary composition and seat allocation ==

Sjálvstýri is a self-governance party.
The number of seats was increased in 1906 and raised to 22.
The change was introduced by the Danish Lagting Act, which was passed on April 11, 1923.
There were 20 parliamentary seats and the Próstur lost his seat.
There were up to 10 appointed seats and this division has persisted until today.
The parliament elected in 2015 had two largest parties, with the Social Democratic Party as the largest and the Republican Party as the second largest.

== Electoral system ==

Eligible voters for the Løgting are Faroese citizens who must be at least 18 years old to vote.
All citizens have the right to be elected, and the right to vote and be elected is granted to all citizens who have reached the age of 18.

== Government formation and composition ==

A bourgeois government was formed based on election results by three parties: the Union Party, the People's Party, and the Centre Party.
Bárður á Steig Nielsen, a member of the Unionists, serves as Prime Minister.
The ABH government has seven ministries, with the Union Party and the People's Party each receiving three and the Centre Party receiving one.

== Government crisis and dissolution ==

The Centre Party left the government in October 2022, and Jenis av Rana, the foreign minister representing the Centre Party, was dismissed from his position due to his negative comments about Søren Pape Poulsen concerning the possibility of Søren Pape Poulsen becoming Prime Minister of Denmark.
The government lost its majority after the Centre Party left and announced early elections on December 8, 2022.
The Union Party increased its support in the most recent local parliament elections, and the People's Party increased its support in the most recent local parliament elections.

== Government formation ==

Kaj Leo Johannesen, a member of the Union Party (Sambandsflokkurin), assumed the position of head of government.
The center-right coalition government included the People's Party (Fólkaflokkurin), the Union Party (Sambandsflokkurin), the Autonomy Party (Sjálvstýrisflokkurin), and the Centre Party (Miðflokkurin).

The first meeting of the Løgting is on Saint Olaf's Day (ólavsøka).
On 29 July the members of the Løgting, the ministers (landsstýrismenn), the High Commissioner of Denmark (ríkisumboðsmaður), and high officials walk in procession (skrúðgonga) from the Parliament building to the cathedral (Havnar kirkja).
After the service the procession returns to Parliament House, and the Løgting is opened.

At the first meeting the Prime Minister (Løgmaður) delivers his Saint Olaf's Day address, in which he gives a general description of the state of the nation.
The Løgting has one major parliamentary debate concerning the state of the nation.
The debate is about Løgmaður's Saint Olaf's Address, and the budget.

As a rule the Løgting debates between 100 and 150 various items in one session.

This Ólavsøka tradition is very old and dates back to the time of the Norwegian rule.
In these former times was the Løgting only held one time the year starting with Ólavsøka and sitting 8 days from 6 in the morning to 3 in the afternoon, with church service each day, and all priests of the Faroes attendant.
In the 17th century this was a bit modified - now only meeting with all priests at Ólavsøka day.

== The President of the Løgting ==

The President of the Løgting presides over its sessions, ensures that constitutional provisions are fulfilled, ensures that relevant rules and procedures within the Løgting are followed, acts as the representative of the Løgting, and forms the board of directors.
All of them are elected from the plenary assembly of the Løgting, and the election takes place immediately at the start of the legislative term.
The president can participate in debates during sessions, but to do so must temporarily give up their seat to one of the vice presidents.
The Speaker presides over the speeches in the Parliament [cite: 4].
The head of the legislative body is the Chairman, and the current Chairman is Jógvan á Lakjuni, who belongs to the People's Party.

The protocols of the Løgting assemblies and its other archives from 1852 up to the present are kept at the Faroese National Archive in Tórshavn.
The Løgting's protocols from 1615 to 1816 are also preserved at the National Archive.
The total archives of the Løgting contain the most important sources of Faroese history.
Thus the Faroese Løgting is a parliament with an exceptionally well-documented history, where the archives in fact cover the period right from 1298 to the present.
There are only a very few parliaments in Europe with archives preserved to the same extent where the records are continuous both through time and in their contents.

Together with the other texts in the Kongsbókin, the Statute concerning sheep breeding on the Faroes (Seyðabrævið) contains information on conditions in society, the economy, the language, culture and cultural history.
The Løgting's archives from 1615 to 1816 contain similar rich sources of material on all aspects of the history of the Faroes in that period.
The Løgting's archives for the period from 1852 to the present also provide the most important source of information on the more recent and latest political history of the Faroes.
The Faroese cultural heritage is founded on this abundance of source material, which is thus at the very heart of the Faroese identity and sense of history right from the landnam period to the present time.

== Location and building ==

The headquarters is the House of the Løgting (Løgtingshúsið), which is a wooden house built in 1856 under Governor Carl Emil Dahlerup.
The House of the Løgting is located in the center of Tórshavn, north of the original meeting place, and the Løgting has moved slightly further into the new city center.
A modern building next to the House of the Løgting constitutes the parliament offices, and there is a significantly larger annex in modern style.
Tinganes peninsula is in the center of Tórshavn, where the national government is currently seated.
Tórshavn is the administrative capital of the islands and developed into a trading and capital city due to the centrally located Thingstätte, despite having a relatively unfavorable natural harbor.
Lagtinget received a building in 1690.

The building was located in Gongin and was poorly constructed, resulting in a roof collapse in 1719.
A new tinghus (parliament building) was completed in 1722, and the Lagting moved in 1759.
The building is known as Tinghúsbygningurin and is located at Tinghúsvegi 1–3.
The annex is known as Gamla apoteki.

== Youth Parliament ==

The Ungdómsting, also known as the Youth Parliament, is organized every other year in February [cite: 9].
The first Ungdómsting began in 2006 [cite: 9].
It aims to engage young people in politics and educates them about legal processes, the work of parliament, and the importance of democracy [cite: 9].
The event is intended for children in the 8th to 10th grades of elementary school, which corresponds to children aged between 15 and 17 years old [cite: 9].
Participants carry out various projects that simulate the actual work of the Løgting [cite: 9].
