# Michel Monet

Michel Monet (17 March 1878 – 3 February 1966) was the second son of Claude Monet and Camille Doncieux Monet.

Born on 17 March 1878, 26 rue d'Édimbourg, in the 8th arrondissement of Paris, where the Monets had moved from Argenteuil, Michel Monet was the younger of Claude and Camille Monet's two sons.
His mother was ill with cancer at the time of his birth, and her already failing health worsened after his birth and she died on 5 September 1879, probably of uterine cancer.
Michel's elder brother, Jean, was born in 1867.
The parents were a couple since 1866 and got married in 1870.
The father was a painter who could earn very little money with his painting, and the family lived in very financially strained circumstances.
Since 1877, year of Ernest Hoschedé's bankruptcy, Alice Hoschedé and her six children had been living with the Monets.
The two families moved from Paris to Vétheuil in August 1878, a move caused by financial constraints, and after Camille's death in 1879, Monet, Alice and the eight children continued living together.
In 1881, they moved to Poissy and in April 1883 to Giverny.
Alice managed the household and supervised the education of the Monet and Hoschedé children.

== Family background and relationships ==

Ernest Hoschedé was a textile merchant who frequently traveled between Vétheuil and Paris, though he visited rarely after the move.
Alice Hoschedé took care of Camille Monet while she and Claude Monet lived together as an unmarried couple.
Ernest Hoschedé died in 1891, and Claude Monet and Alice Hoschedé married the year after his death.
Blanche Hoschedé was Jean Monet's wife, and they married in 1897.
Alice died in 1911, followed by the death of Jean Monet in 1914.
She moved back to the Monet house in Giverny after Alice and Jean Monet died and took care of the elderly Claude Monet.

== Travels and interests ==

In 1904, Claude Monet traveled to Madrid with his wife Alice and their son Michel using their own car, covering the first 800 kilometers of the journey in three days to reach Biarritz.
Michel Monet traveled to Africa repeatedly in the 1930s, where he spent a large part of his life hunting and brought back trophies and films from his travels.
The souvenirs from these travels included ivory, weapons, and masks, and he collected African masks.
Michel Jacques Monet was a French art collector.

Michel Monet married Gabrielle Bonaventure, a former model of his father, in 1927.
The couple had no children.
Gabrielle Bonaventure lived from 1890 to 1964.
She died of stomach cancer on 2 February 1964.
His wife's grave is located in Giverny.
He visited his wife's grave every week.

== Residences ==

He built a house for himself in Sorel-Moussel, which is approximately 40 kilometers away from the previous location.
He has a home in Paris on Boulevard Brune and a residence named 'Les Blondeaux' in Sorel-Moussel, Eure-et-Loir.
He purchased a plot of land in 1929.

When Claude Monet died in 1926, as his sole heir, Michel inherited the entire estate.
Michel never spent time in Giverny and Blanche Hoschedé Monet, his stepsister and sister-in-law, became the caretaker of the house and garden until her death in 1947.
Louis Lebret, who had been Monet's head gardener, stayed on to help Blanche.
After her death the garden was neglected.

== Art collection and legacy ==

He wrote a last will and testament, and in total, he had written 12 wills.
He stipulated that the art collection be exhibited at the Musée Marmottan in Paris.
The executor of the will visited Michel Monet's house, where representatives of the Académie des Beaux-Arts were present.
They discovered an unexpectedly large art treasure in the house, a discovery reported by the French newsreel.
They discovered additional artworks in the father's house, including more than 80 paintings by his father, drawings, sketchbooks, photographs, letters, personal items, and works by friends of the artist.
Pierre-Auguste Renoir, Gustave Caillebotte, Paul Signac, and Eugène Boudin were friends of the artist.

Claude Monet's house in Giverny is accessible to the public as a museum and has been open to the public since 1980.
This naming is due to a bequest.
A French newsreel reported on the discovery of Michel Monet's legacy in the archives of the Institut national de l'audiovisuel.

== Speculation regarding an illegitimate daughter ==

Speculation regarding an illegitimate daughter of Michel Monet arose in 2017 and was triggered by an auction at Christie's auction house in Hong Kong that included individual artworks by Claude Monet and personal items belonging to the artist.
The objects originated from the estate of Rolande Verneige, who died in 2008.
Monet never acknowledged such a paternity, and the deceased woman did not seek to establish this paternity.
The Fondation Monet held a specific view on the matter, stating that Rolande Verneige's mother was merely a friend of Michel Monet's wife and obtained possession of the items through this relationship.
