# Peoples Partnership

The People's Partnership (PP) was a political coalition in Trinidad and Tobago among five political parties: the United National Congress (UNC), the Congress of the People (COP), the Tobago Organization of the People (TOP), Movement for Social Justice (MSJ) and National Joint Action Committee (NJAC). The political leader was Kamla Persad-Bissessar. The coalition was formed in advance of the 2010 general election attempting to form a multi-ethnic opposition bloc against the People's National Movement (PNM) government led by Patrick Manning. The coalition won the 2010 General Elections defeating the People's National Movement on May 24, 2010. On September 7, 2015, the coalition was defeated in the 2015 General Elections to the People's National Movement led by Keith Rowley. The coalition saw the departure of the Movement for Social Justice in 2012 and eventually disbanded on .

== Political background and electoral system ==

Trinidad has a two-party system where smaller political parties play a subordinate role.
The political system in Trinidad is based on the US model, while the electoral system is based on the British system.
The country is divided into electoral districts where one seat per party is determined in each district using a plurality voting system.
The party with the most seats forms the government.
A consequence of the majority voting system is that parties do not receive a parliamentary seat even if parties win many votes nationwide if parties fail to win a majority in a constituency.
Electoral districts with narrow results in past elections receive increased attention, and ten of these constituencies were considered competitive.
Traditionally, the PNM is associated with the African-descended population of the country, while the UNC is associated with the Indian-descended population of the country.
The People's Partnership had a social-democratic character and a progressive character.
The coalition had a left-centrist orientation.

== Member parties ==

The party was founded in 1989 and its leading figures are traditionally of Indian descent.
The COP split from the UNC in 2006 and represents similar positions to the UNC.
Winston Dookeran and Carolyn Seepersad-Bachan won their electoral districts after leaving the UNC.
The MSJ is a socialist-oriented party that is close to trade unions, such as the Oilfields Workers' Trade Union, and was founded shortly before the 2010 elections.
The NJAC was founded in 1969 as part of the Trinidadian Black Power movement.
The TOP party emerged from the Democratic Action Congress (DAC), operates exclusively in Tobago, and advocates for extensive autonomy for the island.
The party won four of the twelve seats in the 2009 regional elections for the Tobago House of Assembly (THA), which is a partially autonomous parliament for Tobago, while the other eight seats were won by the PNM.
The TOP advocated for an expansion of Tobago's autonomy.

== Pre-2010 political context ==

The PNM won the 2002 and 2007 elections, while the UNC lost both contests to the PNM.
Following these victories, the PNM formed the government with Patrick Manning serving as Prime Minister.
In early 2010, this government faced criticism and allegations of corruption and mismanagement.
Manning asked President George Maxwell Richards to dissolve Parliament, and the opposition announced a motion of no confidence.
Parliament was dissolved on April 8, 2010, when the legislative term was only about halfway through.
Manning set the date for a new election on April 16, scheduling it for May 24.
In the 1995 elections, the PNM won an equal number of seats to the UNC, allowing the UNC to form the government after forming an alliance with the National Alliance for Reconstruction (NAR).

The NAR was a small party and won two seats.
Minor parties received increased attention in the subsequent elections.
The victory ended nine years of government by Patrick Manning.

== 2010 general election ==

The People's Partnership won the 2010 general elections by a wide margin [16,17,18].
The Popular Alliance won the election by a landslide and received 60.03% of the votes.
The coalition won 21 constituencies, the UNC won 21 constituencies, the COP won 6 constituencies, and the TOP won 2 constituencies.
The UNC presented 25 candidates and won 21 out of the 25 seats it contested.
The COP won only 6 out of 14 seats.
There were two Tobagonian constituencies, the TOP contested the two seats on Tobago island, and the TOP party was successful in both Tobagonian constituencies.
The PNM achieved 40% of the votes and won only 29% of the seats.
This discrepancy was a result of the plurality voting system.
The UNC secured an absolute majority for itself.
Since the elections, the United National Congress (UNC) had marked hegemony within the alliance.

== Government formation and cabinet ==

The cabinet originally consisted of 29 ministers, including five from the COP party and one from the TOP party.
Winston Dookeran, Errol MacLeod, Carolyn Seepersad-Bachan, and Jack Warner were members of the Cabinet.
Winston Dookeran served as the Finance Minister, while Errol MacLeod served as the Labour Minister and ran for election as a candidate for the UNC.
Carolyn Seepersad-Bachan represented the COP party and served as the Energy Minister.
The Tobago Development Ministry was led by Minister Vernella Alleyne-Toppin, who belonged to the TOP party.
The ministry drafted a bill to expand Tobago's autonomy but did not submit the bill for ratification in parliament.
David Abdulah is also the chairman of the Oilfield Workers Trade Union (OWTU).
Persad-Bissessar restructured the cabinet, expanding it from 29 to 34 positions.

== Tobago autonomy and regional elections ==

Expanding Tobago's autonomy was part of the People's Partnership election platform.
At that time, the TOP held both Tobago seats in the national parliament and four of the twelve constituencies on Tobago.
The TOP did not win a single constituency in the regional elections, while the PNM won a landslide victory in the regional elections.
The TOP fell into political insignificance as a result.
Jack Warner was a controversial minister prior to his resignation and resigned following massive corruption allegations.
The COP lost both mandates in the regional elections and had led the local authorities of San Fernando and Diego Martin until then.
Regional elections were held in Trinidad in October of the same year.

On January 24, 2010, Kamla Persad-Bissessar was elected political leader of the UNC, emerging victorious over the party's founder, Basdeo Panday.
She was formally appointed opposition leader on February 25, 2010.

On April 21, 2010 at Charlie King Junction, Fyzabad, a "Unity Accord" also known as the "Fyzabad Declaration" was signed by the UNC political leader, Kamla Persad-Bissessar, COP political leader Winston Dookeran, NJAC chief servant Makandal Daaga, TOP political leader Ashworth Jack, and president of the Movement for Social Justice, Errol McLeod.
The Fyzabad Declaration was an agreement to run together as a 'People's Partnership' for the upcoming election.
The formation of the coalition was partly justified by the desire to overcome racial barriers in Trinidad.
The UNC was the clearly dominant party of the People's Partnership Coalition.

On May 24, 2010, the People's Partnership led by Kamla Persad-Bissessar defeated the People's National Movement by securing 29 out of 41 seats in the House of Representatives.
Persad-Bissessar was sworn in as the first female prime minister by President George Maxwell Richards, where Anand Ramlogan was sworn in as Attorney General on May 26, 2010.
Shortly thereafter, the People's Partnership won the 2010 local elections on July 26, 2010, securing a majority in the local corporations.
