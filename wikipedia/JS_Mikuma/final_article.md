# JS Mikuma

JS Mikuma (FFM-4) is the fourth ship of the s of the Japan Maritime Self-Defense Force (JMSDF). She was named after the Mikuma River and shares her name with a World War II heavy cruiser Mikuma and Cold War destroyer escort Mikuma.

In 2015 the Japanese defense budget allocated funds to study the construction of a new "compact-type hull destroyer with additional multi-functional capabilities" as well as a new radar system for the destroyer.
In the same year Mitsubishi Heavy Industries (MHI) unveiled the frigate's first concept model (30FF) which they have been developing with their own funds.
The warship was ordered as part of the Reiwa 1 fiscal year plan under the Medium-Term Defense Force Development Plan.
A crew system was being introduced.
The chief engineer officer had not been appointed yet.
The 30DX design has an overall length of 133 m, breadth of 16 m, a standard displacement of 3900 tons with a full load displacement of about 5500 tons, and a maximum speed of over 30 knots.
Weapons include a Mk 45 gun, two remote weapon station above the bridge, 16 Mk 41 VLS at the bow, 8 anti-ship missiles, one SeaRAM, an SH-60Lhelicopter, torpedoes, and decoy launchers.
It can also deploy and recover UUV, USV, and sea mines from the rear ramp beneath the helideck.
It is also expected to use a naval version of the Type 03 Chū-SAM.

Mikuma was laid down on 15 July 2020 at Mitsubishi Heavy Industries, Nagasaki and launched on 10 December 2021.
She was commissioned in 7 March 2023.

== Construction and commissioning ==

The warship was ordered from Mitsubishi Heavy Industries, and the keel laying ceremony occurred at the Mitsubishi Heavy Industries Nagasaki Shipyard.
Mitsubishi Heavy Industries held a naming ceremony, a launching ceremony, and a press conference regarding these events for the vessel 'Mikuma'.
The ship underwent outfitting work and sea trials.

== Operational assignment ==

The ship was assigned to the 13th Destroyer Squadron under direct command of the Maritime Self-Defense Force.

== Joint US-Japan training exercise ==

A joint US-Japan training exercise was conducted from September 25 to October 3 of the same year.
The exercise area included sea and air spaces west of Kyushu, off Shikoku, and Suruga Bay, as well as the Numazu Beach Training Ground.
The transport ship Shimokita, the minesweepers Yakushima and Takashima, MCH-101, and US Navy LCACs participated in the exercise.
The exercise was a special transport training that included cross-deck operations using LCACs, beaching training, search and rescue training, LCAC maintenance training, and PHOTOEX activities.

== Osprey crash response deployment ==

The voluntary dispatch by Japan occurred on November 29, 2023, triggered by the US Marine Osprey crash in the waters off Yakushima.
The ship departed from Sasebo Base at 17:15, approximately 2 hours and 35 minutes after the crash.
The unit arrived at the scene on the night of November 30 at 1:35 AM.
The unit conducted search and rescue operations until December 2 alongside other independently dispatched vessels.

== Naming ==

The ship's name was solicited from the Japan Maritime Self-Defense Force units and others.
Defense Minister Kishida Fumio decided the ship's name.
