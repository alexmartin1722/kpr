# Elsa Bornemann

Elsa Bornemann (February 20, 1952 - May 24, 2013) was an Argentine children's writer who was a doctor of the Arts, polyglot and composer. Her books have been translated into many languages, including Braille.

Elsa Isabel Bornemann was born in the Parque Patricios neighborhood of Buenos Aires to Wilhelm Karl Henri Bornemann, a German immigrant, and Blanca Nieves Fernández, an Argentine of Portuguese and Spanish ancestry.
She was the youngest of three sisters.
She was a teacher, having received her degree in the University of Buenos Aires.
Elsa Isabel Bornemann died in Buenos Aires on May 24, 2013.
During the 1976-83 National Reorganization Process, her book Un elefante ocupa mucho espacio was censored and banned.
The same story was featured on the Honor list of the Hans Christian Andersen Award, the first Argentine to be recognized in such a manner.
Later, her books Bilembambudín o el último Mago and Disparatario were selected as part of the list "The White Ravens" awarded by the Internationale Jugendbibliothek.
Some of her books like Socorro or Queridos monstruos sold more than 100 thousands copies.

== Education and academic career ==

Elsa Isabel Bornemann was a professor of Letters who taught at all educational levels, gave numerous courses and lectures, and participated in various roundtables and juries.
She obtained the title of National Normal Teacher after graduating from the Escuela Normal N° 11 Ricardo Levene.
She received a degree in Letters from the Faculty of Philosophy and Letters, which is part of the University of Buenos Aires.
She obtained several study diplomas in English, German, Italian, Latin, Classical Greek, and Hebrew.

== Early works and first publications (1971–1979) ==

The work *El espejo distraído* was published in 1971.
*Caramelos surtidos* was published by Ediciones Orión and includes the short story "Uno más uno," which is about young lovers.
The book *An Elephant Occupies a Lot of Space* was published in 1975.
*Cuadernos de un delfín* was published in 1976 and features the notebooks of Simo, a young dolphin who is captured at some point in the story, revealing his experiences starting from the moment he is captured.
The book *El libro de los chicos enamorados* was published in 1977 and contains poems about the sensations produced by childhood love that are grouped based on whether the characters want to confess their feelings, get angry, or be friends.
*El último mago o Bilembambudín* was published in 1979, in which Aldana is guided by Jeremías, known as the "last wizard," and travels to the kingdom of Bilembambudín.

== Middle period publications (1981–1990) ==

El niño envuelto was published in 1981, Disparatario in 1983, Los grendelines and Nada de tucanes in 1985, and Lisa de los Paraguas in 1986.
The book ¡Socorro! was published in 1988 and contains twelve horror stories for children presented by a monster created by Victor Frankenstein.
Sol de noche and La edad del pavo were published in 1990, and Lobo Rojo y Caperucita Feroz was published in 1991.
In the story Lobo Rojo y Caperucita Feroz, Red Wolf must cross the forest to visit his grandmother and faces the fearsome Little Red Riding Hood.

== Later works and anthologies (1991–2011) ==

In 1991, the books *Queridos monstruos*, *No somos irrompibles*, and *Los desmaravilladores* were published.
*No hagan olas* was published in 1993.
*Socorro Diez* was published in 1994, and its stories are presented by Quasimodo and feature mysteries, incredible apparitions, and terrifying creatures.
*A la luna en punto* was published in 2000 and is a continuation of *Sol de noche*.
*Amorcitos sub-14* was published in 2003 and contains secrets, declarations, absences, joys, and sorrows shared by children under 14.
*Corazonadas* was published in 2005.
The work titled *Mini-antología de cuentos tradicionales* was published in 2001 and contains fourteen traditional stories that were selected and recreated by Elsa Bornemann.
The books *Treinta y cuatro lauchitas* and *Una trenza tan larga...* were published in 2009.
* Mil grullas* was published in 2011.

"Mil grullas" is a participation in an anthology of short stories by various authors.
The story features two children named Naomi and Toshiro who are located in Hiroshima around 1945.
Toshiro used his hands to make a thousand paper cranes.

== Final publications and posthumous works (2014–2015) ==

Puro ojos, the author's last book, was published posthumously in 2014.
The book Un día, una brújula, a selection of the author's best poems and songs, was published in 2015.

== Publishing details and sales ==

The books were published by Alfaguara and more than two million copies of these books have been sold.
The person participated in anthology books.
Poemas para todos los días was published in 2004.

Elsa Isabel Bornemann wrote for children, young adults, and adults.
The author wrote poetry, novels, and short stories.

== Konex Awards and Recognition ==

The Konex Foundation awarded Merit diplomas in 2004 to one hundred distinguished personalities for achievements in Argentine Literature from the decade between 1994 and 2003.
Elsa Bornemann received an award for her entire body of work benefiting childhood.
She was chosen as the most relevant writer of the last decade and received the Konex Platinum Award for her entire literary work.
The award ceremony took place in Buenos Aires and was presented by the Fundación Konex.

== National and Regional Awards ==

The award was presented in Caracas, Venezuela in 1982 for her work published between 1982 and 1985.
Elsa Bornemann received an ALIJA Honor List award in 1986 for the book 'Puro ojos', where ALIJA stands for the Association of Children's and Young Adult Literature of Argentina.
This recognition was awarded in Buenos Aires in 1987.
The 'Who is Who' guide was edited by the Bologna Book Fair in Italy.
Elsa Bornemann received the Edenor and Radio Clásica de Buenos Aires awards for her contribution to children's literature.
The award was given for the work 'El espejo distraído'.

== Censorship and workshops ==

Argentina was governed by a military dictatorship, and the book 'Un elefante ocupa mucho espacio' was added to the list of banned authors.
The person conducted numerous courses and workshops on literature with Professor Manuel Kedes in Argentina, the Americas, Europe, and Japan.

== Legacy and recognition ==

Many of his works have been reproduced in primary school reading books, literature manuals for various levels, Argentine anthologies, and foreign anthologies.
An exhibition titled "Elsa Bornemann illustrated" took place in 2014 at the National Congress Library of Argentina in honor of Elsa Bornemann.
The Secretary of Culture of the Argentine Nation declared the exhibition of cultural interest.
A reading room for teenagers named after Elsa Bornemann is located in the Argentine National Congress Library.
