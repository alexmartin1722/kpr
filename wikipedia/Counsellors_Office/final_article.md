# Counsellors Office

The Counsellors' Office of the State Council () is an advisory agency directly under the State Council of People's Republic of China. The Central Institute of Culture and History () is a research institute led by the same leadership and located in the same building. They are regarded as the official government think tanks.

== History ==

The State Council Advisory Committee was established in November 1949 and was originally named the Advisory Office of the State Council of the Central People's Government.
On April 7, 1949, just before the liberation of Shanghai, Mao Zedong sent an electric message addressed to the leaders of the East China Bureau of the Central Committee proposing that a Council of Advisors should be established after the liberation of Shanghai to provide advice and strategies for building new Shanghai.
The State Council Advisory Office was established on November 11, 1949.
Advisory offices for municipal governments in provinces and regions were established around 1950.
The State Council formulated the 'Provisional Rules of Work for the State Council Advisory Office'.
The Central Research Institute for Literature and History was established in July 1951 after Mao Zedong proposed the establishment of the institute.

The counsellors and the researchers are appointed by the Premier of the State Council.
Most advisors, fellows, and special researchers are members of democratic parties or non-partisan individuals.
Some advisors, fellows, and special researchers are experts and scholars from the Communist Party of China.
Some advisors, fellows, and special researchers are experienced government officials.

== Functions and Responsibilities ==

The Office of State Council Advisors is abbreviated as the Advisors' Office and is a deputy-ministerial level institution directly under the State Council of the People's Republic of China.
The office is responsible for government advisor work, serves as an intellectual support institution for government decision-making, and has a role in united front work.
The Regulations on the Work of Government Advisers were officially implemented in January 2010, and the implementation of these regulations promoted further standardization of adviser work.
According to the Regulations, the main responsibilities of the Office of Government Advisers include participating in political affairs and offering suggestions, consulting on national issues, democratic supervision, and united front work and fostering connections.
Government advisors serve directly for government leaders and do not represent the interests of any political party or department.
The Council of Advisors is different from various think tanks and has a 'green channel' for offering advice and suggestions.
Advice and suggestions from advisors are sent directly to the leadership for immediate approval, and there are no barriers in presenting advice and suggestions from advisors.

The Office is led by a Chairman Wang Zhongwei, joined by three Vice Chairmen: Wang Weimin, Zhao Bing, Zhang Yantong.
The institute is led by a President: Yuan Xingpei, with a Vice President: Feng Yuan

== Organizational Structure ==

The State Council has established a Research Office for Government Advisors, and provincial people's governments have also established Research Offices for Government Advisors.
Many municipal people's governments under provincial jurisdiction have established Research Offices for Government Advisors.
Local people's governments that establish a Council of Advisors' Office mostly also establish a Research Institute for Literature and History.
The directors of local literature and history research institutes are appointed by the heads of local governments, and the members of local literature and history research institutes are appointed by the heads of local governments.

== International Cooperation ==

In recent years, the State Council's Office of Counselors has actively carried out business and exchange cooperation with think tanks in some countries around the world.
Cooperative relationships have been established with think tanks from 22 countries and regions, involving a total of 33 think tanks.
These cooperative relationships take different forms.
