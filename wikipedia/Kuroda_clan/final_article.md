# Kuroda clan

The was a Japanese samurai clan which came to prominence during the Sengoku period.

The Kuroda clan claimed its origins in Tōtōmi Province.

== Traditional Omi Genji Lineage ==

There are multiple theories regarding the origins of the Kuroda clan.
One theory suggests the Kuroda clan originated from Omi.
The Kuroda descended from the Sasaki of Ōmi, and the Sasaki themselves descended from the Uda Genji.
The Kuroda clan descended from the Sasaki clan, which was a branch of the Kyogoku clan.
The Koga family originated from the Sasaki clan of medieval Omi Province, and the Koga family line descended from the Sasaki clan branch.
In the early 16th century, Kuroda Takamasa was a descendant of the Sasaki clan, a branch of the Omi Genji.
In the early 16th century, a branch of the Sasaki clan left Omi Province, and this migration was aided by another branch of the Omi Genji.
They traveled to the Iku District of Bizen Province.
Moritaka served a powerful family in the same province.
Kuroda Takamasa fled from Omi Province and sought refuge with the Kaji clan in Echigo Province, who were also part of the Omi Genji lineage.
Kuroda Takamasa moved to Fukuoka in Iyo-Koku, Bizen Province.
The story is based on the 'Mugen Monogatari', which was established in the early modern period.

== Alternative Genealogical Theories ==

The Kankyo Zekazue Den states that the lineage before Shigetaka was considered 'interrupted' and introduces a genealogy prior to Shigetaka that traces back to the Hatakeyama clan (Kyogoku branch), which held power in Omi Province during the Middle Ages.
According to tradition, the lineage before Shigetaka was not passed down, yet a man named Munemitsu, believed to be the younger brother of Masanaga Kyogoku, lived in Kuroda Village, Iga District, Omi Province and began using the name Kuroda.
The Kuroda family is considered to be descendants of Munemasa according to the 'Kuroda Family Genealogy' and 'Kanoe Zuka Shozoden', though there are strong views questioning this claim as presented in documents including the Meisho Tsunki and the Harima Kagami.
This genealogy differs significantly from the traditional theory by stating that Kuroda Nagataka's grandfather was actually his biological father, whereas the individual traditionally identified as Kuroda Nagatoka's grandfather is Kuroda Nagatomo.
Kuroda Nobufusa is recorded as the adoptive father of Takahisa but is not recorded as the biological father of Takahisa, and he is recorded as a member of the Kotera clan rather than the Kuroda clan.
The genealogy could serve as evidence for the theory that a Black田 clan distinct from the Omi Minamoto lineage existed in Harima Province from the beginning.
One theory states that the family originated from the Bizen region, while another theory claims they served the Akamatsu clan.
The Kuroda clan of the Fukuoka Domain in Chikuzen Province can be traced back to a kuni no myo (feudal lord) who was active in the late Sengoku period.

One theory suggests the Kuroda clan originated from Akashi and another suggests they originated from Nishi-waki.
This ancestor was based in Bitchu Province.

== Historical Doubts and Scholarly Critique ==

The history of the Kuroda clan's descent remains uncertain, and there are doubts regarding the theory linking the Omi Kuroda clan to the Fukuoka feudal lord Kuroda clan.
These doubts have existed since the early modern period (Edo period), and historians since the early modern period have questioned the connection between the Omi Kuroda clan and the Fukuoka Domain lord Kuroda clan.
Historian Katsumi Suwa states that the traditional theory seeking the origins of the Fukuoka Kuroda clan in the Omi Genji must now be treated with caution.

== Crests and Surname Origins ==

Nagamasa's original surname was Fujiwara.
The Kuroda family crest (Fujiwara-Ba) incorporates the paulownia motif used by the Fujiwara clan.
The Kuroda family primarily used the Ishimochi (white mochi, black mochi) mon as their main crest.

== Chronology and Early Records ==

The origins of the Kuroda clan are said to date back to the late Kamakura period, and records of the family's activities as retainers to the Ashikaga shogun remain.
The existence of the Kuroda family is confirmed until the Tenmon era, which occurred after the time when Kuroda Takamasa is said to have left Omi province.
Takamasa is identified as being from the Omi Kuroda clan, and the 'Kuroda Keifu' states that he left Omi during his lifetime.
The text in 'Kuroda Keifu' provides supplementary evidence suggesting the Omi Kuroda clan and the Fukuoka Domain lord Kuroda clan are of the same lineage, though the genealogy before Shigemori is unknown.

In the 16th century, the Kuroda clan was located in Harima Province.
Under the headship of Kuroda Yoshitaka, the clan served the Oda and later Toyotomi clans.
Yoshitaka specifically worked as a battle tactician, and was considered to be on par with Takenaka Shigeharu, another prominent tactician of the era.
For his service, Yoshitaka received lordship of Nakatsu Castle, in 1587.
In 1600, Yoshitaka and his son Kuroda Nagamasa took part in the Sekigahara Campaign on the side of Tokugawa Ieyasu.
Yoshitaka was also a Roman Catholic with the baptismal name of "Don Simeon".

== Service to Oda Nobunaga ==

The Kuroda clan entered the service of Oda Nobunaga in 1577.

Hachibei Hideyoshi was a close retainer of Oda Nobunaga.

== Service to Toyotomi Hideyoshi ==

The Kuroda clan increased their influence due to the services of Kuroda Yoshitaka, who was an important military advisor to Toyotomi Hideyoshi.
In 1587 (Tensho 15), he became the lord of Nakatsu Castle in Buzen Province, and his domain was valued at 120,000 koku.

== Rebellions and Conflicts ==

Kuroda Hidetada rebelled against his lord, Nagao Harukage.
Uesugi Sadamune, the Shugo of Echigo, ordered the suppression of the rebellion.
Nagao Kagetora, the younger brother of Nagao Harukage, was tasked with suppressing the rebellion.

At the Battle of Sekigahara in 1600, Tokugawa Ieyasu's Eastern Army defeated Ishida Mitsunari's Western Army, and Ieyasu emerged as the dominant power figure in Japan.
Kuroda Yoshitaka and his son Nagamasa supported Ieyasu in combat, and were rewarded for their service with a transfer to the Fukuoka Domain, rated at 520,000 koku of land.

Two branches of the family were founded in 1623.
Kuroda Nagamasa's 3rd son Nagaoki founded the first; he was given 50,000 koku of land which became the Akizuki Domain.
Nagamasa's fourth son Takamasa founded the second; he was given 40,000 koku of land which became the Tōren-ji Domain.

The forces of the Kuroda clan of Fukuoka took part in the Shimabara Rebellion in 1638.
18,000 men under Kuroda Tadayuki assisted in laying siege to Hara Castle.

In 1784, two schools were founded for the Fukuoka domain's samurai sons, Shūyū-kan and Kantō-kan.
Of the two, Shūyū-kan still exists as Shūyū-kan Prefectural High School.

== Succession Crisis and Adoptions ==

The main lineage of the Kuroda family faced a succession crisis during the time of the 6th daimyo, Tsunetaka, after his two sons died in quick succession.
These deaths caused a problem regarding the succession to the domain.
It was initially planned to adopt a grandson as heir, specifically Nagatake (later Aishiro Nagahiro), whose mother Fujiko had married into the Ikeda clan of Okayama Domain.
The adoption plan was changed due to a request from the Shogunate, and Harunori adopted an heir from the Ichijo Tokugawa family instead.
Adoptees continued to be brought in after the initial adoption.
This title was given starting from the second lord, Tadamune.

== Religious Contributions and Retainers ==

Kuroda Nagamasa donated a stone torii gate to Nikko Toshogu Shrine, on which the name 'Kuroda Tsukushi-no-kami Fujiwara Nagamasa' was carved.

During the Boshin War of 1868–69, the Kuroda clan supported the imperial government. Troops from Fukuoka took part in the Battle of Aizu and the Battle of Hakodate, among others.

== Transition from Domain to Prefecture ==

Kuroda Nagatomo was appointed governor of the Fukuoka Domain in 1869.
A counterfeit money scandal was discovered in 1871.
Kuroda Nagatomo was dismissed from his position as governor before the abolition of domains.
Prince Aritsuna of Yūshūkyō became the last governor of the Fukuoka Domain.
The abolition of domains and establishment of prefectures occurred in 1871.
The Fukuoka Domain was dissolved due to the abolition of the domains and establishment of prefectures.
Prince Arisugawa Taruhito was appointed governor by the Emperor and governed the Fukuoka Prefecture for a short time in 1871.

== Establishment of the Kazoku Peerage ==

The Kuroda family was listed among the kazoku (nobility) since 1869 and became part of the kazoku class after the Meiji Restoration.
This status did not affect the Kuroda family's position.
In 1884, the Kazoku Law was implemented, establishing a five-rank peerage system.
The Kuroda clan belonged to the kazoku peerage system, where the former became a Marquisate and the latter became a Viscountcy after the Meiji Restoration.

== Hikota Marquisate ==

Nagatoshi served as vice president of the House of Peers.

== Akiyama Viscountcy ==

The former feudal lord family of Akiyama was the Kuroda family, which was elevated to the rank of Viscount on July 8, 1884, during the reign of Emperor Meiji.
The residence of the Akiyama Kuroda Viscount family was located in Fukuyama-cho, Akasaka-ku, Tokyo City, during the early Showa period.

== Sanagi Baroncy ==

The Sanagi Kuroda family was elevated to the rank of baron on May 9, 1900.
The residence of the Sanagi Kuroda Baron family existed in the early Showa period and was located in Minamiki Village, Asakura District, Fukuoka Prefecture.

== Genealogical sources and historical discrepancies ==

The 'Kan'ei Zekka Keizu-den' was created approximately 40 years after the establishment of the Fukuoka Domain and states that the Kuroda lineage can be traced back to Kuroda Shigetaka, who is identified as the 8th lord of the Kuroda family.
Takamasa is considered the father of Kuroda Shigetaka.
The text 'Kuroda Keifu' was compiled by Kaibara Ekiken, a retainer of the Fukuoka Domain, approximately 60 years after the 'Kanoe Zekka Kizoden'.
According to 'Kuroda Keifu', Takamasa is presented as the father of Kuroda Shigetaka, and the 9th lord of the Kuroda family was named Kuroda Haritaka.
Kuroda Takakage is identified as the younger brother of Haritaka according to the same genealogy, which was created at the end of the Edo period and contains many descriptions that deviate from historical facts.
