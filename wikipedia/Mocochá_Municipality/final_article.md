# Mocochá Municipality

Mocochá Municipality (Yucatec Maya language, 'reflection of the knot in the water') is a municipality in the Mexican state of Yucatán containing of land and located roughly northeast of the city of Mérida.

== Etymology ==

The name's meaning is derived from the word "Moc," which means knot, the word "och," which means shadow, and the word "a," which means water.
This free interpretation assigns the meaning "Water of the hole" to the name.

== Geography ==

The municipality is located in the northern central region and has an area of 47 square kilometers, which represents 0.12% of the total state area.
The terrain in Mocochá is very flat, and its highest point is 10 meters above sea level and is located on an unnamed farm.
To the north, it borders Ishil, and to the south, it borders Yaxkukul.
To the east, it borders Baca, and to the west, it borders Chichmul-Pueblo and Conkal.
The region has a hot steppe climate, with May as the warmest month, December as the coldest month, and September as the rainiest month.

The driest month is March.

== General Information ==

Mocochá de Peón is a municipality and one of the 106 municipalities in the Mexican state of Yucatán.

During pre-Hispanic times, the town fell within the provinces under the chieftainship of Ceh Pech.
Mayan archaeological remains have been found near the municipal seat.
After the conquest the area became part of the encomienda system.
In 1549 Francisco de Montejo the Younger was the encomendero and responsible for 1680 indigenous persons.
In 1571 it had passed to Diego de Santillán.

Yucatán declared its independence from the Spanish Crown in 1821 and in 1825, the area was assigned to the Coastal region with its headquarters in Izamal.
In the mid-nineteenth century, Mocochá was assigned to the Tixkokob Municipality and remained in that jurisdiction until 1918 when it was designated as its own municipality.

The municipal president is elected for a three-year term. The town council has four councilpersons, who serve as Secretary and councilors of public security, ecology and public sanitation.

The Municipal Council administers the business of the municipality. It is responsible for budgeting and expenditures and producing all required reports for all branches of the municipal administration. Annually it determines educational standards for schools.

The Police Commissioners ensure public order and safety. They are tasked with enforcing regulations, distributing materials and administering rulings of general compliance issued by the council.

The head of the municipality is Mocochá, Yucatán.
The municipality consists of 16 populated places.
The larger populated areas of the municipality include Carolina, Kinchén, Kumán, el Porvenir, San Ignacio, Santa Isabel, Tekat, Toóh, Xluch, and Xbená.

== Economy ==

Mocochá is part of the henequen zone where the agave produced in these plots is of good quality.
The henequen activity was the regional vocation of the Carolina and Santa Isabel estates for many years, and these estates were major exporters of henequen fiber from the region.
The Maza Arteaga family were major exporters of the precious fiber abroad.
The agricultural industry declined, and the municipality found a partial solution in raising poultry, pigs, and cattle.
Beans, vegetables, corn, and chili peppers are cultivated.
42.7% of the working population was employed in trade, services, and tourism, 35% was employed in production and construction, 21.3% was employed in agriculture and livestock farming, and 1% was unemployed.

Every year on 14 April is the celebration for the town's patroness, Immaculate Conception.
Popular festivals are held on 15 August in honor of the Most Holy Virgin of the Assumption.

Convent and Church of the Immaculate Conception built in the seventeenth century Hacienda Mocochá Hacienda Tekat Hacienda Toó Ascuncion Mococha is the shrine built in late 1600s which is dedicated to the Virgin of the Assumption.
It is situated in a palm grove garden.
Its entrance doors are dated to 1697.
A small stone image of Virgin in a praying posture is embedded in the western door frame.
There is a double storied camarin at the rear side of the altar where the image is located.
The facade of the church is in a square geometrical design with a pair of three tiered espandans, which have simple openings with decoration of pyramid shaped merlons.
The porch has decorative arcades with stucco ornamentation dated to 1700s.
Three reablos of baroque design are located in the church.
These are modern but crudely painted and of poor quality, as the originals could not be restored.
The altar piece in the church has an image of the Virgin.
There is an adjoining altar where image of St.
Anne carrying the Virgin and the Child is deified.
