# Roermond

Roermond (; or ) is a city, municipality, and diocese in the Limburg province of the Netherlands. Roermond is a historically important town on the lower Roer on the east bank of the river Meuse. It received town rights in 1231. Roermond's town centre has become a designated conservation area.

Through the centuries, the town has filled the role of commercial centre and a principal town in the duchy of Guelders. Since 1559, it has served as the seat of the Roman Catholic Diocese of Roermond. The skyline of the town is dominated by the towers of its two churches, St. Christopher's Cathedral and Roermond Minster ("Munsterkerk" in Dutch). In addition to the churches, the town centre has many significant buildings and monuments.

== Etymology ==

The city name Roermond suggests the confluence of the Rur (Roer) and the Maas rivers, but the name has a different origin.
The confluence of the Rur in the Maas was located several kilometers west of the city before 1338, and the claim that the city is located at the mouth of the Roer is inaccurate because the confluence with the primitive Meuse was located further west.
The second syllable 'mond' likely originates from the term 'monte', which refers to a hillfort, meaning Roermond therefore means 'Rur fortress'.
According to an alternative interpretation, the word 'mond' is derived from the Latin word 'mundium', meaning city, which suggests that Roermond means 'city on the Rur' or 'Rur bridge'.
The name 'Roermond' means 'city on the Roer', and Roermond seems to have the same meaning as Rijnmond (Rotterdam).
The name Ruremonde literally means 'mouth of the Roer', and the new confluence of the Roer is now located very close to the old town.
In Limburgish, Roermond is called Remunj, and the local name for Roermond in Limburgish is Remunj.
In French, Roermond is called Ruremonde, and in historical Italian, Roermond is called Rurmonda or Ruremonda.

Celtic inhabitants of this region used to live on both sides of the river Roer.
Invading Romans built a bridge (now called the Steene Brök, or stone bridge) and founded the first town at Roermond, now a suburb called Voorstad Sint Jacob.

The train station is named Roermond.
After going around Roermond, the line will follow its historical route again, which includes tunneling under the Meinweg.

== Bus and local transport ==

City and rural buses connect various districts and link to Roermond railway station.
The city established 'Shopbuses' in April 2008, which function as shuttle buses.
City buses operate on two routes, one running from the station to Kitskensberg and the other from the station to Herten.
Regional buses serve destinations including Weert, Venlo, and Echt.
Bus lines were operated by Hermes until December 2006, when Veolia Transport took over the task.
Arriva took over the bus lines from Veolia Transport on December 11, 2016.

== Waterways and ports ==

The local transport connection runs along the Meuse river towards Venlo and continues via the Maas line towards Nijmegen.
Most boat and ship traffic is due to tourism.
Roermond has several yacht harbors, an industrial harbor, a landing stage at the mouth of the Rur river, and two berths on the Maas riverbank for larger excursion boats.
Tourist ship traffic uses the Rur estuary.
The area includes an old loading dock called Looskade and a docking pier before the Stenen Brug, which is also called the Maria Theresia Brug.
The docking piers are built on both sides of the Rur river, where overnight stays are not allowed.
The city has an industrial river port and a marina.
The first dock harbor was built just before World War II.
The port was significantly expanded in 1968, an expansion that included the Maashaven, and a marina was established in 1968.
