# Thomas Hay 9th Earl of Kinnoull

Thomas Hay, 9th Earl of Kinnoull (4 July 1710 – 27 December 1787), styled Viscount Dupplin from 1719 to 1758, was a Scottish peer, British politician, and scholar.

Hay was the eldest son of George Hay, 8th Earl of Kinnoull, and Abigail, daughter of Robert Harley, 1st Earl of Oxford and Earl Mortimer.

He was educated at Westminster School and then at Christ Church, Oxford.

On 12 June 1741, at Oxford Chapel, Marylebone, he married Constantia Ernle, the only daughter and heiress of John Kyrle Ernle of Whetham House, near Calne, Wiltshire.
Her great-grandfather was Sir John Ernle, who served as Chancellor of the Exchequer between 1676 and 1689.

They had a son, born 12 August 1742, who died 14 October 1743.
She died in July 1753, and was buried in Calne.
She had left her money to James Money, son of her first cousin, Elizabeth.
A lengthy lawsuit followed between Kinnoull and Money.

He succeeded to the earldom upon his father's death on 28 July 1758.

== Parents and ancestry ==

Abigail Harley died on July 15, 1750.
George Hay lived from 1689 to 1758.
John Kirke Ernle lived from 1683 to 1725.
Sir John Ernle lived from 1620 to 1697.

== Education and early life ==

He received education at Westminster School starting in 1718 and enrolled at Christ Church, Oxford University on June 13, 1726.
While he was a student at the University of Oxford, he received assistance from King George I.
According to clergy members, the Viscount Dupplin was often sad and often shed tears while at Oxford University.

== Marriage and wife ==

Constantia Ernle was born in 1717 and lived from approximately 1717 to June 29, 1753.

== Children ==

They had a son named Thomas John Earnl Hay, who was born on 12 August 1742 and died on 14 October 1743.

== Succession and relatives ==

Robert Hay-Drummond, Thomas Hay's native nephew, was the 10th Earl of Kinnoull.

== Social connections ==

The person was a friend of James Ogilvy, 2nd Earl of Seafield, who was the husband of the person's paternal aunt, and of John Hope, 2nd Earl of Hopetoun, who was the husband of the daughter of the 2nd Earl of Seafield.
Francis Maney was married to Constancia's cousin.

== Father's travels and service ==

The father served from 1728 to 1734.
During his service, he traveled to Constantinople.
In January 1730, he stayed in Lisbon for a while.

== Legal settlement ==

The case was settled by the Earl of Kinnoul receiving an annuity for his lifetime only.

As Viscount Dupplin, he was elected for Scarborough in 1736, but his election was overturned on petition.
He sat as Member of Parliament (MP) for Cambridge from 1741 until 1758.
As an MP, he gradually rose to a position of influence.
In the final two parliaments, he served as chairman of the committee of privileges and elections.
In 1741, he was appointed one of the commissioners of the revenue in Ireland, and in 1746 was made a lord of trade and plantations.
In 1751, Horace Walpole described the earl as "fond of forms and trifles," but "not absolutely a bad speaker."
He took a prominent part in the efforts to improve the condition of Nova Scotia.
In 1754, Thomas Pelham-Holles, 1st Duke of Newcastle made him a Lord of the Treasury.
He also served joint Paymaster of the Forces from 1755 until 1757.

He was well known in political and literary circles, and his friends included Philip Yorke, 1st Earl of Hardwicke, William Murray, 1st Earl of Mansfield, and the Archbishop of Canterbury Thomas Secker.
He was also acquainted with Alexander Pope, who used him as a model for the character Balbus in his Epistle to Dr Arbuthnot.

He served as Chancellor of the Duchy of Lancaster from 1758 to 1762.
In 1759, he served as special envoy to Portugal, returning the next year.
After the Duke of Newcastle resigned his posts in 1762, Kinnoull told George III he would support whatever minister the King should name.
However, when the William Cavendish, 4th Duke of Devonshire was dismissed, Kinnoull resigned his posts from his "inviolable friendship" to Devonshire.

He then retired to the country.
His final post was as Chancellor of the University of St Andrews from 1765 to his death on 27 December 1787.
He was buried in Aberdalgie.

The earldom passed to his nephew, Robert Hay-Drummond.

== Early Parliamentary Elections and Party Affiliation ==

The by-election took place in January 1736, in which the candidate received support from Thomas Osborne, 4th Duke of Leeds, who was the son of the speaker's mother's sister. Viscount Dupplin and the opposing candidate William Osbaldeston both belonged to the Whig party, though the government supported Osbaldeston. Osbaldeston won the votes of local authorities while Viscount Dupplin won the votes of free citizens, leading to Viscount Dupplin being declared the winner at the time of the by-election. The House of Commons ruled in April of that year that voting rights belonged to local authorities, and as a result of this ruling, Osbaldeston was declared the winner.

== Parliamentary Service and Re-elections ==

Viscount Dupplin stood for election in the 1741 British general election and lost the election.
He ran for office with the support of his maternal uncle, Edward Harley, 2nd Earl of Oxford and Mortimer.
He won the election without any opposition.
He was re-elected in the general election of 1747 and in the general election of 1754.

== Early Government Appointments and Patronage ==

In late 1740, an application was made to the first Duke of Newcastle, Thomas Pelham-Holles, seeking the appointment of the father as Governor of Barbados, but the application was unsuccessful.
The father was granted an annuity of 800 pounds funded by secret service money.
The appointment occurred in April 1741 just before a general election.
Prime Minister Robert Walpole gave a high evaluation of the individual.
The position of Irish Revenue Commissioner was equivalent to an annual income of 1,000 pounds.

== Rise to Influence and Treasury Roles ==

Thomas Hay, 9th Earl of Kinnoull, served as Under-Secretary of the Treasury from 1754 to 1755, a promotion granted by Prime Minister Henry Pelham.
In 1755, the individual was appointed jointly with Henry Vane, 1st Earl of Darlington.
The person assisted with election-related affairs during Henry Pelham's tenure, and after Henry Pelham died just before a general election in 1754, the Duke of Newcastle succeeded Henry Pelham.
The person explained Pelham's election plans to the Duke of Newcastle.

== Consideration for Chancellor of the Exchequer ==

In October 1755, the Duke of Newcastle considered selecting a new Chancellor of the Exchequer, and the name of the Viscount Darlington was proposed for the position.
The Earl of Hardwicke and the Baron Mansfield opposed appointing the Viscount Darlington, arguing that it would make him a laughingstock and be detrimental to both the Viscount and the government.
The Viscount Darlington himself showed no interest in the position, and consequently, the plan to appoint him was abandoned.

== Diplomatic Mission to Portugal ==

He was appointed a Privy Councillor on January 27, 1758.
This appointment took place on January 18, 1760.
The appointment was intended as an apology for Portugal's intrusion into Portuguese territorial waters during the Battle of Lagos.
The Earl of Kinnoul arrived in Lisbon on March 8 of that year.
In September of the same year, the Duke of Newcastle sent a letter to the Earl of Kinnoul.
The Duke of Newcastle ordered the Earl of Kinnoul to return home by the end of October.
The Duke of Newcastle stated that everything would be ready by the time the recipient returned.
The Earl of Kinnoul left Lisbon.
The Earl of Kinnoul departed on November 1.

== Later Career, Retirement, and Political Relationships ==

He was favored by three prime ministers: Robert Walpole, Henry Pelham, and Thomas Pelham-Holles, 1st Duke of Newcastle.
His resignation was necessary for Henry Fox's entry into power.
The 4th Duke of Devonshire, William Cavendish, was dismissed in November of the same year (1762).
In July 1765, he was offered a position as Scottish Secretary by the first Marquess of Rockingham's cabinet.
He declined the offer to become Scottish Secretary.
The Duke of Newcastle was indignant about the Earl of Kinnoul's retirement.
The Duke of Newcastle felt as if he had been working hard for over 20 years and was now relieved.
The Duke of Newcastle sent protest letters to many people.
This election took place in 1768.
He had extensive knowledge of finance.
He guided the Duke of Newcastle in his role as First Lord of the Treasury.

== Character and reputation ==

He was generally considered a boring person by his contemporaries.
The 2nd Duchess of Grey, Gemima Yorke, wrote a letter in 1747 describing continuous chatter, debates about the Rebellion Act and the Navigation Act at dinner, and teaching them about colonization and the situation in Nova Scotia that morning.
The History of Parliament stated that the low contemporary evaluation was due to a lack of vocabulary to describe him as boring.
The Oxford Dictionary of National Biography attributes the low opinion of the Earl of Kinnoul to his diverse interests and his attention to detail.
Walpole described the speech as aiming only to understand the subject matter and explain it.
The Oxford Dictionary of National Biography described him as famous.

== Personal connections ==

The person was a friend of Charles Watson-Wentworth, 2nd Marquess of Rockingham, who was Prime Minister, and was acquainted with the writer John Gay.

The 9th Earl built a modest castle near the highest point of Kinnoull Hill, its tower overlooking the Tay.
It was inspired by castles he saw along the Rhine in Germany, during his Grand Tour.
Jane Austen described Kinnoull Tower in Lesley Castle, a story she wrote in 1790, the year after she stayed there with D'Arcy Wentworth, during their ramble through Scotland : I continue secluded from Mankind in our old and Mouldering Castle, which is situated two miles from Perth on a bold projecting rock, and commands an extensive view of the Town and its delightful Environs...
You can form no idea sufficiently hideous, of its dungeon like form.
It is actually perched on a Rock to appearance so totally inaccessible, that I expected to have been pulled up by a rope.
Today, Kinnoull Tower is more easily accessible, via a winding footpath through Kinnoull Hill Woodland Park.
Another of the 9th Earl's lasting legacies is the Perth Bridge over the River Tay, "the Auld Brig" in the local Scots dialect, which he helped fund.
The bridge was designed by John Smeaton.
Construction commenced in 1766 and was completed in 1771.
After retiring from politics, he dedicated himself to managing his estates in Scotland.
He actively engaged in tree planting.
He retired to a village.
After Thomas Hay's death, his possessions passed to Robert Hay-Drummond.
Duplin County, North Carolina, was named for Viscount Dupplin, as the 9th Earl was known when he served on the Board of Trade and Plantations in the 1740s.
Lempster, New Hampshire, was briefly named Dupplin from 1753 to 1767.
