# Emilio Kosterlitzky

Emilio Kosterlitzky (Russian: Эмилио Костерлицкий; 16 November 1853 – 2 March 1928) was a Russian Empire-born Mexican colonel during the Mexican Revolution. He had also served in the Mexican Apache Wars and Yaqui Wars. He is most noted for being the commander of the Mexican Rurales, or border police, during the late Nineteenth Century.

Emil Kosterlitzky was born on November 16, 1853, in Moscow, to a German mother and Russian Cossack father.
He was noted for his language ability; he spoke Russian, Polish, Spanish, French, Italian, English, German, Danish, and Swedish.

In his teens, Emil joined the Russian Navy as a midshipman.
He arrived in America with the Russian Navy in 1871 at the age of 18.
By 1871, at the age of 18, he deserted his ship in Venezuela.
Kosterlitzky then traveled to the Mexican state of Sonora, where he changed his name to Emilio, joined the Mexican Army, and acquired the nickname 'Eagle of Sonora'.

Emil Hermann Kosterlitzky lived in Magdalena de Kino and resided there for 25 years.
He served under Colonel Angel Elias.
He was appointed Marshal of Military Colonies in 1876.
He was promoted to captain of auxiliary troops in 1882.
He was promoted to the rank of major.
He took command of the fiscal gendarmerie group established in Magdalena.
He was promoted to colonel in 1907.

During the 1880s he fought in the Mexican Apache Wars. He also assisted American troops pursuing Apaches across the border under the 1882 United States–Mexico reciprocal border crossing treaty. Kosterlitzky became known to the American troops, who called him the "Mexican Cossack". In 1885, Kosterlitzky was appointed commander of the Gendarmería Fiscal, the customs guard for the Mexican government, by President Porfirio Díaz.

In March 1896, the United States Government had arrested Lauro Aguirre and Flores Chapa, who were both revolutionary insurgents, for being accused of engaging in revolutionary actions since they had established an anti-Díaz newspaper that claimed Porfirio Díaz, the Mexican president, had violated the Constitution of 1857.

== Cananea Strike ==

The Cananea Strike began in June 1906.
Governor Rafael Izábal of Sonora allowed the entry of 275 American Arizona Rangers who were under the orders of Thomas H.
Rynning and had been sent from Magdalena in June 1906 to support order and the forces of Governor Rafael Izábal.
Kosterlitzky dispersed the strikers and forced the armed Americans to withdraw to their country on a train whose destination was Naco.

== Arrest of Manuel Sarabia ==

Manuel Sarabia Díaz de León, the editor of the revolutionary newspaper 'Regeneración', was arrested on June 30, 1907, on charges of murder.
The arrest was carried out by order of Vice-Consul Antonio Maza, who participated in the detention, and was executed by Kosterlitzky.
Sarabia was transported by American authorities from the city of Douglas, Arizona, where Kosterlitzky received him, to a prison in Hermosillo.
Sarabia was released and returned to North American territory.
There were strong protests regarding the detention, and some considered the detention illegal because it occurred on North American territory.

In 1913, Kosterlitzky was commanding a force of 400 men in Northern Mexico to help stop actions of Venustian Carranza and Pancho Villa during the Mexican Revolution. His remaining soldiers had retreated to the border and surrendered to the American garrison of Nogales, Arizona. He was jailed until 1914, when he, his wife, Francisca, and two daughters moved to Los Angeles, California.

He returned to Mexico in 1927, to investigate a plot against the government of the state of Baja California.
Kosterlitzky died in Los Angeles on March 2, 1928, and is buried in Calvary Cemetery in East Los Angeles.
