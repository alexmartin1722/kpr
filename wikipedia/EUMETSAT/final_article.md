# EUMETSAT

The European Organisation for the Exploitation of Meteorological Satellites (EUMETSAT) is an intergovernmental organisation created through an international convention agreed by a current total of 30 European Member States.

EUMETSAT's primary objective is to establish, maintain and exploit European systems of operational meteorological satellites. EUMETSAT is responsible for the launch and operation of the satellites and for delivering satellite data to end-users as well as contributing to the operational monitoring of climate and the detection of global climate changes.

The activities of EUMETSAT contribute to a global meteorological satellite observing system coordinated with other space-faring states.

Satellite observations are an essential input to numerical weather prediction systems and also assist the human forecaster in the diagnosis of potentially hazardous weather developments. Of growing importance is the capacity of weather satellites to gather long-term measurements from space in support of climate change studies.

EUMETSAT is not an institution or agency of the European Union, although the majority of its members are EU member states. The organisation became a signatory to the International Charter on Space and Major Disasters in 2012, thus providing for the global charitable use of its space assets.

== History ==

Eight European countries initiated the Meteosat program, which was launched by the European Space Research Organisation (ESRO), the predecessor of the current ESA, with the aim to create a set of spare parts.
The Meteosat-1 satellite was launched by a Delta rocket from Cape Canaveral on November 23, 1977, and was placed in its intended operational position above the equator and the zero meridian in December 1977.
Continuous imaging had been conducted for two years prior to the premature failure of the radiometer in November 1979, during which the system captured 48 images per day.
The failure was caused by a resistor defect.
An intergovernmental conference took place in Paris and approved the continuation of a program, for which the European Organisation for the Exploitation of Meteorological Satellites (EUMETSAT) is responsible for implementing.
The Meteosat-2 satellite was launched in June 1981 using an Ariane 1 rocket on flight number L03.

The launch took place from Kourou and marked the resumption of operations after a 20-month hiatus.
The satellite was repositioned to the equator and to the zero meridian.
The European Space Agency (ESA) launched the Meteosat Operational Programme (MOP) in March 1983.
The ESA initiated the MOP in anticipation of the establishment and formation of Eumetsat.
The MOP included the assembly and launch of three new satellites: Meteosat-4, Meteosat-5, and Meteosat-6.
The MOP included all operational activities until November 1995.
The event took place in May 1983.
This was the second and last Intergovernmental Conference.
The conference approved the text of the Eumetsat convention and formally opened ratification for European member states.
Eumetsat was established in March 1984.
Eumetsat was designated as a separate section within the European Space Agency.
This separation was intended to prepare for the faster creation of a new organization.
The Data Collection System (DCS) was discontinued in October 1985.
The DCS was operating on the Meteosat-1 satellite.
This takeover was part of an agreement on cooperation within CGMS.

The first meeting of the EUMETSAT Council was hosted by the European Space Agency in Paris, where the Council agreed to locate the headquarters in Darmstadt, Germany.
John Morgan, who is from Great Britain, was elected as the first director and arrived in Darmstadt in August 1986 to begin hiring colleagues.
They worked in a temporary headquarters that served as their home for the next 9 years.
A contract to build a new headquarters was signed in April 1993, and construction work began in the third quarter.
The new headquarters consists of a main building and office wings, with the main building financed mostly by Germany and the office wings financed by member states.
The new headquarters was officially opened in June 1995 and became the venue for the 27th meeting of the Eumetsat Council.
Eumetsat moved to its permanent headquarters after nine years.
An agreement was reached in July 1995 between NOAA and Eumetsat.

Tillmann Mohr, who is from Germany, became the director of Eumetsat in August 1995, replacing John Morgan, who had been the long-time head of Eumetsat.
Technical arrangements for the transmission of High Resolution Images (HRI) were implemented in September 1995 to protect the investment of Eumetsat member countries.
Data reception rights were granted to 35 additional countries that did not include Eumetsat member states, and many of these data reception rights were free of charge.
A Cooperation Agreement was signed in November 1998 in Washington, D.C., between Eumetsat and NOAA for a jointly managed project known as the Initial Joint Polar System.
Eumetsat amended and expanded the Convention in November 1999 to reflect the development of programs concerning global warming.
Cooperation was established in December 1999.
The contract guarantees permanent access for the United States to Meteosat satellite imagery.
INDOEX is an international scientific experiment.
The event occurred in July 1999.

A cooperation agreement concerning MetOp satellites involving Eumetsat, ESA, and Matra Marconi Space was signed in July 2002.
A separate agreement was signed between former Yugoslavia and other parties.
Lars Prahm replaced Tillman Mohr as CEO of Eumetsat in August 2004.

The national mandatory contributions of member states are proportional to their gross national income.
However, the cooperating countries contribute only half of the fee they would pay for full membership.
The convention establishing EUMETSAT was opened for signature in 1983 and entered into force on 19 June 1986.

== Data access for cooperating and non-member states ==

EUMETSAT shares meteorological data with many countries worldwide.
Cooperating countries benefit from lower costs compared to non-EUMETSAT nations when accessing the same data, and they can only obtain full and free access in case of an emergency or meteorological disaster risk.
Contributing countries have access to information limited by their contribution amount, but during emergencies or when there is a threat of meteorological disasters, they can access all information.
Associated countries pay reduced fees for data and have immediate and free access to data in the event of natural disasters.
Some of the world's poorest states can access Meteosat data, with eligibility based on annual per capita income not exceeding $7,901 USD according to the World Bank.

Jason-2 reliably delivers detailed oceanographic data vital to our understanding of weather forecasting and climate change monitoring.

== Collaboration and Data Distribution ==

EUMETSAT and NOAA controlled Jason 2, and NOAA distributed the collected data.
The collaboration involves the reciprocal distribution of data to users and the exchange of European and American detection instruments for installation on respective satellites.

== Additional information ==

Eumetsat produces real-time weather satellite images for its members and provides opportunities for members to participate in application programs using data collected by satellites.
The Finnish Meteorological Institute is involved in implementing the organization's climate program and leads the organization's ozone program from Finland.
The organization originated from the ESA's Meteosat satellite, and different countries negotiated in 1981 and in 1983.
A small secretariat started the operations of EUMETSAT, and this takeover occurred in January 1987.
In 1991, the organization began planning the next generation of weather satellites.
ESA supported its operations, and this support continued until 1995.
The organization's activities are led by a board of directors that decides on the organization's main guidelines and funding level.
The board of directors meets at least twice a year.
Petteri Taalas has been the Chairman of the Board of Directors since summer 2010 and has served as chairman for three years.
Petteri Taalas represents Finland and is the chief executive of the Finnish Meteorological Institute.

There are working groups supporting the Management Board, including the Policy Advisory Committee (PAC) and the Scientific and Technical Group (STG), which was chaired in 2010 by Pirkko Pylkkö from the Finnish Meteorological Institute.
Finland has a representative in all working groups except the Policy Advisory Committee (PAC).
The Working Group on Data and Policy is abbreviated as WGP, and the Meteorological Satellite Coordination Group exists.
EUMETSAT is the European organization for the development and management of weather satellites with its headquarters in Darmstadt.
The organization had approximately 140 employees in 1999.
The website features a weather satellite viewer available in English, Spanish, and Portuguese that provides access to archived data for 2 months.
Member States have full access to EUMETSAT data.

Member States have free access to EUMETSAT data, while cooperating countries receive discounts when accessing the same information and can access all data for free if there is a threat of an emergency situation or natural disaster.
Access to the EUMETSAT database is available to third-party states that are members of WMO, a provision that applies to the poorest countries where the annual income per capita is less than $3,500 based on World Bank statistics.
The European Organisation for the Exploitation of Meteorological Satellites (EUMETSAT) is dedicated to developing a global meteorological satellite monitoring system.
The EUMETSAT website features an online satellite viewer that contains two months of archived data.
