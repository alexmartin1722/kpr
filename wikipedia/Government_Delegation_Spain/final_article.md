# Government Delegation Spain

In Spain, apart from the Central Administration, the central government has a Peripheral Administration. This administration is composed of all those decentralized government services, and they are coordinated by the Government Delegations, which are headquartered in the Spanish regions' capitals. Likewise, this delegations exercise its powers through sub-delegations, headquartered in the provinces and insular directorates, headquartered in some islands. In total, there are 19 government delegations, 44 sub-delegations and 7 insular directorates.

The government delegations were established pursuant Section 154 of the Spanish Constitution and grants to them the task of direct the Government Administration in the regions as well as to direct the regional administration when necessary. The sub-delegations and insular directorates were extended to the national territory in 1997 although it already existed since the 1970s. Their task is to assist the Government Delegate. All of them are part of the Ministry of Territorial Policy and Civil Service.

Both entities are regulated in the Legal Regime of the Public Sector Act of 2015, which was enacted on October 1, 2015.
The regulations are found in Section 2 of Chapter III of Title I of Law 40/2015.
Articles 72 and 73 of this law contain the relevant regulations.
The regulation is also found in Section 1 of Chapter II of Law 6/1997 on the Organization and Operation of the General State Administration (LOFAGE).
The relevant articles are from 22 to 28.

== Legal framework ==

Article 154 of the Spanish Constitution of 1978 exists.
The functions of government delegates are specified in Articles 22 to 28 of Ley 6/1997, the law on the organization and operation of the General State Administration, which was enacted on April 14.

The Government Delegations or Delegations of the Government are the bodies that represents the central government in the autonomous communities.
The Government Delegates have the rank of Under Secretaries and they report to the Prime Minister, although normally this competence is delegated into the Minister for Territorial Policy or, the Secretary of State for Territorial Policy.
They are appointed by the Council of Ministers at the request of the Premier.
If the office of Delegate is vacant, they are replaced by the Sub-delegate until a new Delegate is appointed.
In the regions with a sole province, if there is not sub-delegate is the Secretary-General of the delegation who temporary assumes the office.

Unlike the sub-delegations and insular directorates, the Delegations are constitutionally provided.
The main tasks of the delegations are:

To coordinate the General State Administration services and public bodies in the region.
To inform the citizens about the government activities in the region.
To coordinate the government departments with other public administrations.
To guarantee the correct application of the norms and laws and the respect for the powers of the central government.
To propose measures to the Ministry responsible in order to avoid the inefficiency of the administration and the duplicity of public bodies.
It is up to the Government Delegates to protect the free exercise of the constitutional rights and freedoms and to guarantee public security, through the Government Sub-delegates and the State Security Forces and Corps.
For this purpose, the Delegate is the head of the state law enforcement agencies in the region.

== Nature and status ==

Government delegations in the Spanish public administration have a political character.
The holders of these delegations hold the status of high-ranking officials, a senior official status due to their rank equivalent to undersecretaries.
Government delegates depend legally on the Presidency of the Government.

== Appointment and removal ==

Government delegates are appointed and removed by Royal Decree, which is issued by the Council of Ministers upon a proposal from the President of the Government.
This decree, known as a Real Decreto, is also the instrument by which Government Deputies are appointed.

== Seat and location ==

Government delegates and Government Deputies have their seat in the locality where the Autonomous Government Council is based, which is the city where the autonomous community's government is seated.
This rule applies unless the Council of Ministers determines or decides otherwise.
The rule does not affect any specific provisions explicitly stated in the Statute of Autonomy or prejudice what is established in the Statute of Autonomy.

== Coordination and instructions ==

The Ministry of Territorial Policy and Public Function provides precise instructions to government delegates, which are necessary for the proper coordination of the general administration of the State across Spain.
The main functions include coordinating with the Autonomous Community's own administration.

== Competencies and powers ==

The delegate has authority over legality control and public policies, which are exercised within the scope of the respective autonomous community.
The government delegate has competencies regarding the simplification of administrative structures and sanctioning powers related to public security matters.
The entity has competencies related to the direction of the Government Delegation, the simplification of structures, and the direction of integrated territorial services.

== Public security authority ==

Government delegates are also under the authority of the Ministry of the Interior regarding public security competencies.
In this matter, the State Security Forces and Corps depend functionally on the Ministry of the Interior.

== Annual reporting ==

The government delegate submits an annual report to the government regarding the operation and global evaluation of public state services.
This report is submitted through the Presidency Ministry and the Ministry of Finance and Public Administrations.

== Constitutional basis ==

Article 154 of the Spanish Constitution of 1978 states these functions.

The Government Sub-delegations or Sub-delegations of the Government are bodies that represents the central government in the Spanish provinces.
The Sub-delegations were created by the 1997 General State Administration Organization and Functioning Act to replace the Civil Governors.
The figure of Civil Governor was created in the 19th century.
The Civil Governor is part of the Ministry of Territorial Policy.
It exists a Sub-delegate of the Government in each province under the authority of the regional-level Government Delegate.
The Subdelegation of the Government operates within an Autonomous Community.
They are appointed by the Delegate from career civil servants and they exercise the same powers of the Delegate but a provincial level.
In the single-province autonomous communities and in the autonomous cities, as a general rule, the Government Delegate assumes the powers that the Law attributes to the Government Sub-delegates in the provinces.
These regions are Asturias, Balearic Islands, Cantabria, Ceuta, Melilla, Murcia, Navarre and La Rioja.
In Madrid, since 2003, because of its importance as the capital of the Kingdom and despite being a single-province region, there is both Delegate and Sub-Delegate of the Government.

== Government Delegation for Foreigners and Immigration ==

The Government Delegation for Foreigners and Immigration was created in the year 2000.
The delegation has competencies in matters of foreigners and immigration.

According to Section 70 of the Legal Regime of the Public Sector Act, the existence of Insular Directors is not mandatory.
The Insular Directorates are also included in the Balearic Islands and the Canary Islands.
The government delegate appoints island directors for each island of the Canary and Balearic archipelagos.
When they exist, they are freely appointed by the Government Delegate among civil servants and they depend directly from the Delegate or the Sub-delegate if exists.
Their official title is Insular Directors of the General State Administration and they possess the same powers as a subdelegate.

In order to assist the Government Delegates, there are two kind of collective bodies.
The first kind are to Government Delegations which powers extend in more than one province, while the second is for one-province delegations.
The government delegate has a territorial assistance commission to improve the exercise of their functions.
The commission is called the Territorial Assistance Commission for the Government Delegate.
The purpose of the commission is to better fulfill the directive and coordinating function of the Government Delegate.
The Sub-delegates also have an assistance bodies and there is a nation-wide committee to coordinate all Delegations.

These bodies are chaired by the Delegate of the Government and integrated by the Sub-delegates of the Government of the provinces of its jurisdiction and the heads of the other departments and agencies of the Delegation.
They exist to coordinate the actions of the different bodies, to homogenize the policies, to advise the Delegate of the Government and to discuss any other matter that the Delegate considers relevant.
A territorial assistance commission for the Government Delegate is created in each multi-provincial autonomous community.
The purpose of creating this commission is to better fulfill the directive and coordinating function of the Government Delegate.
The commission exists in every community with at least two provinces.
The commission also exists in the two archipelagos.
In the Balearic Islands and the Canary Islands, island directors are also integrated into the commission.

In the single-province Autonomous Communities, it exists an assistance body chaired by the Delegate of the Government and integrated by the Secretary-General of the Delegation (who runs day-to-day the Delegation) and the heads of the other departments and agencies of the Delegation.

In each Sub-delegation of the Government it exist an assistance committee to the Sub-delegate integrated by the Secretary-General of the Sub-delegation and the heads of the other departments and services of the Sub-delegation. They do the same duties as the other assistance bodies but at a provincial level.

The Interministerial Coordination Committee on the State Peripheral Administration is a body of the Ministry of Territorial Policy and Civil Service chaired by the Minister and integrated by the Secretary of State for Territorial Policy (deputy chair), the Secretary-General for Territorial Coordination, all the Under Secretaries of the government departments, all the Delegates of the Government and the Director-General for Internal Policy. To the meetings of the committee also assists the Deputy Director-General for the Boost of Peripheral Administration which acts as Secretary of the Committee and other senior officials of the Administrations invited by the chair.

The Committee is in charge to improve the coordination of the central government Peripheral Administration, to boos the share of information, to establish a unique criteria of action and to discuss relevant issues for the government policy in the regions.

== Coordination and instructions ==

The competent ministry in matters of Public Administrations is responsible for issuing precise instructions intended for the correct coordination of the General State Administration in the territory, and in 2024, the Ministry of Territorial Policy and Democratic Memory was the competent ministry in matters of Public Administrations.
The Minister of the Interior is responsible for issuing necessary instructions within the scope of State competencies, including instructions regarding public freedoms and citizen security.
This does not affect the competence of other ministries, which can issue instructions related to their respective areas of responsibility.
