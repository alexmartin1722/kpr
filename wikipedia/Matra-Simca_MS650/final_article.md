# Matra-Simca MS650

The Matra-Simca MS650 is a Group 6 prototype race car introduced in 1969 for the International Championship for Makes. The MS650 replaced the previous Matra-Simca MS630.

== Development and Design ==

Work on a new vehicle concept began at Matra in 1969 parallel to the construction of the MS640.
The development of the MS640 was discontinued following a severe test accident involving Henri Pescarolo.
Previous Matra prototypes were all closed sports cars, whereas the MS650 was the first Spyder produced by Matra.
The spelling with 'Y' was protected by Porsche, so the car received the internal designation 'Spider'.
The vehicles were listed as 'Spider' in the Le Mans entry lists, and the successor models were also listed as 'Spider' in the Le Mans entry lists.
The Spider was designed by Bernard Boyer and had an aluminum monocoque.
The Spider was powered by a proprietary 3-liter 12-cylinder engine.
The development of the MS650 was accelerated after Pescarolo's accident to prepare for Le Mans.
The two new vehicles were named MS640 and MS650.
The MS640 and MS650 were intended to complement the MS630 and MS630/650 models.
These vehicles complied with the Group 6 regulations.
Group 6 regulations allowed prototypes with an engine displacement of up to 3000 cm³.
Group 4 vehicles had an engine displacement of 5000 cm³.
At least 25 examples were required for Group 4 vehicles.

The MS640 was destroyed in an accident during a private test that nearly cost the life of driver Henri Pescarolo, and the car was never used in a race.
Designer Choulet left Matra in October 1969 to join Porsche, and Jean-Luc Lagardère refused to authorize the construction of another MS640.
The MS650 frame was made of steel tubing and was similar to the MS640 frame.
The MS650 suspensions were similar to those used in the MS80, which was used in Formula 1.
The bodywork was made of fiberglass and was of a boat-like type.
The curb weight of the MS630 was 820 kg, while the Ferrari 312 P weighed 680 kg and the Porsche 908 weighed 650 kg.
The car remained among the heaviest in its category.
The MS9 engine had a displacement of 2999 cm³, was a V12 configuration, and had previously been mounted on the MS630.

The MS9 engine produced 410 horsepower and reached its peak power at 9800 rpm.
This power was superior to the 360 of the Porsche and almost equal to the 420 of the Ferrari.
The MS650 was built in only three examples with chassis numbers MS650-01, MS650-02, and MS650-03.
The MS650 was replaced by the MS660, a replacement that was definitive.
The MS660s featured a monocoque chassis.

== 1969 24 Hours of Le Mans ==

No Matra sports car had ever crossed the finish line at Le Mans.
The MS650 cars were surprisingly fast and remarkably durable.
Matra benefited from the high number of retirements, as all Porsche 917s and both Ferrari 312Ps retired from the race.
The Matra cars were able to match the speed of the Porsche 908 over long distances.
The team management recorded only one failure.
The chassis of these two cars originated from the previous year's MS630 models, and these two cars already had the bodywork of the MS650 model.
The race consisted of 158 laps.
David Hobbs and Mike Hailwood finished third, while Beltoise and Courage completed the same lap as Hobbs and Hailwood.
During the final hour, Jacky Ickx and Hans Herrmann engaged in a duel for victory that was held in Grand Prix style.
Matra celebrated a victory at the end of the year with the MS650 car, which was Matra's first win in a major sports car race.
Matra designed two new vehicles for the 1969 24 Hours of Le Mans.
The 24 Hours of Le Mans was the third-to-last race of the season.

== 1000 km of Paris ==

Jean-Pierre Beltoise and Henri Pescarolo won the 1000 km of Paris, which took place in Montlhéry.

== 6 Hours of Watkins Glen ==

The next race was the 6 Hours of Watkins Glen, in which only one MS650 competed.
The car started from the third position and was forced to retire at lap 109.
Three Porsche 908s finished ahead of the MS650.

== 1000 km of Montlhéry ==

The MS650 won the 1000 km of Montlhéry at the end of the season.

== Championship Overview ==

The Matra MS650 competed in the World Sportscar Championship and secured second place in its category.

The car made its debut at the 1969 24 Hours of Le Mans with a four car entry. Jean-Pierre Beltoise/Piers Courage in the MS650, Jean Guichet/Nino Vaccarella in the MS630, Nanni Galli/Robin Widdows and Johnny Servoz-Gavin/Herbert Müller were both in the MS630/650. Beltoise/Courage finished fourth followed by Guichet/Vaccarella in fifth, Galli/Widdows seventh but Servoz-Gavin/Müller retired when an electrical circuit failed. For the 1969 6 Hours of Watkins Glen, Matra entered two cars for Servoz-Gavin/Pedro Rodriguez in the MS650 and Guichet/Widdows in the MS630/650. Servoz-Gavin/Rodriguez finished fourth and Guichet/Widdows retired with a broken clutch. For the final race of the 1969 season, the 1000km of Zeltweg, Matra only entered Servoz-Gavin/Rodriguez but the car retired through accident.

In the International Championship for Makes, Matra had scored six points, earning it fifth place in the championship.

The MS650 was equipped with a new engine for the new season, the MS12, which produced 430 horsepower and reached 10,500 rpm.
The MS650 won first place at the 1000 km of Buenos Aires, a race that took place at the beginning of the year.
Two vehicles finished the race, with the MS650 cars finishing fifth overall.
The MS650 driven by Brabham and Beltoise achieved third place in its category before the car retired on lap 161.
The first race of the season was the 1970 24 Hours of Daytona, Matra had two cars for Jack Brabham/François Cevert and Jean-Pierre Beltoise/Henri Pescarolo.
Brabham/Cevert finished tenth and Beltoise/Pescarolo finished 18th.
Matra made changes to the driver line ups for the 12 Hours of Sebring, Pescarolo/Servoz-Gavin and Cevert/Dan Gurney.
Pescarolo/Servoz-Gavin finished fifth and Cevert/Gurney finish 12th.
Matra again made changes to the driver line ups for the 1000km of Brands Hatch, The team put Brabham/Beltoise together but kept Pescarolo/Servoz-Gavin.
Brabham/Beltoise finish 12th and Pescarolo/Servoz-Gavin retired with engine failure.
The MNZ saw Brabham/Beltoise fifth followed by Pescarolo/Servoz-Gavin in sixth.
The team skipped the next three races, the Targa Florio, 1000km of Spa-Francorchamps and 1000km of Nürburgring.
The event took place on April 25 and was the 1000 km of Monza race.
Two MS650 cars participated in the race, with the Beltoise/Brabham car winning first place in its category and the Pescarolo/Gavin car winning second place in its category.
The team entered three cars for the 24 Hours of Le Mans Beltoise/Pescarolo in the new Matra-Simca MS660, Brabham/Cevert and Patrick Depailler/Jean-Pierre Jabouille/Tim Schenken were both in the MS650 but all three cars retired with engine failure.
The team skipped the final two race of the 1970 season, the Watkins Glen 6 Hours and Austrian 1000km.

In the International Championship for Makes, Matra had scored four points, earning it fourth place in the championship.

The 1000 km of Montlhéry was not a valid race for the sport prototype world championship.
The 1000 km of Buenos Aires was not a valid race for the championship.

== Preservation and Auction ==

The winning car from Montlhéry was auctioned in April 2009 in Paris and sold for 1,490,646 euros.
