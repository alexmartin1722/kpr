# Iyotetsu 700 series

The is an electric multiple unit (EMU) train type operated by the private railway operator Iyo Railway (Iyotetsu) in Matsuyama, Ehime, Japan, since 1987.

The trains were converted from former Keio 5000 series EMU cars between 1987 and 1994.

== Conversion and body modifications ==

The trains were modified by Keio Heavy Machinery Maintenance.
The Keio 5000 series underwent a change in car body width during the expansion process, resulting in narrow-body cars with old numbers ending in 01–06 or 51–56 and wide-body cars with old numbers ending in 07–14 or 57–62 due to their relationship with the original vehicle models.
The destination sign on the front was removed, similar to the previously introduced Keio 800 series, while the type display screen on the side has been covered so that only the destination display remains on the side.
Vehicles modified to the MoHa 720 series were connected to the leading cars of the MoHa 710 series using a continuous curtain, which required the width of the wide passage at the connection surface to be reduced to match the width of the leading car's passage.

== Track gauge and bogie adaptation ==

The Keio Emperor Electric Railway has a track gauge of 1372mm, while the Iyo Railway has a track gauge of 1067mm.
To accommodate the track gauge change, electric cars were replaced with bogies from scrapped vehicles and control cars had their axles replaced.
All three types of bogies were manufactured by Sumitomo Metal Industries.
The FS321 is an air spring bogie that was a scrapped part from the Odakyu 2200 series, with the donor cars being DeHa 2217 and DeHa 2218.
The transfer followed a similar precedent to the FS316, which was a scrapped part from the Odakyu 2220 series.
Six sets were transferred in 1984 for the renewal of aging bogies on the FS340 130 series, which belongs to the Tobu 2000 series and had been undergoing scrapping since 1988.
Most of the vehicles were equipped with FS340.
Initially, cars MoHa 717 and MoHa 718 used FS321.
Initially, cars MoHa 712, MoHa 714, MoHa 715, MoHa 722, MoHa 724, and MoHa 725 used FS-316.
The FS321 does not pass through the wheel turning lathe.
The cars using the FS316 were MoHa 722, MoHa 724, and MoHa 725.

== Traction motors and drive systems ==

The source vehicle was equipped with an Oriental Motor TDK-824 drive and a Mitsubishi Electric MB-3032-A drive.
The TDK-824 drive uses a hollow shaft parallel cardan drive system, while the MB-3032-A drive uses a WN drive system.
Both main motors have the same output, but the gear ratios of the two main motors are different.

== Control equipment and braking ==

Toshiba Electric manufactured the ES-556B model, of which 12 units were produced for cars numbered 711 to 719 and 721 to 723.
Hitachi manufactured the MMC-HTB-20B model, of which 6 units were produced for cars numbered 710, 720, and 724 to 727.
The ES-556B uses a main motor that is a spare part from the Deha 2700 series and continues to use this main motor even after the driving method changed.
The MMC-HTB-20B is used in vehicles that were originally Kuha 5700 series cars and have been electrified, with electrification equipment from the Keio era intermediate motor cars relocated to these vehicles.
The ES controller cannot use regenerative braking, and vehicles equipped with the MMC controller also do not use electric braking.

== Coupling compatibility ==

It is possible to operate the train coupled with the 610 series, which uses similar main traction motors.
There are differences in equipment such as broadcasting devices between the trains.
No example exists of these trains being physically connected and operated together due to equipment differences.

== Interior and exterior features ==

All vehicles were equipped with the same Mitsubishi Electric CU-127R type centralized distributed air conditioning unit.
A new livery update was initiated to match the 610 series, replacing the previous color scheme that was the same as the city line vehicles.
A second repainting project was implemented starting from the 2015 fiscal year and involved a single-color orange scheme.
By March 2019, all train sets had been repainted.
Stickers featuring Micky-an were placed on the passage doors of the KuHa 765 cars.

The trains were purchased from Keio Corporation and introduced between 1987 and 1994 to replace older models including the 110 series and 120 series.
The older models had been the mainstay of Iyo Railway's railway line fleet and accounted for more than half of Iyo Railway's railway line electric trains.
The number of these train cars has decreased due to the introduction of the 3000 series.
The motored cars were mounted on former Tobu Railway and Odakyu EMU bogies to match the Iyotetsu track gauge of , as the trains originally ran on Keio gauge.
Keio Deha 5101-5112 were the prototype cars for MoHa 711-719 and MoHa 721-723.
Keio Deha 5101-5112 were originally suspension-driven vehicles derived from the Deha 2700 series.
The main traction motors and main controllers of Keio Deha 5101-5112 were reused from the Deha 2700 series.
The reuse of the main traction motors changed the drive system from suspension drive to cardan drive.
The train formation included a Kogyo-era suspension-driven car.
Except for the base car originally designated as Deha 5114 (Moha 727), none of the other cars were air-conditioned at that time.
The air conditioning retrofit was implemented by Iyo Railway after the start of operations.
When air conditioning was installed, a sign reading 'Cooler trains added this year' was attached in the same way as on the 800 series.
Later, the information was displayed via stickers on the side windows.
FS321 was replaced by FS340 by November 1993.
The first livery update started in 1994 and was completed in 1997.
There was a transition period during which trains with mixed old and new paint schemes were operated.
Three-car set 725 was the first Iyotetsu EMU to be repainted in the new all-over orange Iyotetsu livery, in August 2015.
Three-car sets were scrapped.

In September 2015, two 700 series cars were sold to the Choshi Electric Railway in Chiba Prefecture for 1.3 million yen.
Car 763 and Motor Car 713 were transferred to Choshi Electric Railway.
These cars became the 3000 series for Choshi Electric Railway.

== Successor models ==

Iyo Railway introduced a new train series called the 3000 series between the 2009 and 2011 fiscal years.
The 3000 series was designed for suburban lines and consisted of fixed formations of three cars.
There were 10 sets of these three-car formations.
Commercial operations for the 3000 series began on March 26, 2016.
A new train model, the 7000 series, was announced in November 2023.
The 7000 series is fully manufactured by the company itself and is intended to replace all existing 700 series trains.
The new 7000 series trains are scheduled to begin operations in February 2025.
