# École Militaire station

École Militaire () is a station on Line 8 of the Paris Métro. It is located north of and named after the École militaire in the 7th arrondissement.

The station is located under Avenue de La Motte-Picquet, northeast of Place de l'École-Militaire.
Oriented on a north-east/south-west axis, it is positioned between the La Motte-Picquet–Grenelle and La Tour-Maubourg Métro stations, but is separated from the latter by the current Champ de Mars ghost station.
The station is located on the border between the Quartier du Gros-Caillou and the Quartier de l'École Militaire.
The station is located between the École militaire and the Champ de Mars.
The entrance to the station is located between École Militaire and Champ de Mars.
The École-Militaire square is located at the north corner of the École militaire.
There is a pedestrian commercial section on Rue Cler.
The Eiffel Tower is within walking distance.

The station was opened on 13 July 1913 as part of the original section of Line 8 between Beaugrenelle (now Charles Michels, which is on Line 10) and Opéra.

It owes its name to its location north of the École militaire, at the end of the south-eastern perspective of the Champ de Mars.
It was founded in 1750 on the proposal of the financier Joseph Paris Duverney, with the support of Madame de Pompadour, with the aim of creating an academic college for cadets of humble origins.
On the grounds of the Grenelle farm, developed by Ange-Jacques Gabriel, construction began in 1752, but the school did not open until 1760.
It received young Bonaparte as a pupil in 1784.
Nowadays, the École militaire brings together all higher military education organisations.

From the 1950s until the 2000s, the station's walls were covered with a metallic panels with green horizontal uprights and golden and illuminated advertising frames.
Subsequently, this arrangement was supplemented by Motte style seats, first in yellow, then green for colorimetric uniformity.
As part of the RATP Renouveau du métro programme, the station corridors were renovated on 25 June 2004, then the platforms in 2007, resulting in the removal of their metal bodywork.
This latest modernisation was completed in 2008.

On 9 October 2019, half of the nameplates on the station platforms were temporarily replaced by the RATP in order to celebrate the 60th anniversary of Asterix and Obelix, as in eleven other stations.
Taking up in particular the characteristic typography of the comic strip by René Goscinny and Albert Uderzo, École Militaire was humorously renamed École Légionnaire in reference to the tenth book in the series, Astérix Légionnaire.

In 2019, 4,468,169 riders entered the station, which placed it at the 99th position out of 302 Métro stations for its use.

== Construction and architecture ==

The station was built in an open excavation pit.
This construction differs from the more common elliptical cross-section design found in Paris.
Long beams rest on these support beams.
These long beams support small vaults.
The vaults are made of brick.
The station has a shallow depth.
The roof consists of semicircular sections.
These sections support a set of steel beams.
Using small beveled tiles is uncommon for this type of station.
Larger or flat tiles are usually preferred in such stations.

== Rolling stock and line history ==

Trains of the Sprague-Thomson type operated on Line 8 until 1975.
In 1937, the line was split between Lines 8 and 10 at the La Motte-Picquet – Grenelle station.
The MF-67 trains were replaced by the MF 77 series starting in 1980.

== Renovation and modernization ==

Wall paneling from the 1960s was removed during the renovation.
The tiles were not visible until 2009.
The station followed the Carrossage style.
The station was equipped with its own lighting system as part of the renovation.
Station names appear in white letters on a blue metal panel.
The seats are shaped like an inclined bowl, have a smile-shaped hole in the base, and allow part of the seat to be used as a backrest.
The advertising frames were illuminated from above.
The renovation scheme was widely used on the network to renovate stations quickly and at low cost.
The platform renovation involved replacing the beveled white tiling.

École Militaire is a standard configuration station.
It has two platforms separated by the metro tracks.
The station is 75 meters long and 13.50 meters wide.
The station has side platforms that are 4 meters wide and 75 meters long.
Each platform is 75 meters long.
Established flush with the ground, the ceiling is made up of a metal deck, the silver-coloured beams of which are supported by vertical walls.
The curved ceiling was removed in 2007 as part of a station renovation.
The bevelled white ceramic tiles cover the walls, the tunnel exits, the outlets of the corridors, the tympanums, and the pediments.
The metal advertising frames are tilted and the name of the station is inscribed in Parisine font on enamelled plates.
The metal curvature featured illuminated golden advertising panels.
The seats, in Akiko style, are green.
Green 'Motte' style seats were installed before the renovation.
The lighting is semi-direct, projected onto the walls, the advertising frames, the first row of ceiling vaults, and the piers.
The decoration is similar to that of La Muette station on Line 9 and Charles Michels station on Line 10.
