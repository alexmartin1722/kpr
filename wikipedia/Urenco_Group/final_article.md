# Urenco Group

The Urenco Group is a British-German-Dutch nuclear fuel consortium operating several uranium enrichment plants in Germany, the Netherlands, United States, and United Kingdom. It supplies nuclear power stations in about 15 countries, and states that it had a 29% share of the global market for enrichment services in 2011. Urenco uses centrifuge enrichment technology.

Urenco, headquartered in Stoke Poges, England, is owned one third by the UK government, one third by the Dutch government, and the final third equally by two major German utilities, E.ON and RWE.

== History ==

Urenco is an acronym for Uranium Enrichment Company.
The Almelo Agreement was between Great Britain, Germany, and the Netherlands to unify the research of the three countries on centrifuge technology and to use it commercially.
The organization was founded as a private limited English company on August 31, 1971, after the expiration of the Amelot Treaty, which was signed by the governments of Germany, the Netherlands, and the United Kingdom.
The company's work is based on research by Jacob Kistemaker, Gernot Zippe, and Max Steenbeck that took place during and after World War II.
Urenco started with three pilot plants located at three European sites.
Construction of the first two commercial plants began after the pilot plants, with one located at Capenhurst (E21) and the other at Almelo (SP3).
The first customer deliveries began in 1976, and the facilities reached full capacity in 1980 with a total uranium enrichment capacity of 460 tons per year.
The subsequent production expansion could be well adapted to actual delivery contracts due to the modular design of centrifuge technology.

Demand forecasts from the 1970s turned out to be completely exaggerated in the 1980s and 1990s.
The capacity of Urenco facilities was more than 10,000 tons of separation work per year in 2008 and is planned to be expanded further.
The company began using centrifuge technology for uranium enrichment in 2013.
Korea Electric Power Corporation made an equity investment on April 3, 2013, under a plan where Yurenco would hold 51% of the shares and Korea Electric Power Corporation (KEPCO) would hold 49% of the shares.
Negotiations failed due to obstacles such as shareholding issues, effectively cancelling the investment plan, and it is expected that negotiations will not resume unless a dramatic reversal occurs.
Korea Electric Power (KEPCO) realized the importance of raw material supply while exporting nuclear power plants to the UAE and is preparing to invest in expanding uranium enrichment facilities.

== Operations and Facilities ==

URENCO Ltd. operates facilities with self-developed centrifuge technology in Germany, the Netherlands, and the United Kingdom.
The Gronau uranium enrichment plant is located in northwestern Germany near the border with the Netherlands.
The facility is supplied with raw materials that are transported by trains carrying uranium hexafluoride (UF6) originating from the Comurhex plant in Pierrelatte, which is located in the Drôme region.
The Gronau plant has been under expansion for years and received necessary approvals in 2005 that allowed the plant to expand its facilities, an expansion that would increase production capacity by 2.5 times.
Construction of a uranium enrichment centrifuge facility was underway on April 23, 2007, in Piketon, Ohio, USA.
The centrifuges are designed to obtain uranium-235 for nuclear fuel.
The company building the factory is USEC, the United States Uranium Enrichment Corporation, which acquired the uranium enrichment business in 1993.

The US government privatized the uranium enrichment business in 1993.

== Worker safety and radiation incidents ==

An incident occurred at the Uranium enrichment plant in Gronau on January 21, 2010, when a worker was contaminated and exposed to increased ionizing radiation.

== Waste export to Russia ==

The company came under public criticism in 2007 after a report by the ZDF program Frontal21 stated that the company did not care about the poor safety conditions prevailing in Russia or the associated health risks for Russian workers.
Enrichment waste from Gronau was shipped to Siberia starting in 1996, with the last shipment occurring in August 2008.
This waste was transported by rail, declared as 'recyclable material' (Wertstoff), and received by the contract partner TWEL.
The waste was stored outdoors in steel barrels.
Other companies, including Tenex, are involved in the export of depleted UF6.
According to company figures, only 10 to 15 percent of the depleted uranium hexafluoride returned to Germany in the form of fuel, while the remaining portion stayed in Russia.
A television documentary titled 'Albtraum Atommüll' reported these details.
A special train carrying 600 tonnes of uranium hexafluoride was confirmed in late July 2019.

The destination of the train was Russia, a confirmation issued by the Ministry for Economic Affairs, Innovation, Digitalisation and Energy of North Rhine-Westphalia.

The company came under scrutiny in connection with the Fukushima nuclear disaster.

== Public opposition and protests ==

The establishment of UCN in Almelo was accompanied by significant social resistance, with animosity present in the 1970s and 1980s.
Multiple demonstrations were held against UCN, including one on March 4, 1978, attended by 50,000 opponents of nuclear energy.
Demonstrations against Urenco's role in uranium enrichment proliferation occurred frequently during the 1970s, and the motto of the procession was "No peace with nuclear energy." A large torch procession was held annually during Easter.
Urenco was repeatedly targeted by blockade actions by the group called BAN.

== Contract details and enrichment process ==

Urenco Nederland had an enrichment contract for its tails with the Russian nuclear technology company Techsnabeksport (Tenex), an arrangement that lasted until 2009.
Tenex enriched Urenco's tails to a level of approximately 0.3% to 0.7%, where 0.7% is the concentration of natural uranium ore and the feed material for Urenco's process is natural uranium ore.
Uranium enriched by Tenex was returned to Urenco Nederland at the location of Almelo.
Energoatom and Urenco signed an agreement on August 18, 2016.

== Russia's enrichment capacity and usage ==

Russia is the only country in the world with gas centrifuge overcapacity and has enrichment contracts with various Western countries.
Russia used a portion of Urenco's depleted uranium, which was mixed with highly enriched uranium from nuclear warheads in a process known as 'down blending' or downblending.
The resulting mixture has an enrichment level of 3% to 5% and is suitable for use in nuclear power plants.
Urenco and Tenex state that Russia can use depleted uranium in the future as fuel for developing breeder reactors, and Russia is capable of using this depleted uranium.

== Storage and alternative disposal methods ==

A portion of the depleted uranium is sent for conversion to France, where the material is converted into uranium oxide and returned for storage at COVRA, which stands for Centrale Organisatie voor Radioactief Afval.
There were three methods of dealing with tails: one method was storage on their own premises, another was converting uranium hexafluoride (UF₆) into uranium oxide, and the converted uranium oxide is stored at COVRA.
Depleted uranium residue was partially stored in Russia at locations including Seversk, Angarsk, and Novo-oeralsk.
Urenco stores depleted uranium on its own premises in a similar manner, has always stored a portion of its own tails, and continues to store this portion of its own tails.
