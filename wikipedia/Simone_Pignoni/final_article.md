# Simone Pignoni

Simone Pignoni (April 17, 1611 – December 16, 1698) was an Italian painter of the Baroque period.

Simone Pignoni was born in Florence in 1611.
He initially attended a Latin school.
His father wanted him to work in a bookbinder's workshop.
He began his apprenticeship in the workshop of Zanobi Pignoni, a bookbinder and relative of his.
Domenico Passignano frequently visited the bookbinding workshop and noticed the boy's talent.
Passignano observed his qualities and found him.
Passignano entrusted him to one of his former students, Fabrizio Boschi.
He did not stay with Fabrizio Boschi for long because this master had a very different temperament from his own.
He eventually returned to Passignano's workshop.
Francesco Furini was more in line with Pignoni's inclinations.
He apprenticed with Fabrizio Boschi, then with the more academic and puritanical Domenico Passignano, and finally with Francesco Furini.
He is best known for painting in a style reminiscent of the morbidly sensual Furini.
Reflective of this obsession is his self-portrait, c.
1650, in which he depicts himself building up a plump naked female from a skeleton.
The biographer Baldinucci, in what little he notes of the painter, recalls him as the scandalous "imitator of (Furini's) licentious inventions".
A more complete biography was recorded by his pupil Giovanni Camillo Sagrestani.
Described as endowed with a "bizarre and amenable intelligence", Pignoni apparently had a late-life conversion to more pious painting.
There is one episode recalled that during a serious illness "because in his life he had focused on studying about female forms, and (now) having resigned himself to the impending infinity, his spiritual father urged him to purge those errors with the flame, and once guided by a good disposition, he suddenly was cured by the Lord."
The story is likely just a pious tale.
Baldinucci's biography of Furini also recorded a similar, near-death renunciation of his art of the naked figure.
Among his more conventional works are a St.
Agatha cured by St.
Peter (attributed) in the Museo Civico di Trieste; a St.
Louis providing a banquet for the poor (c.
1682) now in the church of Santa Felicita in Florence, commissioned by Count Luigi Gucciardini; and a Madonna and child in glory with archangels Saints Michael and Raphael in battle armor and San Antonio of Padua (1671) for the Cappella di San Michele in Santissima Annunziata.
He painted an Allegory of Peace in Palazzo Vecchio.
A Penitent Magdalen that has been attributed to Pignoni is found in the Pitti Palace.
In San Bartolomeo in Monteoliveto, he painted a Madonna appearing to Blessed Bernardo Tolomeo.
In addition to Sagrestani, another pupil was the priest Luca Querci of Cutigliano.
Francesco Botti was one of his students.
Giovanni Camillo Ciabilli was one of his students.

== Life ==

Simone Pignoni was born in Florence and died in Florence.

== Style and Technique ==

Pignoni enjoyed coat-of-arms painting, a genre in which Francesco Furini created similar works decades earlier.
His style features a loose and bold brushstroke, with colors influenced by Venetian art.
Pignoni's design may be related to that of Flemish painters, and his works were full of female figures.

== Reception ==

His contemporaries criticized his paintings, his opulent ladies, his mannered subjects, and particularly his self-portrait, in which he covered the skeleton with flesh using a brush.
Francesco Gori described him as an imitator of Furini's licentious imagination.

== Selected Works ==

An artwork depicting Saint Charles Borromeo and Saint Mary Magdalene of the Pazzi is located in the chapel of San Michele Arcangelo, which is part of the Basilica della Santissima Annunziata in Florence.
The work titled 'Marie-Madeleine pénitente' has an unknown or disputed attribution.
The work titled 'Apparition of the Virgin to Blessed Bernardo Tolomeo' is located in the Museum of Fine Arts of Lyon.
The work titled 'Mystical Marriage of Saint Catherine' is located in the Museum of Fine Arts of Lyon.
The artwork titled 'The Miracle of Embroidery' is an oil on canvas painting measuring 120 x 152 cm with inventory number 69-9-1, located in the Fine Arts Museum of Brest.
Madonna with Child and Saints was executed around 1670 and is located in the Church of San Lorenzo in Montalbiolo.
A painting dated 1675 depicting Saint Joseph is located on an altar in the oratory of the Compagnia del Sacramento in Castelfranco di Sopra.

The Reliquary of Saint Sixtus was created by Simone Pignoni in 1614 and is exhibited in the Treasure Museum of Impruneta.
There is a painting in the convent of Celle at Cortona that depicts Saint Lucy.
There is a painting in the church of San Biagio in Casale that depicts the Madonna offering the Child to Saint Felix of Cantalice.
