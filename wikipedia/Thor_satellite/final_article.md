# Thor satellite

Thor (previously known as Marcopolo) is a family of satellites designed, launched and tested by Hughes Space and Communications (now part of Boeing Satellite Systems) for British Satellite Broadcasting (BSB), and were used for Britain's Direct Broadcast Service. Thor is owned by Telenor. Marcopolo 1 launched on 27 August 1989 on the 187th launch of a Delta rocket, and Marcopolo 2 launched on 17 August 1990, on a Delta II rocket. Marcopolo I had the Hughes designation HS376.

Although the satellites performed as designed, BSB merged with Sky Television to form British Sky Broadcasting and the BSB satellites were sold off and renamed. This also resulted in the obsoletion of the Squarial satellite-reception antenna, which was designed to operate with Thor 1 only.

== Ownership and operations ==

The Thor satellite family is Norwegian and belongs to the Telenor Satellite Broadcasting consortium.
Telenor Satellite Broadcasting is a Norwegian company and a subsidiary of Telenor ASA.
Telenor Satellite Broadcasting is part of the Norwegian telecommunications company Telenor, which was formerly known as Televerket when it was a public company.

== Technical specifications ==

Thales Alenia Space developed the satellite.
The THOR satellites are located in geostationary orbit at 0.8 degrees west longitude.
Satellite reception antennas were designed to match Marcompo.

== Services and coverage ==

The satellite provides communication services to Northern Europe and other regions including Europe and the Middle East.
Most of the THOR satellites' transponders are also occupied by Digi TV, a Romanian company, and FTA (Free-to-Air) television is available.

Sirius W, previously known as Sirius 1, was launched on 27 August 1989.
It was Hughes Space and Communications's first satellite.
Telenor purchased the satellite from BSkyB.
The satellite's position was changed from 31 degrees West to 1 degree West.
Marcopolo 1 was sold in December 1993 to Nordic Satellite AB of Sweden and operated until 2000 as Sirius 1 at 5°E.
It was then moved to 13°W, and renamed Sirius W.
It had 5 Ku band transponders.
The five transponders were used for five analog channels.

Marcopolo 2 was launched on 18 August 1990. It had 5 Ku band transponders. It was sold in July 1992 to Telenor of Norway and renamed Thor 1. It was located at 0.8°W. It was switched off in January 2002, and in November of that year it was moved to 7.4°W and reactivated with digital test signals broadcasting toward Scandinavia. Marcopolo 2 was sent up to the junk orbit in early January 2003.

Thor 2 was launched on 21 May 1997, and retired in 2008 although it remained in an inclined geostationary orbit until January 2013 when it de-orbited to the graveyard orbit.
In April 2009, THOR 2 changed its orbital position to 5.0°E, where the satellite is currently located 5 degrees to the east.
Thor 2 and Thor 3 are Hughes HS-376HP satellites.
It weighs 1467 kg, and has 15 Ku band transponders, with 3 spares, powered by 40-watt traveling-wave tube amplifiers (TWTAs).

Thor 3 (the "3" is officially "III") was a satellite used by Canal Digital in the Nordic areas for DTH services.
Canal Digital is a Scandinavian digital platform and a platform served by these satellites.
It launched on 9 June 1998 with an expected lifetime at 12 years, and was located at 0.8°W.
It had 14 active Ku band transponders powered by 47-watt traveling-wave tube amplifiers (TWTAs).
Most of the THOR satellites' transponders are occupied by Canal Digital.
The satellite was targeted on Scandinavia, Eastern Europe, and parts of Central Europe.
Thor 3 is no longer in operation and was moved to its graveyard orbit in 2019.
THOR 3 is currently at an approximate position of 4°W.
Thor 3 was replaced by Thor 6 in June 2010.

Intelsat 10-02, also known as Thor 10-02, was launched on 16 June 2004, located at 1°W, and was beamed toward Central Europe and the Middle East.
The Intelsat 1 East satellite broadcasts numerous pay-TV companies.
These satellites broadcast to Romania.
Digi TV is a platform served by these satellites.

Thor 5 was launched on 11 February 2008 by International Launch Services (ILS) using a Russian Proton rocket.
It was first planned that the satellite would be called Thor 2R', but Telenor named it Thor 5.
Telenor ASA ordered a communications satellite from Orbital Sciences Corporation in September 2006.
The satellite was intended for a geostationary orbit and is based on an OSC satellite with the base model named STARTM.
The satellite cost 217 million dollars and weighs 1960 kg.
Thor 5 was put into service in February 2009 and replaced the Thor 2 satellite.
Previous Thor satellites include Thor 2 and Thor 3.
The satellite was renamed Thor 5 upon completion.
The Thor 5 is located at a longitude of 0.8 degrees west relative to its station and orbits from a geosynchronous orbit.
It has 24 active Ku band transponders with a transmission power of 3600 W.
The satellite's operational lifetime is 15 years.
The Thor 5 transmits TV programs and its transmission coverage area includes the Nordic countries.
The Thor 0.8 East satellite broadcasts numerous pay-TV companies [cite: 2].
Telenor is a platform served by these satellites [cite: 2].
Free television and radio channels are also available via these satellites [cite: 2].
Sky News is one of the free channels available [cite: 2].
1Music Channel is one of the free channels available [cite: 2].

Thor 6 is a European satellite based on the Spacebus-4000B2 platform that was launched from the Guiana Spaceport on 29 October 2009.
A Proton rocket was used to place the satellite into orbit.
Telenor Satellite Broadcasting announced on 15 December that Thor 6 had completed all necessary in-orbit and ground-related testing and would commence commercial service in late December.
Thor 6 has 36 Ku band transponders.
It provides direct-to-home television broadcasting services from the orbital location 0.8°W.
It replaced Thor 3.

Thor 7 has been built by SSL. It was successfully launched by Arianespace from Kourou spaceport on 26 April 2015. It will mainly serve maritime customers and have a total capacity of 9 Gbit/s. It was launched in tandem with the Italian/French Sicral 2 defense satellite.
